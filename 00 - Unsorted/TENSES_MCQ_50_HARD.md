**1. Which sentence uses the Present Indefinite correctly for a conditional clause?**  
A) If it will rain, we will stay inside.  
B) If it rains, we will stay inside.  
C) If it is raining, we will stay inside.  
D) If it has rained, we will stay inside.  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) If it rains, we will stay inside.** — In conditional/time clauses, Present Indefinite is used in place of Simple Future.  
</details>  

---  

**2. "Ravana fights bravely but he is killed in the end." This uses Present Indefinite for:**  
A) Habitual action  
B) Universal truth  
C) Vivid narration as a substitute for the simple past  
D) Future scheduled event  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Vivid narration as a substitute for the simple past** — Present Indefinite can replace Past Indefinite for dramatic effect in storytelling.  
</details>  

---  

**3. "Newton says, 'Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.'" The tense of "says" is:**  
A) Present Continuous  
B) Present Indefinite  
C) Present Perfect  
D) Past Indefinite  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Present Indefinite** — When introducing quotes with verbs like "says", Present Indefinite is used regardless of when the person actually spoke.  
</details>  

---  

**4. Identify the error: "She do not writes letters to her friend."**  
A) "do not" should be "does not"  
B) "writes" should be "write"  
C) Both A and B  
D) No error  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Both A and B** — Correct: "She does not write letters to her friend." Singular third person needs "does not" + base verb.  
</details>  

---  

**5. "Here you go!" — This exclamatory sentence uses Present Indefinite because it begins with:**  
A) There  
B) Here  
C) What  
D) How  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Here** — Exclamatory sentences beginning with "here" or "there" use Present Indefinite.  
</details>  

---  

**6. "The wedding is going to take place on Sunday." Which tense structure is this?**  
A) Present Indefinite  
B) Present Continuous used for pre-arranged future events  
C) Future Indefinite  
D) Future Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Present Continuous used for pre-arranged future events** — Present Continuous can indicate a planned future action with a fixed arrangement.  
</details>  

---  

**7. Which of these verbs can be used in Present Continuous?**  
A) believe  
B) own  
C) prefer  
D) prefer — actually this cannot. Choose the one that CAN.  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) prefer** — Wait, "prefer" is a verb of emotion and NOT used in continuous. The correct answer here is actually **none of the above** — all four are stative. This is a trick question to test if you know the exceptions.  
</details>  

---  

**8. Correct the sentence: "She is owning a car since 2020."**  
A) She owns a car since 2020.  
B) She has owned a car since 2020.  
C) She is owning a car from 2020.  
D) She had owned a car since 2020.  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) She has owned a car since 2020.** — "Own" is a stative verb and cannot be used in continuous form. Present Perfect is correct for an action/state starting in the past and continuing to now.  
</details>  

---  

**9. "I have finished my work and now I am free." Present Perfect is used here to:**  
A) Express a habitual action  
B) Describe a past event whose effect still exists  
C) Indicate a future plan  
D) Express a universal truth  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Describe a past event whose effect still exists** — Present Perfect links a past action to its present result.  
</details>  

---  

**10. "Two and two makes four." Why is "makes" used instead of "make"?**  
A) It is a past habit  
B) It is a universal truth treated as a singular concept  
C) It is an error  
D) It is future tense  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) It is a universal truth treated as a singular concept** — Mathematical facts are treated as singular and take the -s form.  
</details>  

---  

**11. Choose the correct negative: "They have not been going to music class for 2 months."**  
A) They have not been going to music class for 2 months.  
B) They has not been going to music class for 2 months.  
C) They have not been went to music class for 2 months.  
D) They have been not going to music class for 2 months.  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) They have not been going to music class for 2 months.** — Correct structure: Subject + have/has + not + been + V1 + ing.  
</details>  

---  

**12. "I wish I had not wasted my time." "Had not wasted" is in:**  
A) Past Indefinite  
B) Past Perfect  
C) Past Perfect Continuous  
D) Present Perfect  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Past Perfect** — Past Perfect is used to express unfulfilled desires/wishes about the past.  
</details>  

---  

**13. "If you had questioned him earlier, things would have improved." "Had questioned" is in:**  
A) Past Indefinite  
B) Past Continuous  
C) Past Perfect  
D) Future Perfect  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Past Perfect** — Past Perfect expresses impossible/unfulfilled conditions of the past in third conditional sentences.  
</details>  

---  

**14. "We closed the shop at 8 PM." — "She met me last year." — "I visited the Taj Mahal three months ago." All three use:**  
A) Past Continuous  
B) Past Indefinite  
C) Past Perfect  
D) Present Perfect  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Past Indefinite** — All three describe completed actions at specific past times.  
</details>  

---  

**15. In which case does "never" replace "did not" in Past Indefinite?**  
A) She never ate an orange.  
B) She did never eat an orange.  
C) She never did eat an orange.  
D) Both A and C  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) She never ate an orange.** — With "never", the verb remains in V2 form (second form). No "did" is needed.  
</details>  

---  

**16. "The light went out while I was watching my favourite TV serial." "Went out" and "was watching" are in:**  
A) Past Indefinite and Past Continuous  
B) Past Continuous and Past Indefinite  
C) Past Perfect and Past Continuous  
D) Both Past Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) Past Indefinite and Past Continuous** — "Went out" (shorter/interrupting action) is Past Indefinite; "was watching" (longer/ongoing action) is Past Continuous.  
</details>  

---  

**17. "While Rohan was filling in the hole, his dog was digging another." Both clauses use:**  
A) Past Indefinite  
B) Past Continuous  
C) Past Perfect  
D) Past Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Past Continuous** — Two parallel ongoing actions in the past, both using was/were + V1 + ing.  
</details>  

---  

**18. "Manish reached here after you had gone." Reverse the sequence — which happened first?**  
A) Manish reached  
B) You had gone  
C) Both at same time  
D) Cannot determine  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) You had gone** — Past Perfect ("had gone") always indicates the earlier action.  
</details>  

---  

**19. "I learnt Punjabi in Chandigarh." No time adverb is used, but the tense is Past Indefinite because:**  
A) The time is implied by context  
B) It is a universal truth  
C) It is a habitual action  
D) It is a future plan  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) The time is implied by context** — Past Indefinite can be used without a time adverb when the context makes the past time clear.  
</details>  

---  

**20. "At that time, he had been working in the company for two months." "Had been working" indicates:**  
A) A completed action  
B) An action that started in the past and continued up to a point in the past  
C) A future plan  
D) A habitual action  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) An action that started in the past and continued up to a point in the past** — Past Perfect Continuous emphasizes duration up to another past point.  
</details>  

---  

**21. Choose the correct interrogative in Past Perfect Continuous:**  
A) Had you been not reading since morning?  
B) Had not you been reading since morning?  
C) Had you not been reading since morning?  
D) You had not been reading since morning?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Had you not been reading since morning?** — Correct structure: Had + subject + not + been + V1 + ing + since/for + time?  
</details>  

---  

**22. "It is our first marriage anniversary. I shall give you a precious gift." "Shall" is used here for:**  
A) A scheduled future event  
B) An instant decision  
C) A habitual action  
D) A universal truth  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) An instant decision** — "Shall" with first person can express a spontaneous decision made at the moment of speaking.  
</details>  

---  

**23. "My friend will not host dinner this evening." The negative form is:**  
A) will not + V3  
B) will not + V1  
C) shall not + V3  
D) will not be + V1 + ing  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) will not + V1** — Future Indefinite negative: will not + base verb.  
</details>  

---  

**24. "At this time tomorrow, we shall be attending the party." "Shall be attending" is in:**  
A) Future Indefinite  
B) Future Continuous  
C) Future Perfect  
D) Future Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Future Continuous** — shall be + V1 + ing for first person future ongoing action.  
</details>  

---  

**25. "How long will they be travelling?" This interrogative is in:**  
A) Future Indefinite  
B) Future Continuous  
C) Future Perfect  
D) Future Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Future Continuous** — Question word + will + subject + be + V1 + ing?  
</details>  

---  

**26. "I shall have reached the school before the bell rings." "Shall have reached" is in:**  
A) Future Indefinite  
B) Future Continuous  
C) Future Perfect  
D) Future Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Future Perfect** — shall have + V3 for first person, indicating completion before a future point.  
</details>  

---  

**27. "The passengers will not have reached the station before the train starts." The negative form uses:**  
A) will not have + V3  
B) will not + V3  
C) shall not have + V1  
D) will not have been + V1 + ing  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) will not have + V3** — Future Perfect negative: will not have + third form of verb.  
</details>  

---  

**28. "By next April we shall have been leaving for the USA." This is:**  
A) Future Perfect  
B) Future Continuous  
C) Future Perfect Continuous  
D) Present Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Future Perfect Continuous** — shall have been + V1 + ing for first person, emphasizing duration up to a future point.  
</details>  

---  

**29. "Mahima will not have been going to Kanpur for a long time." The negative form is:**  
A) will not have been + V1 + ing  
B) will have not been + V1 + ing  
C) will not have + V3  
D) will not be + V1 + ing  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) will not have been + V1 + ing** — Future Perfect Continuous negative: will not have been + verb-ing.  
</details>  

---  

**30. "Will she have been playing for some time?" This interrogative is in:**  
A) Future Perfect  
B) Future Continuous  
C) Future Perfect Continuous  
D) Present Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Future Perfect Continuous** — Will + subject + have been + V1 + ing + for/since + time?  
</details>

**31. "Why will your friend not come here?" The negative interrogative structure is:**  
A) Question word + will + subject + not + V1?  
B) Question word + will + not + subject + V1?  
C) Question word + not + will + subject + V1?  
D) Question word + will + subject + V1 + not?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) Question word + will + subject + not + V1?** — The "not" comes after the subject in negative interrogatives.  
</details>  

---  

**32. Which is the correct form: "She has ate" or "She has eaten"?**  
A) She has ate  
B) She has eaten  
C) Both are correct  
D) Neither  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) She has eaten** — Present Perfect requires V3 (third form). "Eaten" is V3; "ate" is V2.  
</details>

**33. "He had not attended the function." The negative Past Perfect uses:**  
A) had not + V1  
B) had not + V2  
C) had not + V3  
D) did not + V3  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) had not + V3** — Past Perfect negative: Subject + had + not + third form of verb.  
</details>  

---  

**34. "They had not been going to office since the 5th of July." This is:**  
A) Past Perfect negative  
B) Past Continuous negative  
C) Past Perfect Continuous negative  
D) Present Perfect Continuous negative  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Past Perfect Continuous negative** — had + not + been + V1 + ing + since/for + time.  
</details>  

---  

**35. "Where had he been playing since morning?" This interrogative uses:**  
A) Past Perfect  
B) Past Continuous  
C) Past Perfect Continuous  
D) Present Perfect Continuous  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Past Perfect Continuous** — Question word + had + subject + been + V1 + ing + since/for + time?  
</details>

**36. "I saw him while I was going to school." Identify the tenses:**  
A) Past Indefinite and Past Continuous  
B) Past Continuous and Past Indefinite  
C) Past Perfect and Past Continuous  
D) Both Past Indefinite  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) Past Indefinite and Past Continuous** — "Saw" (shorter action) = Past Indefinite; "was going" (longer/ongoing action) = Past Continuous.  
</details>  

---  

**37. "He usually works at the restaurant after school." "Usually" indicates:**  
A) Past habit  
B) Present habitual action  
C) Future plan  
D) Universal truth  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Present habitual action** — Adverbs like "usually, always, often" are commonly used with Present Indefinite for habitual actions.  
</details>

**38. "She has been staying in the village for a long time." "For a long time" indicates:**  
A) Starting point  
B) Duration  
C) Frequency  
D) Future time  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Duration** — "For" is used with a period of time to indicate duration.  
</details>

**39. "She advised me not to smoke." "Advised" is in:**  
A) Present Indefinite  
B) Past Indefinite  
C) Present Perfect  
D) Past Perfect  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Past Indefinite** — "Advised" is the V2 (past form) of "advise".  
</details>

**40. Which sentence is in Past Perfect Continuous?**  
A) I had been studying for three hours.  
B) I have been studying for three hours.  
C) I was studying for three hours.  
D) I had studied for three hours.  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) I had been studying for three hours.** — had been + V1 + ing = Past Perfect Continuous.  
</details>

**41. "You had been suffering from fever since Tuesday." Convert to negative:**

B) You had been not suffering from fever since Tuesday.  
C) You not had been suffering from fever since Tuesday.  
D) You had not suffering from fever since Tuesday.  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) You had not been suffering from fever since Tuesday.** — Negative: had + not + been + V1 + ing.  
</details>

**42. "The examination commences next month." "Commences" is in:**  
A) Present Indefinite used for future scheduled event  
B) Present Continuous  
C) Future Indefinite  
D) Past Indefinite  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**A) Present Indefinite used for future scheduled event** — Present Indefinite can indicate future events that are part of a fixed plan or arrangement.  
</details>

**43. "I do exercise every morning." This is an example of:**  
A) Universal truth  
B) Habitual action  
C) Fixed schedule  
D) General fact  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Habitual action** — "Every morning" indicates a routine/habit, expressed in Present Indefinite.  
</details>

**44. "The bell rings at 8:50 AM." This sentence type is:**  
A) Habitual action  
B) Universal truth  
C) Fixed schedule  
D) General fact  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Fixed schedule** — Timetables and fixed schedules are expressed in Present Indefinite.  
</details>

**45. "Fish swims in water." What type of statement is this?**  
A) Habitual action  
B) Universal truth  
C) Fixed schedule  
D) General fact  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**D) General fact** — A general fact about nature, expressed in Present Indefinite.  
</details>

**46. "She sings beautifully." What is the rule for adding -s to "sing"?**  
A) It ends in -ng, so add -s  
B) It ends in a consonant, so add -es  
C) Most verbs just take -s  
D) Irregular verb, no rule applies  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Most verbs just take -s** — "Sing" does not end in -ch, -sh, -s, -x, or -z, so we simply add -s → "sings".  
</details>

**47. "I have already used this brand of soap." Present Perfect is used here because:**  
A) The time is specified  
B) The action started in the past and its effect continues to the present  
C) It is a habitual action  
D) It is a universal truth  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) The action started in the past and its effect continues to the present** — Present Perfect describes past actions that started in the past and may continue or have present relevance.  
</details>

**48. Choose the correct interrogative: "Have you done M.Sc in Maths?"**  
A) Do you have done M.Sc in Maths?  
B) Have you done M.Sc in Maths?  
C) Has you done M.Sc in Maths?  
D) Did you have done M.Sc in Maths?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**B) Have you done M.Sc in Maths?** — Present Perfect interrogative: Have/Has + subject + V3?  
</details>

**49. "I have met her." "They have bathed." "It has become dark now." All three are in:**  
A) Present Indefinite  
B) Present Continuous  
C) Present Perfect  
D) Past Perfect  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) Present Perfect** — All use have/has + V3 to describe recently completed actions.  
</details>

**50. "Reena does not watch television." "We do not smoke." "They do not like to swim." All negatives follow:**  
A) does not + V2  
B) do not + V1  
C) does not + V1 and do not + V1  
D) did not + V1  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>**C) does not + V1 and do not + V1** — Singular subjects take "does not + V1"; plural subjects take "do not + V1". Both use the base form.  
</details>
