2. Read the passage given below:

Water is essential for life, yet fresh water is limited and must be used wisely. Only a tiny share of the water on earth is fit for drinking, and the same supply has to serve farms, factories, hospitals and homes. In many towns, people waste water without realising it, because it arrives so easily from a tap.

A single dripping tap can waste nearly 20 litres of water in a day. That is enough drinking water for a small family. Leaking pipes in bathrooms and gardens often go unnoticed for weeks, quietly draining away a resource that millions of people walk kilometres to collect.

Simple habits can make a big difference. Turning off the tap while brushing your teeth, fixing leaking pipes, and using a bucket instead of a hose to wash vehicles can save hundreds of litres every month. Taking shorter showers and watering plants in the evening also help, because less water evaporates in the cooler air.

Schools and communities can also help by practising rainwater harvesting, a method of collecting and storing rainwater from rooftops for later use. The collected water can be used for gardening and cleaning, which reduces the pressure on the town supply. In 2024, Sunrise Public School began a "Save Every Drop" campaign. Students checked taps for leaks, placed posters around the campus, and recorded how much water different actions saved in one month.

The results showed that small efforts, when done regularly, can stop water from being wasted and protect this precious resource for the future. The school now repeats the campaign every year, and several neighbouring schools have asked the students to explain their method.
