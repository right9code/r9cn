## Chapter 05: Reported Speech  

**Reported Speech** (also known as **Narration**) refers to the method of conveying words spoken by a person to someone else. There are two distinct ways to report what someone said:  

1. **Direct Speech:** The actual words of the speaker are quoted verbatim within double quotation marks (inverted commas `"..."`).  
   - *Example:* Rohan said, "I am reading an interesting novel."  
2. **Indirect Speech (Reported Speech):** The substance or meaning of the speaker's words is reported without quoting their exact words, with necessary grammatical changes in tenses, pronouns, and adverbs of time and place.  
   - *Example:* Rohan said that he was reading an interesting novel.  

---

### Key Terms in Reported Speech  

- **Reporting Clause:** The clause that contains the person reporting and the verb of reporting (e.g., *Rohan said to me*).  
- **Reporting Verb:** The verb in the reporting clause (e.g., *said*, *told*, *asked*, *enquired*).  
- **Reported Speech:** The actual statement or question placed inside inverted commas in direct speech.  

---

### Basic Rules of Conversion (Direct to Indirect)  

1. **Punctuation:**  
   - Remove the comma after the reporting verb and remove all quotation marks (`"..."`).  
   - End the reported sentence with a full stop (`.`), even if the direct speech is an interrogative or exclamatory sentence.  
2. **Connectors:**  
   - Introduce the reported statement with an appropriate connector such as *that* (for statements), *if / whether* (for yes/no questions), or question words like *who, where, what* (for wh-questions).  
3. **Assertive Word Order:**  
   - Change all interrogative and exclamatory structures into assertive structures (Subject + Verb order).  

---

### 1. Rules for Changing Pronouns and Possessives (The SON Formula)  

The pronouns in the reported speech change according to the **SON** rule:  

| Person in Reported Speech | Changes According to | Formula Rule |  
| :--- | :--- | :---: |  
| **1st Person** (*I, me, my, mine, we, us, our, ours*) | **S**ubject of the reporting verb | **1 $\rightarrow$ S** |  
| **2nd Person** (*you, your, yours*) | **O**bject of the reporting verb | **2 $\rightarrow$ O** |  
| **3rd Person** (*he, him, his, she, her, it, they, them, their*) | **N**o change | **3 $\rightarrow$ N** |  

#### Examples:  
- **1st Person:** He said, "I have lost my watch." $\rightarrow$ He said that **he** had lost **his** watch.  
- **2nd Person:** She said to me, "You are late." $\rightarrow$ She told me that **I** was late.  
- **3rd Person:** Mohan said, "They are playing." $\rightarrow$ Mohan said that **they** were playing.  

---

### 2. Rules for Changing Tenses  

#### A. When the Reporting Verb is in the Present or Future Tense:  
If the reporting verb is in the **Present** (*says, has said*) or **Future Tense** (*will say*), the **tense of the verb in the reported speech does NOT change**.  
- *Direct:* He says, "I am unwell."  
- *Indirect:* He says that he **is** unwell.  
- *Direct:* She will say, "I have completed the homework."  
- *Indirect:* She will say that she **has completed** the homework.  

---

#### B. When the Reporting Verb is in the Past Tense (*said, said to*):  
The tense of the reported speech changes into the corresponding past tense as follows:  

| Direct Speech Tense | Indirect Speech Tense | Direct Speech Example | Indirect Speech Example |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **Simple Present (V1 / s/es)** | **Simple Past (V2 / did)** | He said, "I play cricket." | He said that he **played** cricket. |  
| **Present Continuous (is/am/are + V-ing)** | **Past Continuous (was/were + V-ing)** | She said, "I am painting a scenery." | She said that she **was painting** a scenery. |  
| **Present Perfect (has/have + V3)** | **Past Perfect (had + V3)** | Aman said, "I have passed the exam." | Aman said that he **had passed** the exam. |  
| **Present Perfect Continuous (has/have been + V-ing)** | **Past Perfect Continuous (had been + V-ing)** | They said, "We have been waiting for hours." | They said that they **had been waiting** for hours. |  
| **Simple Past (V2 / did)** | **Past Perfect (had + V3)** | She said, "I bought a new bicycle." | She said that she **had bought** a new bicycle. |  
| **Past Continuous (was/were + V-ing)** | **Past Perfect Continuous (had been + V-ing)** | He said, "It was raining heavily." | He said that it **had been raining** heavily. |  
| **Past Perfect (had + V3)** | **No change (Past Perfect remains had + V3)** | Ravi said, "I had finished my dinner." | Ravi said that he **had finished** his dinner. |  
| **Past Perfect Continuous** | **No change** | She said, "I had been working." | She said that she **had been working**. |  

---

#### C. Changes in Modal Auxiliaries:  
- **will** $\rightarrow$ **would**  
- **shall** $\rightarrow$ **should** (or **would**)  
- **can** $\rightarrow$ **could**  
- **may** $\rightarrow$ **might**  
- **must** $\rightarrow$ **had to** (for present obligation) / remains **must** (for universal moral truth or permanent rule)  
- *would, could, should, might, ought to, used to* $\rightarrow$ **remain unchanged**.  

---

#### D. Exceptions to the Tense Change Rule (No Change in Tense Even with Past Reporting Verb):  
The tense in the reported speech remains **unchanged** when the statement expresses:  
1. **A Universal Truth / Scientific Fact:**  
   - *Direct:* The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."  
   - *Indirect:* The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.  
2. **A Habitual Fact / Routine Action:**  
   - *Direct:* Father said, "I go for a morning walk every day."  
   - *Indirect:* Father said that he **goes** for a morning walk every day.  
3. **A Historical Fact / Event:**  
   - *Direct:* The teacher said, "India gained independence in 1947."  
   - *Indirect:* The teacher said that India **gained** independence in 1947.  
4. **A Proverb or Maxim:**  
   - *Direct:* Grandfather said, "Honesty is the best policy."  
   - *Indirect:* Grandfather said that honesty **is** the best policy.

### 3. Changes in Words Expressing Time, Distance, and Proximity  

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |  
| :--- | :--- |  
| **now** | then |  
| **here** | there |  
| **today** | that day |  
| **tonight** | that night |  
| **yesterday** | the previous day / the day before |  
| **tomorrow** | the next day / the following day |  
| **this** | that |  
| **these** | those |  
| **ago** | before |  
| **thus** | so |  
| **last night / last week** | the previous night / the previous week |  
| **next week / next year** | the following week / the following year |  
| **hither** | thither |  
| **hence** | thence |

### 4. Sentence-Specific Conversion Rules  

#### A. Assertive Sentences (Statements)  
- Reporting verb `said` remains `said`.  
- `said to + object` changes to `told + object`.  
- Conjunction **`that`** connects the reporting clause with reported speech.  
- *Example:* She said to me, "I can help you with your project." $\rightarrow$ She **told me that** she **could** help me with my project.  

---

#### B. Interrogative Sentences (Questions)  
1. **Reporting Verb:** `said / said to` changes into `asked`, `enquired of`, `questioned`, or `demanded`.  
2. **Yes/No Questions (starting with auxiliary verbs like *is, am, are, do, does, did, has, have, can, will*):**  
   - Use connector **`if`** or **`whether`**.  
   - Convert question order into statement order (Subject + Verb).  
   - Remove question mark (`?`).  
   - *Direct:* He said to me, "Are you joining the debate competition?"  
   - *Indirect:* He **asked me if I was** joining the debate competition.  
3. **Wh- Questions (starting with *what, when, where, why, who, whom, whose, which, how*):**  
   - Do **NOT** use *that, if*, or *whether*.  
   - Retain the **wh-word** as the connector.  
   - Change question order into assertive order.  
   - *Direct:* The stranger said to me, "Where do you live?"  
   - *Indirect:* The stranger **asked me where I lived**. *(Not: where did I live)*  

---

#### C. Imperative Sentences (Commands, Requests, Advice, Suggestions)  
1. **Reporting Verb:** Changes into `ordered`, `commanded`, `requested`, `advised`, `urged`, `warned`, or `forbade` according to the mood of the sentence.  
2. **Infinitive Construction:** Replace inverted commas with **`to + V1`** (for positive imperative) and **`not to + V1`** (for negative imperative).  
   - *(If `forbade` is used, do not use `not` as forbid already carries a negative meaning).*  
3. Remove words of politeness like *please* or *kindly*.  
- *Direct (Command):* The teacher said to the boys, "Stand up quietly."  
  $\rightarrow$ *Indirect:* The teacher **ordered the boys to stand up quietly**.  
- *Direct (Request):* She said to her friend, "Please lend me your camera for a day."  
  $\rightarrow$ *Indirect:* She **requested her friend to lend her her camera for a day**.  
- *Direct (Advice):* The doctor said to the patient, "Do not take oily food."  
  $\rightarrow$ *Indirect:* The doctor **advised the patient not to take oily food** (or *forbade the patient to take oily food*).  

---

#### D. Exclamatory and Optative Sentences  
1. **Reporting Verb:** Changes to `exclaimed with joy / sorrow / surprise / anger / disgust / regret` or `wished / prayed / greeted`.  
2. Conjunction **`that`** is used.  
3. Interjections like *Hurrah!, Alas!, Wow!, Bravo!, Oh!* are omitted and their emotion is expressed in the reporting verb.  
4. Exclamatory word order becomes assertive statement order.  
- *Direct:* The captain said, "Hurrah! We have won the championship!"  
  $\rightarrow$ *Indirect:* The captain **exclaimed with joy that they had won the championship**.  
- *Direct:* The traveller said, "Alas! I have lost all my money."  
  $\rightarrow$ *Indirect:* The traveller **exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost all his money**.  
- *Direct (Optative):* Grandmother said to me, "May you live long!"  
  $\rightarrow$ *Indirect:* Grandmother **prayed that I might live long**.

### PRACTICE QUESTIONS  

#### I. Convert the following sentences from Direct into Indirect Speech.  

1. Rohan said to me, "I am preparing a science model for the national exhibition."  
2. The teacher said, "Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius."  
3. She said to him, "Did you see the eclipse yesterday?"  
4. The police officer said to the driver, "Show me your driving license."  
5. Mother said to Varun, "Why are you sitting idle in your room?"  
6. Priya said, "I have been practicing classical dance since morning."  
7. The librarian said to the students, "Do not make noise in the reading hall."  
8. Ananya said, "Hurrah! My elder sister has cleared the civil services exam!"  
9. He said, "I will definitely help you tomorrow."  
10. The doctor said to the patient, "Take these medicines regularly for two weeks."

#### II. Multiple Choice Questions (CBSE Board Pattern)  

**1. Choose the correct indirect speech for:**  
*The teacher said, "The sun is a huge ball of burning gases."*  
- (A) The teacher said that the sun was a huge ball of burning gases.  
- (B) The teacher said that the sun is a huge ball of burning gases.  
- (C) The teacher told that the sun had been a huge ball of burning gases.  
- (D) The teacher asked if the sun is a huge ball of burning gases.  

**2. Choose the correct indirect speech for:**  
*Kavita said to me, "Can you lend me your dictionary for an hour?"*  
- (A) Kavita asked me that if I could lend her my dictionary for an hour.  
- (B) Kavita told me if I can lend her my dictionary for an hour.  
- (C) Kavita asked me if I could lend her my dictionary for an hour.  
- (D) Kavita requested me that I could lend her my dictionary for an hour.  

**3. Choose the correct indirect speech for:**  
*The inspector said to the suspect, "Where were you at 9:00 PM yesterday?"*  
- (A) The inspector asked the suspect where he was at 9:00 PM the previous day.  
- (B) The inspector asked the suspect where had he been at 9:00 PM yesterday.  
- (C) The inspector asked the suspect where he had been at 9:00 PM the previous day.  
- (D) The inspector enquired the suspect where he was at 9:00 PM the previous day.

**Read the dialogue below and complete the reported paragraph by filling in the blanks.**

**Aryan:** I have my mathematics pre-board examination tomorrow and I haven't revised geometry yet.  
**Father:** Don't panic. Focus on the key formulas and solve two practice theorems tonight.

### Answer Key  

#### I. Direct into Indirect Speech  
1. Rohan told me that he was preparing a science model for the national exhibition.  
2. The teacher said that water boils at 100 degrees Celsius. *(Universal scientific truth)*  
3. She asked him if / whether he had seen the eclipse the previous day (or the day before).  
4. The police officer ordered / told the driver to show him his driving license.  
5. Mother asked Varun why he was sitting idle in his room.  
6. Priya said that she had been practicing classical dance since morning.  
7. The librarian forbade the students to make noise in the reading hall (or *ordered / advised the students not to make noise in the reading hall*).  
8. Ananya exclaimed with joy that her elder sister had cleared the civil services exam.  
9. He said that he would definitely help me the next day (or the following day).  
10. The doctor advised the patient to take those medicines regularly for two weeks.
