## Chapter 03: Modals  

The verbs that express the mood, mode, or manner of the actions denoted by the principal verb are called **Modals** (or **Modal Auxiliaries**). Unlike primary auxiliary verbs (*be*, *do*, *have*), modals are not used alone to express an action; they always accompany a base form of a principal verb (V1) to convey meanings such as ability, permission, possibility, necessity, obligation, duty, advice, or willingness.  

The main modal auxiliaries are: **can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to**.  
The semi-modals (or marginal auxiliaries) are: **need, dare, used to, have to / has to / had to**.  

---

### Nature and Characteristics of Modals  

1. **Modals are never used alone without a principal verb.**  
   - *(Incorrect)* She can the piano.  
   - *(Correct)* She **can play** the piano.  

2. **Modals do not change their form according to the person or number of the subject.** They have no *-s*, *-es*, *-ed*, or *-ing* forms.  
   - I **must** study.  
   - He **must** study. *(Not: He musts study.)*  
   - They **must** study.  

3. **Modals do not take an infinitive 'to' before the main verb (except *ought to*, *used to*, and *have to*).**  
   - *(Incorrect)* We should to respect our elders.  
   - *(Correct)* We **should respect** our elders.  

4. **Two modal auxiliaries cannot be used together directly.**  
   - *(Incorrect)* You must can do this work.  
   - *(Correct)* You **must be able to** do this work.  

5. **Modals are always followed by the base form (first form) of the verb (V1).**  
   - He **may come** today. *(Not: He may comes / came / coming.)*  

---

### Functions and Uses of Individual Modals  

#### 1. CAN  
**(i) To express ability or capability in the present:**  
- She **can** speak four languages fluently.  
- I **can** solve this complex puzzle in minutes.  

**(ii) To seek or grant informal permission:**  
- **Can** I borrow your pen for a moment? *(Seeking permission)*  
- You **can** go home now after finishing the task. *(Granting permission)*  

**(iii) To express general possibility:**  
- Accidents **can** happen anywhere if you are careless.  

**Negative Form:** *Cannot* / *Can't*  
- We **cannot** enter the hall without an admit card.  

---

#### 2. COULD *(Past of 'Can')*  
**(i) To express general ability or capability in the past:**  
- When I was ten, I **could** swim across this river.  
- He **could** run faster than anyone in his school.  

**(ii) To make a polite request in the present (more polite than *can*):**  
- **Could** you please explain this concept once again?  
- **Could** I speak with Mr. Sharma, please?  

**(iii) To express conditional or remote possibility:**  
- If we leave now, we **could** catch the 4 PM train.  

**Negative Form:** *Could not* / *Couldn't*  
- She **could not** attend the seminar due to fever.  

---

#### 3. MAY  
**(i) To seek or give formal permission:**  
- **May** I come in, Sir? *(Formal request)*  
- Yes, you **may** take this seat. *(Formal permission)*  

**(ii) To express a strong or realistic possibility / probability:**  
- Look at those dark clouds; it **may** rain tonight.  
- The principal **may** visit our classroom today.  

**(iii) To express a wish, prayer, or blessing:**  
- **May** God bless you with health and happiness!  
- **May** you succeed in all your endeavours!  

**(iv) To express purpose in a subordinate clause (after *so that* / *in order that*):**  
- We work hard so that we **may** excel in life.  

**Negative Form:** *May not*  
- You **may not** submit the assignment after the deadline.  

---

#### 4. MIGHT *(Past/Weaker form of 'May')*  
**(i) To express weak or remote possibility (less likely than *may*):**  
- The sky is mostly clear, but it **might** rain later.  
- If you call him now, he **might** still be at the office.  

**(ii) To make a very hesitant or diffident request:**  
- **Might** I suggest a small alternative to this proposal?  

**(iii) As the past tense of *may* in indirect speech:**  
- Direct: He said, "I may join the club."  
- Indirect: He said that he **might** join the club.  

**Negative Form:** *Might not* / *Mightn't*  
- They **might not** arrive before sunset.  

---

#### 5. SHALL  
**(i) With first person (*I*, *We*) to make offers, suggestions, or ask for advice in questions:**  
- **Shall** we go for a walk in the evening? *(Suggestion)*  
- **Shall** I carry this heavy luggage for you? *(Offer)*  

**(ii) With second and third person (*You*, *He*, *She*, *They*) to indicate a command, promise, threat, or official rule:**  
- You **shall** report to the principal immediately. *(Command)*  
- You **shall** receive your certificate tomorrow. *(Promise)*  
- Trespassers **shall** be prosecuted. *(Official warning / Law)*  

**Negative Form:** *Shall not* / *Shan't*  
- He **shall not** enter this laboratory without permission.  

---

#### 6. SHOULD *(Past form of 'Shall')*  
**(i) To express duty, moral obligation, or responsibility:**  
- We **should** respect our national symbols.  
- Citizens **should** obey traffic regulations.  

**(ii) To give advice, suggestion, or recommendation:**  
- You **should** sleep for at least seven hours each night.  
- He **should** consult a doctor about his persistent cough.  

**(iii) After the conjunction *lest* (to express fear or precaution):**  
- Walk fast lest you **should** miss the morning assembly.  

**(iv) To express logical expectation or likelihood:**  
- The train left on time, so it **should** arrive by 5 PM.  

**Negative Form:** *Should not* / *Shouldn't*  
- You **should not** waste your time on trivial matters.  

---

#### 7. WILL  
**(i) With first person (*I*, *We*) to express determination, promise, threat, or intention:**  
- I **will** secure top marks this term. *(Determination)*  
- We **will** stand by you in difficult times. *(Promise)*  
- I **will** report your indiscipline to the authorities. *(Threat)*  

**(ii) With second and third person (*You*, *He*, *They*) to indicate simple future:**  
- The school **will** reopen on Monday.  

**(iii) In questions with second person (*You*) to make requests or invitations:**  
- **Will** you please pass the salt? *(Request)*  
- **Will** you come to my birthday party? *(Invitation)*  

**(iv) To express characteristic habits or routine behavior:**  
- A baby **will** cry when it is hungry.  

**Negative Form:** *Will not* / *Won't*  
- I **will not** tolerate dishonesty.  

---

#### 8. WOULD *(Past form of 'Will')*  
**(i) To express past habitual action (routine in the past):**  
- During summer vacations, my grandfather **would** tell us fascinating stories every evening.  

**(ii) To make a polite request or invitation (more formal and courteous than *will*):**  
- **Would** you mind opening the window?  
- **Would** you like a cup of green tea?  

**(iii) To express a preference or wish (often with *rather* or *like*):**  
- I **would rather** read a book than watch television.  
- I **would like** to thank all the teachers for their guidance.  

**(iv) In conditional sentences expressing unreal or imaginary situations:**  
- If I had a wings, I **would** fly across the world.  

**Negative Form:** *Would not* / *Wouldn't*  
- She **would not** listen to anyone's advice when she was angry.  

---

#### 9. MUST  
**(i) To express compulsion, necessity, or strong obligation from the speaker's viewpoint:**  
- You **must** wear a helmet while riding a two-wheeler.  
- We **must** complete this syllabus before the examination.  

**(ii) To express logical certainty or strong deduction:**  
- There is light in the room; someone **must** be inside.  
- She has worked continuously for ten hours; she **must** be exhausted.  

**(iii) To express strong recommendation:**  
- You **must** watch this documentary; it is truly inspiring.  

**Negative Form:** *Must not* / *Mustn't* *(Expresses strict prohibition)*  
- You **must not** touch live electrical wires. *(Prohibition)*  

---

#### 10. OUGHT TO  
**(i) To express moral duty, ethical obligation, or social righteousness (similar to *should*, but with external moral weight):**  
- We **ought to** take care of our elderly parents.  
- Everyone **ought to** plant trees to preserve the environment.  

**(ii) To express strong probability or likelihood based on reason:**  
- The prices **ought to** come down after the new harvest.  

**Negative Form:** *Ought not to* / *Oughtn't to*  
- You **ought not to** speak ill of your classmates.  

---

### Semi-Modals and Marginal Auxiliaries  

#### 1. HAVE TO / HAS TO / HAD TO  
- Used to express obligation or compulsion coming from external circumstances, rules, or laws (unlike *must*, which comes from internal conviction).  
- **Has to** (singular subject, present) / **Have to** (plural subject and *I/We*, present) / **Had to** (past for all subjects).  
  - I **have to** wake up at 5 AM tomorrow for the entrance test.  
  - Students **have to** wear their uniform on all working days.  
  - Yesterday, he **had to** walk three miles because his bicycle broke down.  

#### 2. NEED / NEED NOT (NEEDN'T)  
- **Need** as a modal indicates requirement or necessity.  
- Most commonly used in negative sentences and questions to denote **absence of obligation**:  
  - You **need not** bring heavy luggage; everything is provided.  
  - **Need** I write the entire essay again?  
- **Need not have + V3:** Indicates that an action was performed which was unnecessary.  
  - You **need not have bought** extra pens; I had plenty.  

#### 3. DARE / DARE NOT (DAREN'T)  
- Denotes having courage or boldness to do something.  
- Used as a modal in negative sentences and interrogative sentences:  
  - How **dare** you open my private letters?  
  - He **dare not** speak against the decision of the team.  

#### 4. USED TO  
- Indicates a discontinued past habit, routine, or past state that no longer exists in the present:  
  - My father **used to** play cricket during his college days.  
  - There **used to** be a large banyan tree in front of our house.

### Summary Table: Modals by Communicative Function  

| Communicative Function | Primary Modals Used | Example |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **Ability (Present)** | can | She **can** speak Sanskrit fluently. |  
| **Ability (Past)** | could | When young, he **could** climb trees easily. |  
| **Permission (Informal)** | can | **Can** I use your notebook? |  
| **Permission (Formal)** | may, could | **May** I leave ten minutes early today? |  
| **Possibility (Strong/General)** | may, can | The train **may** be delayed due to fog. |  
| **Possibility (Remote/Weak)** | might, could | Take an umbrella; it **might** rain later. |  
| **Obligation / Compulsion (Internal)** | must | I **must** finish this chapter before sleeping. |  
| **Obligation / Compulsion (External Rules)** | have to / has to | You **have to** submit the project on Friday. |  
| **Moral Duty / Advice** | should, ought to | We **ought to** help those in distress. |  
| **Strict Prohibition** | must not | Students **must not** bring cellphones into the exam hall. |  
| **Absence of Obligation** | need not (needn't) | You **need not** wait here any longer. |  
| **Polite Request / Offer** | would, could, shall | **Would** you mind passing the water jug? |  
| **Determination / Promise** | will | I **will** win the first prize this time. |  
| **Discontinued Past Habit** | used to, would | We **used to** visit the village every vacation. |
