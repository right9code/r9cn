## Chapter 07: Clauses — Noun Clause and Relative Clause

A **clause** is a group of related words containing both a **subject** and a **finite verb**. Unlike a phrase, a clause has a predicating verb and makes a more complete unit of meaning.

| Unit | Has Subject? | Has Finite Verb? | Stands Alone? |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Phrase** | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **Clause (Subordinate)** | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Sentence / Main Clause** | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

---

## PART I: THE NOUN CLAUSE

### Definition

A **Noun Clause** is a subordinate clause that performs the grammatical function of a **noun** within a sentence. It answers the question **"What?"** or **"Whom?"** posed to the main verb.

**E.g.**
- I know **that he is honest**. *(I know what? → that he is honest = Noun Clause)*
- **What she said** surprised the entire class. *(What surprised them? → What she said = Noun Clause)*

---

### Words That Introduce Noun Clauses

| Category | Connectors / Introduction Words |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Subordinating conjunction** | that |
| **Yes/No doubt** | if, whether |
| **Interrogative pronouns** | what, who, whom, whose, which |
| **Interrogative adverbs** | when, where, why, how |
| **Compound relative** | whatever, whoever, whomever, whichever, whenever, wherever, however |

---

### Functions of Noun Clauses

| Function | Explanation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Subject of a verb** | The noun clause acts as the subject | **That she passed the exam** delighted everyone. |
| **2. Object of a transitive verb** | Answers *what/whom* after the verb | He admitted **that he had made a mistake**. |
| **3. Object of a preposition** | Follows a preposition | Pay attention to **what the teacher says**. |
| **4. Subject complement (Predicate noun)** | Follows a linking verb | The truth is **that no one was at fault**. |
| **5. In apposition to a noun** | Explains an abstract noun (*fact, news, idea, belief*) | The news **that the school won the trophy** spread fast. |

---

### Word Order Rule in Noun Clauses

After a question word connector, **noun clauses always use assertive (statement) word order**, not question word order.

| Direct Question | Noun Clause (Assertive Word Order) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Where **does** she live? | I know where she **lives**. ❌~~I know where does she live.~~ |
| What **is** his name? | Tell me what his **name is**. ❌~~Tell me what is his name.~~ |
| How **did** they manage? | I wonder how they **managed**. ❌~~I wonder how did they manage.~~ |

## PART II: THE RELATIVE CLAUSE (ADJECTIVE CLAUSE)

### Definition

A **Relative Clause** (also called an **Adjective Clause**) is a subordinate clause that **modifies or qualifies a noun or pronoun** (called the **antecedent**) in the main clause. It works exactly like an adjective.

**E.g.**
- The student **who topped the exam** received a scholarship. *(Which student? → who topped the exam)*
- I found the book **that you had recommended**. *(Which book? → that you had recommended)*

---

### Relative Pronouns and Relative Adverbs

| Relative Word | Refers To | Function in Clause | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **who** | Persons | Subject | The boy **who won** the race is my cousin. |
| **whom** | Persons | Object | The doctor **whom we consulted** was experienced. |
| **whose** | Persons / Things | Possession | The child **whose bag was stolen** was in tears. |
| **which** | Animals / Things | Subject or Object | The car **which he bought** is brand new. |
| **that** | Persons, Animals, Things | Defining clause only | The pen **that I lost** was a gift from my father. |
| **where** | Places | Relative adverb | This is the school **where I studied**. |
| **when** | Time | Relative adverb | I remember the night **when the storm came**. |
| **why** | Reason | Relative adverb | I know the reason **why he was absent**. |

---

### Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

| Feature | Defining (Restrictive) | Non-Defining (Non-Restrictive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Identifies *which* person/thing is meant | Adds extra information about an already identified noun |
| **If removed** | Sentence loses its essential meaning | Sentence still makes complete sense |
| **Punctuation** | **No commas** | **Commas** before and after the clause |
| **'That' allowed?** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| **Example** | The man **who called yesterday** is my uncle. | My uncle, **who called yesterday**, is a doctor. |

---

### When to Use *Who* vs. *Whom*

- **Who** → subject of the relative clause (replace with *he/she/they*)
  - The woman **who** called is my aunt. *(She called → subject)*
- **Whom** → object of the relative clause (replace with *him/her/them*)
  - The teacher **whom** everyone admires has retired. *(Everyone admires *her* → object)*

---

### When to Use *Which* vs. *That*

- **That** → used in **defining** clauses for persons, animals, and things.
- **Which** → used in **non-defining** clauses (with commas) and after prepositions.
  - This is the only book **that** can help you now. *(Defining)*
  - His latest novel, **which** has sold millions of copies, won the Booker Prize. *(Non-defining)*

---

### Noun Clause vs. Relative Clause — Side-by-Side

| Feature | Noun Clause | Relative Clause |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Grammatical role | Acts as a **noun** | Acts as an **adjective** |
| Preceded by | A **verb**, **preposition**, or starts the sentence | Immediately follows the **noun / pronoun (antecedent)** |
| Key question | Answers **"What?" / "Whom?"** | Answers **"Which one?" / "What kind of?"** |
| Example A | I know **where she lives**. *(Know what?)* | I visited the city **where she lives**. *(Which city?)* |
| Example B | Tell me **what happened**. *(Tell what?)* | The event **that happened** was unforgettable. *(Which event?)* |

#### VI. Error Correction

| No. | Incorrect | Correct | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. | which | **who** | *Boy* is a person; use *who* as subject |
| 2. | who's | **whose** | *Whose* = possessive; *who's* = who is |
| 3. | in it | *(remove 'in it')* | *Where* already replaces 'in it' — no repetition |
| 4. | him | *(remove 'him')* | *Whom* already serves as the object — no repetition |
| 5. | that | **who** | Non-defining clause (with comma) cannot use *that* |
| 6. | which its | **whose** | Use *whose* to show possession |
| 7. | which | **where* / **at which** | School is a place — use *where* (or *in which*) |
