### Clauses (Noun, Relative, and Adverb Clauses)  

---

#### 1. Fundamentals: What is a Clause?

A **clause** is a group of words that contains both a **subject** and a **finite verb** (a verb that has a specific tense and agrees with the subject). It forms part of a sentence or can sometimes stand as a sentence on its own.

##### Phrase vs. Clause vs. Sentence

| Unit | Has a Subject? | Has a Finite Verb? | Makes Complete Sense on Its Own? | Example |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Phrase** | ❌ No | ❌ No | ❌ No | *in the morning* |
| **Subordinate Clause** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | ❌ No (needs a main clause) | *when the sun rose* |
| **Main Clause / Sentence** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | *The birds began to sing.* |

---

##### The Architecture of Clauses

* **Main Clause (Independent Clause):** Expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a simple sentence.  
  * *Example:* **The train arrived**, *after we had waited for an hour.*
* **Subordinate Clause (Dependent Clause):** Contains a subject and verb but depends on the main clause to make complete sense. It is connected using a subordinating conjunction or a relative word.  
  * Subordinate clauses are divided into three functional categories:
    1. **Noun Clause** (acts as a noun)
    2. **Relative / Adjective Clause** (acts as an adjective modifying a noun/pronoun)
    3. **Adverb Clause** (acts as an adverb modifying a verb, adjective, or adverb)

---

#### 2. The Noun Clause

##### Definition
A **Noun Clause** is a subordinate clause that performs the job of a **noun** in a sentence.

> **Identification Trick:**  
> Ask **"What?"** or **"Whom?"** to the main verb. If the clause answers that question, it is a **Noun Clause**.  
> * *He knows **that the earth is round**.* $\rightarrow$ He knows **what?** $\rightarrow$ *that the earth is round*.

---

##### Connectors Used in Noun Clauses

* **Conjunctions:** *that, if, whether*
* **Question Pronouns:** *who, whom, whose, what, which*
* **Question Adverbs:** *when, where, why, how*
* **Compound Relatives:** *whatever, whoever, whomever, whichever, wherever, whenever*

---

##### The 5 Grammatical Functions of a Noun Clause

A noun clause can occupy any position that a single noun can occupy:

###### 1. Subject of a Verb
The clause comes before the verb and acts as the performer or topic of the action.
* **That she won the scholarship** delighted her parents.  
  *(What delighted them? $\rightarrow$ That she won the scholarship)*
* **What he told the police** remains a secret.

###### 2. Object of a Transitive Verb
The clause comes directly after an action verb and receives the action.
* The doctor confirmed **that the patient was out of danger**.  
  *(The doctor confirmed what? $\rightarrow$ that the patient was out of danger)*
* I cannot decide **which course I should pursue**.

###### 3. Object of a Preposition
The clause directly follows a preposition (*to, in, at, on, about, of, upon*).
* Pay close attention to **what your teacher explains**.  
  *(Preposition: to $\rightarrow$ Object: what your teacher explains)*
* Success depends on **how hard you prepare**.

###### 4. Subject Complement (Predicate Noun)
The clause follows a linking verb (*is, are, was, were, seems, appears*) and explains the subject.
* The main problem is **that we have run out of funds**.  
* My belief was **that the team would bounce back**.

###### 5. In Apposition to a Noun or Pronoun
The clause stands right next to an abstract noun (*fact, news, idea, rumour, belief, discovery*) to explain what that noun actually is.
* The news **that the flight was cancelled** upset the passengers.  
  *(What was the news? $\rightarrow$ that the flight was cancelled)*
* The theory **that dinosaurs were wiped out by an asteroid** is widely accepted.

---

##### Critical Board Exam Rules for Noun Clauses

###### Rule A: Assertive Word Order (Subject + Verb)
Never use question word order (Verb + Subject) inside a noun clause. Do not invert the subject and auxiliary verb, and never use a question mark unless the entire main sentence is a question.

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tell me where **are you going**. | Tell me where **you are going**. | Use statement word order: Subject (*you*) before Verb (*are going*). |
| I wonder what **is his name**. | I wonder what **his name is**. | Verb (*is*) goes after the subject (*his name*). |
| She asked me how **did I solve** it. | She asked me how **I solved** it. | Remove auxiliary *did* and use simple past *solved*. |

###### Rule B: "That" vs. "Whether / If"
* Use **that** when there is certainty or a confirmed statement:  
  * *I know **that** he is honest.*
* Use **whether / if** when there is doubt, uncertainty, or an indirect yes/no question:  
  * *I do not know **whether** he will attend the meeting.*  
  * ❌ *I doubt that he will come.* $\rightarrow$ ✅ *I doubt **whether / if** he will come.*

#### 3. The Relative Clause (Adjective Clause)

##### Definition
A **Relative Clause** (also called an **Adjective Clause**) is a subordinate clause that qualifies, describes, or restricts a noun or pronoun in the main clause. The word it modifies is called the **antecedent**.

> **Identification Trick:**  
> A relative clause immediately follows a noun/pronoun and answers: **"Which one?"** or **"What kind of?"**  
> * *The student **who topped the school** received a medal.* $\rightarrow$ **Which student?** $\rightarrow$ *who topped the school*.

---

##### Relative Pronouns and Relative Adverbs

| Connector | Refers To | Role in the Clause | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **who** | People | Subject | The girl **who won the prize** is my sister. |
| **whom** | People | Object | The author **whom we invited** arrived on time. |
| **whose** | People / Things | Possession | The boy **whose bicycle was stolen** reported it. |
| **which** | Animals / Things | Subject / Object | The phone **which broke yesterday** was brand new. |
| **that** | People / Animals / Things | Defining clause only | The book **that you lent me** was thrilling. |
| **where** | Places | Adverbial (location) | This is the town **where I grew up**. |
| **when** | Time | Adverbial (time) | I remember the day **when we first met**. |
| **why** | Reason | Adverbial (cause) | Tell me the reason **why you are late**. |

---

##### Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Understanding the distinction between these two types is vital for punctuation and pronoun choice:

| Feature | Defining (Restrictive) Clause | Non-Defining (Non-Restrictive) Clause |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Essential to identify *which* person or thing is meant. | Adds extra, non-essential background information. |
| **If removed?** | The sentence becomes vague or loses its core meaning. | The sentence still makes complete, specific sense. |
| **Punctuation** | **No commas** are used. | Enclosed in **commas** (before and after). |
| **Can use "that"?** | ✅ Yes (*that* is standard). | ❌ Never use *that* (use *who* or *which* only). |
| **Example** | *The doctors **who treated the boy** were skilled.* | *Dr. Sen, **who treated the boy**, is a skilled surgeon.* |

---

##### Crucial Grammar Rules for Relative Clauses

###### 1. "Who" vs. "Whom" Test
* Replace the relative pronoun with **he/she/they** $\rightarrow$ use **who** (Subject).
* Replace the relative pronoun with **him/her/them** $\rightarrow$ use **whom** (Object).
  * *The student [who / whom] answered first was praised.* $\rightarrow$ **He** answered $\rightarrow$ **who**.
  * *The teacher [who / whom] the students admire has retired.* $\rightarrow$ They admire **him** $\rightarrow$ **whom**.

###### 2. The Special Reign of "That"
Use **that** instead of *which* or *who* when the antecedent is preceded by:
* Superlative degrees (*the best, the tallest, the most interesting*)
* Restrictive modifiers: *the only, the same, the very*
* Indefinite pronouns: *all, none, little, few, everything, nothing, anything*
  * *He is the **best** teacher **that** I have ever had.* (Not *whom*)
  * *This is the **only** pencil **that** I have left.* (Not *which*)
  * *All **that** glitters is not gold.* (Not *which*)

###### 3. Omission of the Relative Pronoun (Contact Clauses)
You can omit the relative pronoun **only when it functions as the object** of the relative clause:
* ✅ *The book **(that)** I bought yesterday is interesting.* (Subject of clause is *I*, verb is *bought* $\rightarrow$ pronoun is the object $\rightarrow$ can omit).
* ❌ *The boy **who** broke the window ran away.* (Pronoun is the subject of *broke* $\rightarrow$ cannot omit).

###### 4. Reduced Relative Clauses (Participial Phrases)
You can shorten defining relative clauses by removing the relative pronoun and the auxiliary verb:
* **Active Verb $\rightarrow$ Present Participle (-ing):**  
  * *The man **who is standing** near the gate is my uncle.* $\rightarrow$ *The man **standing** near the gate is my uncle.*
* **Passive Verb $\rightarrow$ Past Participle (V3):**  
  * *The bridge **that was constructed** in 1910 collapsed.* $\rightarrow$ *The bridge **constructed** in 1910 collapsed.*

#### 4. The Adverb Clause

##### Definition
An **Adverb Clause** is a subordinate clause that modifies a **verb, adjective, or adverb** in the main clause. It explains circumstances such as time, place, reason, purpose, condition, concession, manner, or degree.

> **Punctuation Rule for Adverb Clauses:**  
> * If the Adverb Clause begins the sentence, place a **comma** after it:  
>   * *Although he worked hard, he did not pass.*
> * If the Adverb Clause follows the main clause, **no comma** is required:  
>   * *He did not pass although he worked hard.*

---

##### The 8 Types of Adverb Clauses

###### 1. Adverb Clause of Time
Answers **"When?"** the action occurs.  
* **Conjunctions:** *when, whenever, while, as, after, before, since, until, till, as soon as, no sooner...than, hardly...when*
* *Example:* **As soon as** *the bell rang, the children dispersed.*
* *Example:* *She has not visited us* **since** *she relocated to Pune.*

###### 2. Adverb Clause of Place
Answers **"Where?"** the action occurs.  
* **Conjunctions:** *where, wherever, anywhere*
* *Example:* *You may park your vehicle* **wherever** *space is available.*

###### 3. Adverb Clause of Cause / Reason
Answers **"Why?"** the action occurs.  
* **Conjunctions:** *because, since, as, for, considering that*
* *Example:* *The train was cancelled* **because** *the tracks were flooded.*
* *Example:* **Since** *you are already here, let us begin the meeting.*

###### 4. Adverb Clause of Purpose
Answers **"With what aim / goal?"** the action was taken.  
* **Conjunctions:** *so that, in order that, lest*
* *Example:* *He woke up at 5:00 AM* **so that** *he could revise his notes.*
* *Important Note on "Lest":* **Lest** means "for fear that" and is always followed by **should** (never *will* or *would*):  
  * ✅ *Walk carefully* **lest** *you* **should** *stumble.*  
  * ❌ *Walk carefully lest you will stumble.*

###### 5. Adverb Clause of Condition
Answers **"Under what terms?"** the action will happen.  
* **Conjunctions:** *if, unless, provided that, as long as, in case, supposing*
* *Example:* **Unless** *you submit your assignment today, you will lose marks.*
* *Example:* *Carry an umbrella* **in case** *it rains.*

###### 6. Adverb Clause of Concession / Contrast
Expresses an unexpected contrast or opposition (happens despite the condition).  
* **Conjunctions:** *though, although, even though, even if, whereas, while*
* *Example:* **Although** *the questions were difficult, Priya attempted all of them.*
* *Example:* *He kept working* **even though** *he was running a fever.*

###### 7. Adverb Clause of Manner
Answers **"How / In what way?"** the action is performed.  
* **Conjunctions:** *as, as if, as though*
* *Example:* *Complete the diagram exactly* **as** *the instructor showed you.*
* *Example:* *He spoke* **as if** *he knew the secret already.*

###### 8. Adverb Clause of Degree / Comparison
Answers **"How much / To what extent?"** or compares two elements.  
* **Conjunctions:** *as...as, so...that, than, the more...the more*
* *Example:* *Rohit runs* **faster than** *his brother does.*
* *Example:* *The box was* **so** *heavy* **that** *nobody could lift it.*

---

#### 5. Master Comparison: How to Distinguish Between Clauses

A single connector word (such as *where, when, why, that*) can introduce all three types of clauses depending on its syntactic role.

| Connector | Noun Clause (Answers: *What?*) | Relative Clause (Modifies: *Noun Antecedent*) | Adverb Clause (Answers: *When/Where/Why?*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **where** | *I know **where he lives**.*<br>(Know what? $\rightarrow$ Noun) | *This is the **house** **where he lives**.*<br>(Which house? Modifies *house* $\rightarrow$ Relative) | *He travels **where he wants**.*<br>(Travels where? Modifies *travels* $\rightarrow$ Adverb) |
| **when** | *Tell me **when the bus departs**.*<br>(Tell what? $\rightarrow$ Noun) | *I remember the **day** **when the bus departed**.*<br>(Which day? Modifies *day* $\rightarrow$ Relative) | *The crowd cheered **when the bus departed**.*<br>(Cheered when? Modifies *cheered* $\rightarrow$ Adverb) |
| **why** | *I do not understand **why he failed**.*<br>(Understand what? $\rightarrow$ Noun) | *He gave no **reason** **why he failed**.*<br>(Which reason? Modifies *reason* $\rightarrow$ Relative) | — |
| **that** | *She knows **that you are sincere**.*<br>(Knows what? $\rightarrow$ Noun) | *The **gift** **that you gave me** is precious.*<br>(Which gift? Modifies *gift* $\rightarrow$ Relative) | *He worked so hard **that he passed easily**.*<br>(Result/Degree $\rightarrow$ Adverb) |

---

#### 6. Common Traps & Exam Errors to Avoid

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *I know where **does she work**.* | *I know where **she works**.* | Noun clauses take statement word order, not question word order. |
| *This is the author **who's** book won the prize.* | *This is the author **whose** book won the prize.* | *Who's* = who is / who has. *Whose* = possessive pronoun. |
| *This is the town where I was born **in it**.* | *This is the town where I was born.* | *Where* already contains the prepositional meaning; *in it* is redundant. |
| *The car **that** the tire was punctured stopped.* | *The car **whose** tire was punctured stopped.* | Use *whose* to show possession for both people and objects. |
| *My father, **that** is an engineer, works in Noida.* | *My father, **who** is an engineer, works in Noida.* | Never use *that* in non-defining clauses (clauses with commas). |
| *Work hard lest you **will** fail.* | *Work hard lest you **should** fail.* | *Lest* is strictly followed by the modal verb *should*. |
| *The news **which** he passed made us happy.* | *The news **that** he passed made us happy.* | A noun clause in apposition to *news, fact, idea* requires *that*. |

##### Solutions for Exercise 3: Error Detection & Correction

1. **Incorrect:** *why did you miss*  
   * **Correction:** *why you missed*  
   * **Rule:** Noun clauses must follow assertive statement word order (Subject + Verb), never interrogative inversion (*did you miss*).  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** Could you please explain to me why you missed the practical examination yesterday?

2. **Incorrect:** *that*  
   * **Correction:** *who*  
   * **Rule:** The relative pronoun *that* is never used in non-defining clauses enclosed in commas. Use *who* for individuals.  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam, who was known as the Missile Man of India, was deeply loved by students.

3. **Incorrect:** *which its*  
   * **Correction:** *whose*  
   * **Rule:** Use the possessive relative pronoun *whose* rather than combining *which* with a possessive pronoun (*its*).  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** She bought a luxury watch whose strap was made of pure leather.

4. **Incorrect:** *will*  
   * **Correction:** *should*  
   * **Rule:** The conjunction *lest* is followed strictly by the modal auxiliary *should*.  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** Hurry up lest you should miss the morning school bus.

5. **Incorrect:** *there*  
   * **Correction:** *(remove 'there')*  
   * **Rule:** The relative adverb *where* already expresses the location; adding *there* is a redundant repetition.  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** This is the exact hospital where my younger brother was born in 2012.

6. **Incorrect:** *because*  
   * **Correction:** *that*  
   * **Rule:** Sentences beginning with "The reason why..." should be completed with a noun clause introduced by *that*, not *because* (which creates a redundant expression of cause).  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** The reason why he remained absent was that he had missed the early morning train.

7. **Incorrect:** *which*  
   * **Correction:** *that*  
   * **Rule:** After indefinite antecedents like *all, none, everything, little*, always use the relative pronoun *that*.  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** All that glitters on the surface is not necessarily pure gold.

8. **Incorrect:** *that*  
   * **Correction:** *whether / if*  
   * **Rule:** When the main verb expresses doubt or uncertainty, use *whether* or *if* to introduce the noun clause instead of *that*.  
   * **Corrected Sentence:** The manager doubted whether the new intern could handle such a demanding assignment alone.
