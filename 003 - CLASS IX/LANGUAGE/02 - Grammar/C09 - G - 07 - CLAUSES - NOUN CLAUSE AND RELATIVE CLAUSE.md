## Chapter 07: Clauses — Noun Clause and Relative Clause  

A **Clause** is a group of words that forms part of a sentence and contains a **Subject** and a **Finite Verb** (predicate).  

### Differences Between Phrase, Clause, and Sentence  

- **Phrase:** A group of words that makes sense but not complete sense and has **no subject-verb combination** (e.g., *in the morning*, *with great joy*).  
- **Clause:** A group of words containing a **subject and a finite verb** (e.g., *when the bell rang*, *that he is innocent*).  
- **Sentence:** A group of words containing at least one independent clause that expresses a **complete and standalone thought** (e.g., *The bell rang loudly.*).


### PART 1: The Noun Clause  

#### 1. Definition  
A **Noun Clause** is a subordinate (dependent) clause that performs the function of a **noun** in a sentence.  

**Identification Trick:**  
To identify a noun clause, locate the verb in the main clause and ask the question **"What?"** or **"Whom?"**. The answer will be the noun clause.  
- *Sentence:* I know **that the earth is round**.  
  *(Question: I know what? $\rightarrow$ Answer: that the earth is round = Noun Clause)*  

---

#### 2. Connectors Introducing Noun Clauses  
Noun clauses are introduced by:  
- The conjunction **`that`**  
- Connectors **`if`** / **`whether`** (expressing doubt/choice)  
- Interrogative pronouns/adverbs: **`what, who, whom, whose, which, where, when, why, how`**  
- Compound relative pronouns: **`whatever, whoever, whomever, whichever`**  

---

#### 3. Syntactic Functions of Noun Clauses  

A noun clause can function in five primary ways:  

| Function | Explanation | Example |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **1. Subject of a Verb** | Comes before the main verb and acts as the subject. | • **What he said** surprised everyone.<br>• **That she succeeded** made her parents proud. |  
| **2. Object of a Transitive Verb** | Follows a transitive verb and answers *what/whom*. | • She knows **where the keys are kept**.<br>• I believe **that honesty always wins**. |  
| **3. Object of a Preposition** | Comes immediately after a preposition. | • Pay careful attention **to what the teacher explains**.<br>• There is no truth **in what he claimed**. |  
| **4. Subject Complement (Predicate Noun)** | Follows a linking verb (*is, was, were, seems, appears*) and completes the subject. | • My greatest hope is **that we will achieve our dreams**.<br>• Life is **what we make of it**. |  
| **5. In Apposition to a Noun / Pronoun** | Explains or renames a preceding noun (usually abstract words like *news, fact, rumour, belief*). | • The news **that the flight was cancelled** shocked us.<br>• The fact **that he worked hard** ensured his success. |  

---

### PART 2: The Relative Clause (Adjective Clause)  

#### 1. Definition  
A **Relative Clause** (also known as an **Adjective Clause**) is a subordinate clause that qualifies, modifies, or describes a **noun or pronoun (the antecedent)** in the main clause, functioning exactly like an adjective.  

**Identification Trick:**  
To identify a relative clause, look at the noun/pronoun it follows and ask: **"Which one?"**, **"What kind of?"**, or **"Whose?"**.  
- *Sentence:* This is the boy **who won the national chess championship**.  
  *(Which boy? $\rightarrow$ who won the national chess championship = Relative Clause)*  

---

#### 2. Relative Pronouns and Relative Adverbs  

| Relative Word | Refers To | Function in the Clause | Example |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **Who** | Persons | Subject of the relative clause | The teacher **who taught us science** has retired. |  
| **Whom** | Persons | Object of the relative clause | The doctor **whom we consulted** was very experienced. |  
| **Whose** | Persons / Living things | Possessive (ownership) | The student **whose model won first prize** was felicitated. |  
| **Which** | Animals / Inanimate things | Subject or Object | I bought a bicycle **which has gear shifts**. |  
| **That** | Persons, animals, things | Defining clause (Subject/Object) | The book **that you recommended** was fascinating. |  
| **Where** | Places | Relative adverb of place | This is the village **where I was born**. |  
| **When** | Time | Relative adverb of time | I remember the day **when we first met**. |  
| **Why** | Reasons | Relative adverb of reason | This is the reason **why he remained silent**. |  

---

#### 3. Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses  

Relative clauses are divided into two crucial categories:  

#### A. Defining (Restrictive) Relative Clauses  
- Gives **essential information** that identifies or restricts the noun.  
- If removed, the sentence loses its primary meaning or becomes incomplete.  
- **No commas** are used.  
- Can use *that* instead of *which* or *who*.  
  - *Example:* The students **who study regularly** score good marks.  
    *(Meaning: Only those specific students who study regularly)*  

#### B. Non-Defining (Non-Restrictive) Relative Clauses  
- Gives **extra, non-essential information** about an already identified noun.  
- If removed, the sentence still makes clear, complete sense.  
- **Enclosed within commas (`, ... ,`)**.  
- **Cannot use `that`** (must use *who, whom, whose, which*).  
  - *Example:* Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**, who was a renowned scientist,** served as the President of India.  
    *(Extra information: The main sentence 'Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam served as the President of India' is already clear).*  

---

### Side-by-Side Comparison: Noun Clause vs. Relative Clause  

| Feature | Noun Clause | Relative Clause |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **Primary Role** | Acts as a Noun (Subject, Object, Complement) | Acts as an Adjective (modifies a Noun/Pronoun) |  
| **Preceding Word** | Preceded by a **Verb**, **Preposition**, or stands alone at the start of a sentence | Preceded immediately by the **Noun / Pronoun (Antecedent)** it describes |  
| **Key Question Asked** | Answers **"What?"** or **"Whom?"** | Answers **"Which one?"** or **"What kind of?"** |  
| **Example A** | I know **where he lives**. *(I know what? $\rightarrow$ Noun Clause)* | I visited the house **where he lives**. *(Which house? $\rightarrow$ Relative Clause)* |  
| **Example B** | Tell me **what happened**. *(Tell me what? $\rightarrow$ Noun Clause)* | The event **that happened yesterday** was memorable. *(Which event? $\rightarrow$ Relative Clause)* |  

---

### Synthesizing / Combining Sentences Using Relative Clauses  

1. **Rule for People (Subject): Use *who***  
   - *Sentence 1:* The man is an architect.  
   - *Sentence 2:* He designed this green building.  
   $\rightarrow$ The man **who designed this green building** is an architect.  

2. **Rule for People (Object): Use *whom / who***  
   - *Sentence 1:* We met a famous author.  
   - *Sentence 2:* Everyone admires her books.  
   $\rightarrow$ We met a famous author **whom everyone admires**.  

3. **Rule for Possession: Use *whose***  
   - *Sentence 1:* I have a friend.  
   - *Sentence 2:* His father is an astronaut.  
   $\rightarrow$ I have a friend **whose father is an astronaut**.  

4. **Rule for Things/Objects: Use *which / that***  
   - *Sentence 1:* The laptop was expensive.  
   - *Sentence 2:* I bought it last week.  
   $\rightarrow$ The laptop **which I bought last week** was expensive.
