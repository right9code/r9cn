## Chapter 04: Determiners  

**Determiners** are words that are placed before a noun or noun phrase to identify, modify, limit, or determine its meaning. They indicate whether the noun is specific or general, singular or plural, near or far, and quantify the amount or proportion of the noun. Determiners are also referred to as "fixing words".  

Unlike descriptive adjectives (which describe the quality, size, or color of a noun and can have degrees of comparison like *tall, taller, tallest*), determiners do not have degrees of comparison and simply point out or restrict the noun's reference.  

---

### Characteristics of Determiners  

1. A determiner always comes **before** a noun (e.g., *this book*, *every student*).  
2. If there are adjectives modifying the noun, the determiner comes **before the adjective** (e.g., *a bright young student*, *these beautiful flowers*).  
3. In modern English grammar, a singular countable noun generally cannot appear alone without a determiner (e.g., we say *The boy is reading a book*, not *Boy is reading book*).  
4. Determiners specify quantity, possession, definiteness, or distribution.  

---

### 1. Articles (A, An, The)  

Articles are the most common determiners. They are divided into:  
- **Indefinite Articles:** *A*, *An* (refer to non-specific singular countable nouns).  
- **Definite Article:** *The* (refers to specific persons, things, or previously mentioned nouns).  

#### Use of Indefinite Articles: *A* and *An*  
- The choice between *a* and *an* depends entirely on the **initial sound** of the word following it, not on the spelling:  
  - **'A'** is used before a singular countable noun beginning with a **consonant sound**:  
    - *a boy, a horse, a table*  
    - *a university* (starts with /juː/ consonant sound)  
    - *a European* (starts with /juː/ consonant sound)  
    - *a one-rupee coin* (starts with /w/ consonant sound)  
    - *a union, a useful device*  
  - **'An'** is used before a singular countable noun beginning with a **vowel sound** (a, e, i, o, u):  
    - *an umbrella, an elephant, an orange*  
    - *an honest person* ('h' is silent, starts with /ɒ/ vowel sound)  
    - *an hour, an heir, an honourable deed*  
    - *an MLA, an MP, an FIR, an NCC cadet, an MBA* (letters pronounced with initial vowel sounds: /em/, /ef/, /en/)  

#### Use of Definite Article: *The*  
*The* is used when we refer to a particular person, animal, or thing, or one already mentioned.  
1. **When a person/thing is mentioned for the second time:**  
   - I saw **a** dog in the street. **The** dog was barking loudly.  
2. **Before unique objects in nature:**  
   - *the sun, the moon, the earth, the sky, the universe*  
3. **Before names of oceans, seas, rivers, gulfs, canals, deserts, and mountain ranges:**  
   - *the Pacific Ocean, the Arabian Sea, the Ganges, the Sahara Desert, the Himalayas, the Suez Canal*  
   - *(Note: Individual peaks do not take 'the': Mount Everest, K2)*  
4. **Before names of holy books, scriptures, and newspapers:**  
   - *the Bhagavad Gita, the Holy Quran, the Bible, the Times of India*  
5. **Before superlative degrees of adjectives:**  
   - Mount Everest is **the highest** mountain peak in the world.  
   - She is **the most talented** singer in our school.  
6. **Before ordinals:**  
   - He was **the first** student to finish the paper.  
   - This is **the second** time I have reminded you.  
7. **Before musical instruments and inventions:**  
   - She plays **the violin** beautifully.  
   - **The telephone** was invented by Alexander Graham Bell.  
8. **Before plural names of countries / nations containing words like *Republic, Kingdom, States*:**  
   - *the United Kingdom (UK), the United States of America (USA), the Netherlands, the Philippines*  
9. **Before adjectives representing an entire class of people:**  
   - **The rich** should help **the poor**. *(The rich = rich people)*  

#### Omission of Articles (Zero Article)  
Articles are omitted:  
- Before proper nouns: *Raman, Delhi, India, Sunday, July*  
- Before abstract nouns used in a general sense: *Honesty is the best policy. Beauty lies in simplicity.*  
- Before names of materials/substances: *Gold is a precious metal. Water is essential for life.*  
- Before names of languages, meals, diseases, and sports: *He speaks French. We had lunch at 1 PM. Malaria is dangerous. They play cricket.*

### 2. Demonstratives (This, That, These, Those)  

Demonstratives point to specific objects or people in space or time:  

| Determiner | Number | Proximity (Distance) | Example |  
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |  
| **This** | Singular / Uncountable | Near (in place or present time) | **This** pen writes smoothly. / **This** water is fresh. |  
| **That** | Singular / Uncountable | Distant (in place or past time) | **That** mountain looks picturesque. / **That** day was memorable. |  
| **These** | Plural | Near (in place or time) | **These** mangoes are sweet and juicy. |  
| **Those** | Plural | Distant (in place or time) | **Those** houses on the hill belong to the villagers. |  

---

### 3. Possessives (My, Your, His, Her, Its, Our, Their, Whose)  

Possessives indicate ownership, belonging, or relationship with the following noun:  
- **My** elder brother is an aerospace engineer.  
- Is this **your** water bottle?  
- The puppy wagged **its** tail happily. *(Note: 'its' = possessive determiner; 'it's' = it is)*  
- We must preserve **our** cultural heritage.  
- The students presented **their** science working models.  
- **Whose** notebook is lying on the teacher's table?  

---

### 4. Quantifiers  

Quantifiers specify the quantity, amount, or degree of the noun.  

#### A. *Some* and *Any*  
- **Some:** Used with countable (plural) and uncountable nouns in **affirmative (positive) sentences**, and in **polite offers/requests** where 'yes' is expected:  
  - There is **some** milk in the refrigerator. *(Uncountable)*  
  - I bought **some** storybooks from the fair. *(Countable plural)*  
  - Would you like **some** warm soup? *(Polite offer)*  
  - Could you lend me **some** money? *(Polite request)*  
- **Any:** Used with countable and uncountable nouns in **negative sentences** and **general questions**:  
  - There isn't **any** water in the jug. *(Negative)*  
  - Do you have **any** doubts regarding the theorem? *(Question)*  
  - You can choose **any** book you like from the shelf. *(Meaning 'it doesn't matter which')*  

#### B. *Much* and *Many*  
- **Much:** Used with **uncountable nouns** to denote a large quantity/amount (mostly in negative sentences and questions):  
  - He does not have **much** patience.  
  - How **much** time do we have left?  
- **Many:** Used with **plural countable nouns** to denote a large number:  
  - **Many** students participated in the science exhibition.  
  - Are there **many** historical monuments in your city?  

#### C. *Little, A Little, The Little* (Used with Uncountable Nouns)  
- **Little:** Almost none, negligible amount (has a **negative meaning**):  
  - There is **little** hope of his recovery. *(Almost no hope)*  
- **A Little:** A small quantity, some at least (has a **positive meaning**):  
  - A **little** knowledge is a dangerous thing. *(Some knowledge)*  
  - There is a **little** milk left for your coffee.  
- **The Little:** The specific small quantity that is available (all that is there):  
  - He spent **the little** money he had saved on books.  

#### D. *Few, A Few, The Few* (Used with Plural Countable Nouns)  
- **Few:** Almost none, negligible in number (has a **negative meaning**):  
  - **Few** students attended the optional session in the rain. *(Almost no students)*  
- **A Few:** A small number, at least some (has a **positive meaning**):  
  - **A few** students scored more than 95% in mathematics.  
- **The Few:** The specific small number mentioned (all of that small group):  
  - **The few** friends he had remained loyal during his hardships.  

#### E. *Each* and *Every* (Distributive Determiners)  
- **Each:** Refers to individual members of a small, definite group (two or more) considered separately:  
  - **Each** of the two participants received a certificate.  
  - There were trees on **each** side of the road.  
- **Every:** Refers to all members of a larger, indefinite group considered collectively (three or more):  
  - **Every** child deserves access to quality education.  
  - The school bell rings **every** forty-five minutes.  

#### F. *Either* and *Neither*  
- **Either:** One or the other of two; or each of two:  
  - You can park your car on **either** side of the avenue.  
- **Neither:** Not the one nor the other of two (negative sense; followed by singular noun/verb):  
  - **Neither** candidate was suitable for the post.  

#### G. *All, Both, Enough, Several, Another, Other*  
- **All:** Denotes the entire quantity or total number (*All students were present; All the sugar was used*).  
- **Both:** Refers to two persons/things together (*Both brothers are doctors*).  
- **Enough:** Adequate quantity or number (*We have enough food for everyone; There are enough chairs*).  
- **Several:** More than two but not very many (*Several people raised questions*).  
- **Another:** One more in addition to (used with singular countable nouns: *Have another cup of tea*).  
- **Other:** Additional or remaining persons/things (used with plural or uncountable nouns: *Other students were waiting outside*).

### PRACTICE QUESTIONS  

#### I. Fill in the blanks with suitable determiners.  

1. _______ sun rises in the east and sets in the west.  
2. Honesty is _______ virtue which _______ honest person cherishes.  
3. Would you like to have _______ more hot coffee?  
4. He had _______ friends in the new city, so he felt very lonely. *(few / a few / little)*  
5. _______ of the two boys was able to solve the tricky riddle. *(Neither / Every / All)*  
6. There is _______ hope of finding the lost necklace now. *(little / few / many)*  
7. She has spent _______ money she had in her piggy bank. *(the little / the few / a few)*  
8. Are there _______ vacant seats in the auditorium for the evening show? *(any / some / much)*  
9. _______ student in the class must submit the project by Monday. *(Every / All / Several)*  
10. He bought _______ umbrella and _______ one-way train ticket to Jaipur.
