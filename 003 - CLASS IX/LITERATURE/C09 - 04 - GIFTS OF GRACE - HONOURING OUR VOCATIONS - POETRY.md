
## Gifts of Grace: Honouring Our Vocations  

*I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear;*  

*Those of craftsperson, each one celebrating their craft, woven with colours and myriad hues.*  

*The artisans with lutes, each hailing varied emotions and celebrating dreams, echoing in the streets.*  

*The carpenters celebrating; they create anything out of wood with mathematical precision,*  

*The electricians humming; they get ready for work, work with cables and wires to brighten our lives,*  


*The boatmen gathering their nets from the shore,*  
*sailing, and singing while at work, return to tell*  
*the tales of life at sea,*  

*The shoemakers affirming the quality of their*  
*work, for the feet that walk, dance, run, jump,*  
*return home.*  

*The delicious singing of the cook, or the rhythm of*  
*designer, mason, each celebrating what belongs to*  
*them and to none else,*  

*The voice of their vocation is the voice of their*  
*identity. I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied*  
*voices I hear!*

### II. Let us appreciate the poem  
1. Rhyme Scheme and Lineation  
(i) The poem is written in free verse and does not follow a strict rhyme scheme.  
(ii) The varying line lengths create a natural, musical rhythm that reflects the different sounds and motions of different occupations.  
(iii) Most lines begin with "The" followed by a specific worker (e.g., "The carpenters", "The electricians") and describe their specific action or contribution.  

2. Speaker  
(i) The speaker appears to be an observer or poet who loves and respects all workers. His/Her role is to celebrate the dignity of labor and show how every worker contributes to the nation.  

3. Tone and Mood  
- A. The tone is **reverential** and **celebratory**, depicting a sense of admiration and respect for the artisans and craftspersons.  
- B. There is a **joyful** mood throughout the poem, capturing the vibrancy and richness of cultural traditions and skills.  

4. Imagery  
(i) Two visual images:  
- "woven with colours and myriad hues" (craftspersons creating colorful crafts).  
- "gathering their nets from the shore" (boatmen at the beach).  
(ii) *The poem includes auditory imagery through mentions of artisans with lutes, **electricians humming**, and **the delicious singing of the cook**, emphasising the sounds associated with each vocation.*  

5. Metaphor  
(i) True. Equating singing to something delicious creates a pleasing sensory connection.  

6. Personification  
(i) "The voice of their vocation is the voice of their identity."  

7. Repetition  
(i) The poet repeats this line at the beginning and end to emphasize the main theme: celebrating the unity, diversity, and dignity of work across the entire country.

(i) Each vocation symbolises the dignity of labor, individual identity, and the collective strength of the nation.  

## Critical Reflection  
### I. Extract-Based Questions

2. What do quality shoes help with, according to the poet?  
According to the poet, quality shoes protect and comfort human feet during their daily life journeys. They support people as they walk to work, dance in celebration, run for exercise, and jump in play, ensuring that everyone returns home safely after fulfilling their daily duties.  

3. What does 'return home' symbolise besides the literal act of returning?  
Besides the literal act of walking back home, 'return home' symbolises peace, emotional security, family warmth, and the satisfying completion of a day's honest labor. It represents the comforting end of life's daily struggles and returning to the safety of one's loved ones.  

4. Identify the phrase that tells us that every worker's contribution is distinct.  
The phrase that emphasizes the unique contribution of every worker is "each celebrating what belongs to them and to none else". This highlights that every single worker possesses a special skill, identity, and personal pride that cannot be duplicated by anyone else.  

5. Complete the following with suitable words.

### II. Short Answer Questions  
1. Why does the poet say, 'I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear'?  
The poet says this because he sees the nation's true glory and vibrant unity in the dedicated hard work, craft, and joy of its diverse working people. Every carpenter, electrician, cook, and artisan contributes their unique talent to build and celebrate the nation.  

2. What does the electrician 'humming' while getting ready for work suggest?  
The electrician humming while preparing for work suggests that he approaches his daily duty with enthusiasm, joy, and a positive mindset. It shows that he takes pride in his essential role of bringing light, power, and safety to people's homes.  

3. Explain the significance of the line, 'The voice of their vocation is the voice of their identity.'  
This line means that a person's work is not just a source of earning money, but a core part of their self-worth and identity. Work gives individuals purpose, creative expression, and a proud, meaningful place within their community and nation.  

4. Do you think the role of all the people belonging to different vocations is important in our daily lives? Support your answer with a reason.  
Yes, every vocation is essential because modern society relies on an interconnected web of skills. Masons build our homes, cooks prepare our food, electricians maintain power, and shoemakers protect our feet. Without any one of these workers, daily life would face immense disruption.  

5. Why is the poet celebrating all the vocations in the poem? Explain by giving examples from your context.  
The poet celebrates all vocations to promote equal respect for all honest labor. In our daily lives, a street cleaner who keeps our surroundings hygienic is as important as a doctor who treats illness. Celebrating all jobs breaks social prejudices and honors human dignity.  

6. How does the poet use sensory imagery to bring out the beauty of everyday work?  
The poet uses vivid visual images like colorful woven fabrics and boatmen's fishing nets alongside cheerful auditory images of lutes, humming electricians, and singing cooks. These sensory details turn ordinary daily tasks into a lively, musical, and beautiful artistic experience.
