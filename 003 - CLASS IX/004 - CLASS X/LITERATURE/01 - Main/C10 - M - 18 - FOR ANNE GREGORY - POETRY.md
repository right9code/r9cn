*This poem is a conversation between a young man and a young woman. What are they arguing about?*

Thrown into despair

5## Chapter 10: For Anne Gregory  

by WILLIAM BUTLER YEATS  

### Central Idea of the Poem  

The poem 'For Anne Gregory' is about the perception of love by different people. The poem is in the form of a dialogue between two people, Anne Gregory and another person, whose identity has not been made clear by the poet.  

The poem shows the true nature of humans as they are always carried away by the outer beauty and never pay attention to the true nature of a person. Only God can love a person for his inner beauty and not for his physical beauty.  

### Explanation of the Poem  

**STANZA 1**  

“Never shall a young man,  
Thrown into despair  
By those great honey-coloured  
Ramparts at your ear,  
Love you for yourself alone  
And not your yellow hair.”  

**Word Meanings**:  
    **Despair** - a miserable condition or hopelessness
    **Ramparts** - protecting wall around a fort, here it is used for Anne's honey-coloured hair around her ears  

**Explanation**: In these lines, the speaker (most probably the poet) says to Anne that her beautiful honey-coloured (yellowish) hair can make any young man fall hopelessly in love with her. This love is not for Anne but for her beautiful yellow hair.  

Anne's honey-coloured hair (Outer beauty) around her ears have been compared to walls, which prevents anyone from looking inside her soul. This beauty can capture any man's attention in such a way that he may never be able to look into Anne's inner beauty or character. Thus, no man can love Anne for her inner beauty and neither he can love Anne for her yellow hair as he was thrown into despair.  

**STANZA 2**  

“But I can get a hair-dye  
And set such colour there,  
Brown, or black, or carrot,  
That young men in despair  
May love me for myself alone  
And not my yellow hair.”  

**Word Meanings**:  
    **Hair-dye** - a natural or synthetic substance used to change the colour of a person's hair  

**Explanation**: In these lines, Anne replies to the speaker.  

Anne says that she can get her hair-dyed and change the colour of her hair into brown or black or carrot. She believes that such an act will make the young men love her for herself or her inner beauty and not for her outward appearance (yellow hair).  

Anne wants to convey that what is visible from the outside is very artificial. It is changeable and thus is not important or true.  

**STANZA 3**  

'I heard an old religious man  
But yesternight declare  
That he had found a text to prove  
That only God, my dear,  
Could love you for yourself alone  
And not your yellow hair.'  

**Word Meanings**:  
    **Yesternight** - last night
    **Declare** - to announce  

**Explanation**: In these lines, the speaker mentions an old religious man who had announced last night that he had found a religious text. It is written in this text that only God is capable of looking beyond the external beauty of a person.  

Here, the speaker means that humans do not have the capacity and understanding to look inside the soul of a person. Humans are always attracted towards the outer beauty. They never care to know the person behind the beautiful appearance. Therefore, the speaker finally tells Anne that only God can love her for her inner qualities and not for her yellow hair.  

### Poetic Devices Used in the Poem  

**Metaphor** - A device which compares two things or qualities which are unlike  
    - honey-coloured ramparts  

**Alliteration** - Repetition of initial consonant sounds in the same line  
    - And not your yellow hair  

**Rhyme Scheme** - abcbdb

### Short Answer (SA) Type Questions  

**[2/3 Marks each]**  

1. Between whom does the conversation in the poem takes place? What does the first speaker say to the second speaker?  

**Ans.** The poem 'For Anne Gregory' is a conversation between Anne Gregory and another speaker whose identity is not disclosed by the poet. However, we assume that it is the poet himself. The first speaker (probably the poet) tells the second speaker (Anne) that young mens would love her for her outer beauty, i.e. her beautiful yellow hair. They will not love her for herself or her inner beauty.  

2. Why would young men never love Anne Gregory for 'herself alone'?  

**Ans.** Young men would never love Anne Gregory for 'herself alone' because they are attracted to her external beauty.  

Her outward appearance, i.e. her yellow hair are so beautiful and attractive that no man can ignore it and pay attention to her inner beauty or soul.  

3. What did the old religious man declare? What did he want to prove?  

**Ans.** The old religious man declared to the poet that he had found a text the previous night. In this text it is written that only God is capable of looking beyond external beauty.  

Humans are always attracted towards the physical appearance of a person and are not capable of looking beyond external beauty. The old man wants to prove that only God can love a person for his inner beauty.  

### Long Answer (LA) Type Questions  

**[5 Marks each]**  

1. The poet in the poem 'For Anne Gregory' conveys that we should give importance to the inner beauty and not the physical appearance. Elaborate with reference to the poem.  

**Ans.** In the poem 'For Anne Gregory' a conversation between Anne Gregory and another speaker, most probably the poet, takes place. The poet through this conversation conveys that we should give importance to the inner beauty and not the physical appearance. He tells Anne Gregory that many young men fall in love with her because of her beautiful hair. They do not love her for her inner qualities.  

To such a statement, Anne replies that she would change the colour of her hair so that men would love her for her personality and inner beauty. This reply along with the poet's assertion that only God loves one for one's inner beauty, presents the poet's message clearly. The poet believes that outward appearances are artificial and hence, are not true. Thus, man must prefer internal beauty over physical appearance.  

2. God does not love a person for his/her outer appearance but for the person himself. Elaborate.  

**Ans.** God does not love any person for his/her outer appearance and richness. It is human beings who pay attention to material things. Unlike God, they prefer to see only outward beauty. They prefer things that appear pretty from outside and never look for what lies inside. In the poem, 'For Anne  

Gregory' the poet highlights this preference by man. He informs Anne Gregory that men (many young men) love her only for her beautiful yellow hair. They do not see her true nature or personality. The poet further points that it is only God who can avoid external beauty and look beyond it. He even mentions a text that proves it as truth. For the poet's outward appearances are temporary as it fades away and thus is not true. The truth for him is clear due. God does not love a person for his/her outer appearance, he loves him for his inner qualities.  

3. Do we love people because we like their appearances or we are fascinated by their physical appearance? How does Anne Gregory want to be loved? Is her wish fulfilled?  

**Ans.** It is completely right to say that we love people because of their external appearance and not for their good qualities or nature. The world today is full of showiness where people have become objects. Their value is judged through their physical beauty or how they look. People are loved because they look beautiful.  

In the poem 'For Anne Gregory' the poet asserts that Anne is loved for her beautiful yellow hair and not for her personality. However, Anne Gregory does not want to be loved for her beauty. She is ready to get her hair dyed to show the artificiality of her outward appearance. She wants to be loved for herself.  

Anne's wish does not get fulfilled. The poet tells her that it is only God who would love her for her internal qualities. Humans will go on being tempted by her beautiful yellow hair.
