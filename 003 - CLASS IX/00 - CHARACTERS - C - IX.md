## Chapter Details

### 01 — [[C09 - 01 - HOW I TAUGHT MY GRANDMOTHER TO READ|How I Taught My Grandmother to Read]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Sudha Murty | The narrator, a twelve-year-old girl who reads *Kashi Yatre* aloud to her grandmother and later becomes her first teacher |
| Krishtakka (Avva) | The narrator's sixty-two-year-old grandmother, illiterate but determined, who learns the Kannada alphabet to become independent |
| Triveni | A popular Kannada writer whose easy, convincing style and early death left a mark on Kannada literature; author of *Kashi Yatre* |
| The old lady of *Kashi Yatre* | The novel's protagonist, whose ardent wish to reach Kashi mirrors the grandmother's own longing |
| The orphan girl of *Kashi Yatre* | The young character in the novel whose wedding is funded by the old lady's savings — the story's moral heart |
| The narrator's cousins | Companions on the week-long wedding trip to the neighbouring village that leaves the grandmother without a reader |
| The grandmother's friends | Village women with whom the grandmother eagerly discusses each episode of the serial at the temple courtyard |

### 03 — [[C09 - 03 - THE POT MAKER|The Pot Maker]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Sentila | A girl in a village family of potters who dreams of becoming a pot maker like her mother and grandmother, and persists until she succeeds |
| Arenla | Sentila's mother, an expert pot maker worn down by her labour, who wants her daughter to take up weaving instead and struggles to teach her |
| Mesoba | Sentila's father, summoned before the village council to explain why Arenla withholds the craft from their daughter |
| Onula | The kind middle-aged widow who supervises the girls' dormitory and, away from the mother's shadow, teaches Sentila to make a perfect pot |
| The village council elders | The elders who summon Mesoba and warn that the community's traditional skills must be passed on, not owned |
| The expert potters | The village potters who are first amused by the little girl's insistence, but let her watch and absorb the craft |
| The villagers | The gossiping community that questions Arenla's refusal to teach, and later races to the house when she dies |

### 05 — [[C09 - 05 - WINDS OF CHANGE|Winds of Change]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| India's pankha artisans | The regional craftspersons of Rajasthan, Gujarat, Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Odisha and Bihar who make distinct fans from bamboo, sola, leather, brass and silk |
| Gujarat's home-based women workers | Industrious women who tirelessly embroider mirror-work and cross-stitch hand fans in different shapes and sizes |
| Indian tribal communities | The tribes who adopted the handicraft, embedding grass and metal with geometric patterns in white ink and red backgrounds |

### 07 — [[C09 - 07 - VITAMIN-M|Vitamin-M]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Ravi | The grandson left in charge of Grandpa during his holidays, who secretly trails him across the city and ends up following the wrong man |
| Grandpa | Ravi's seventy-five-year-old grandfather, a former lawyer who resents being babied, outwits Ravi with his yellow cap and gifts everyone on his own birthday |
| Ravi's mother (Vidya) | Grandpa's anxious, overprotective daughter, who speaks to her father like a child yet forgets his birthday — the real candidate for Vitamin-M |
| Ravi's father | The quiet provider who buys Grandpa's Tamil paper on his way home, confused by the teasing at the gift giving |
| The stranger on the bus | The commuter in white pyjamas whom Grandpa's gifted yellow cap leads Ravi to mistake for his grandfather |
| The little boy in the park | The child who innocently offers the hiding Ravi "a better place to hide" and exposes him |
| The little boy's mother | The loud mother who threatens Ravi with her umbrella for shooing her child |
| The street vendors at the tea stall | The ear-splitting comb-seller, ribbon-seller and others who surround and loudly question the hiding Ravi |

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|

### 13 — [[C09 - 13 - CARRIER OF WORDS|Carrier of Words]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Khetaram | A *Gramin Dak Sewak* for fifteen years, sole postman of Somarad Branch Post Office, who carries mail on foot across the Thar to hamlets 2.5 km from the Indo-Pakistan border |
| The Postmaster-General of Rajasthan Western Region | The official who explains that the GDS are invaluable because they deliver on foot where no other means reaches |
| The postmaster | The unnamed official who explains the special service conditions that retain the large GDS workforce |
| Budh Singh | A village elder who recalls that the scheme to hand the GDS job to patwaris was rejected because "they would not be able to do the job" |
| Mulk Raj Anand | The author of *Story of the Indian Post Office*, quoted on how much remote villagers depend on the money order |
| Panna Devi | A villager whose *dak* Khetaram sets out to deliver before sundown |
| Khetaram's family of five | The household that a single crop of bajra cannot feed, and for whom his assured GDS income is survival |
| The villagers | The desert families who trust Khetaram to read and write their letters and offer him jaggery for good news |
| The BSF soldiers | The camp men who give Khetaram a lift and a cup of tea in return for carrying their *dak* |
