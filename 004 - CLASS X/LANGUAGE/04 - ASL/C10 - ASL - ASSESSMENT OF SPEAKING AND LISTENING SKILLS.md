# ASL - ASSESSMENT OF SPEAKING AND LISTENING SKILLS  

CBSE has clearly outlined the significance of Speaking and Listening Skills as a part of co-scholastic areas of achievement by every learner and also stressed the clear integration of Assessment of Speaking and Listening Skills as a part of the curriculum.
As good communication skills raise the self-esteem of a student, CBSE essentially desires that the students acquire proficiency in it by the time they leave school. In the present day global markets, speaking and listening are considered to be the essential skills of real life. So it has been felt for a long time that CBSE must focus on assessing speaking and listening skills for qualification in examination.
Considering this, CBSE has proposed to give 5% weightage to the Assessment of Speaking and Listening Skills from 2017-18 onwards. Therefore, the Assessment of Speaking and Listening Skills will be done formally at the term-end examination. Schools may conduct ASL themselves as per the guidelines provided by the CBSE.
The overall purpose of the test is to help improve and standardise students' communicative skills. Thus, in keeping with the international standards, the CBSE has put this assessment in place.  

## LISTENING SKILLS  

The Listening Comprehension section tests the student's ability to listen for basic interpersonal, instructional and academic purposes. A number of sub-skills need to be developed in the everyday classroom transaction. Given below are some of the sub-skills of listening which need to be assessed.
    (i) Listening for specific information
    (ii) Listening for general understanding
    (iii) Predictive listening
    (iv) Inferential listening
    (v) Listening for pleasure
    (vi) Intensive listening
    (vii) Evaluative listening  

**ROLE OF EXAMINER**  

Before the Examiner is assigned the job of assessing a candidate's performance in any of the skills that is to be assessed.
It is extremely important to keep in mind the factors listed below as they can significantly influence the expected outcome of the Listening Test.
- Size of the room
- Acoustics of the room
- Quality of the recording of the listening input
- Quality of the oral rendering of the listening input (In case it is to be read aloud by the Examiner), in terms of volume, clarity, enunciation, intonation, pace etc.
- Seating arrangement
- Equipment used  

**TEST ADMINISTRATION : FOR THE EXAMINER (TEACHER)**  

- Select the time and date of the assessment.
- Plan the seating arrangement in advance.
- Decide exactly as to how much time is to be reserved for instructions or any possible interaction regarding clarifications, etc and for the actual test.
- Make the announcement giving general instructions directed to the candidates before the recording is played or before the reading of the text.
- Adhere strictly to the time specified for each of the three phases of the Test (Pre-listening, While listening and Post-listening)
- The general instructions given below should be followed while conducting the test.  

**THE LISTENING ASSESSMENT**  

The Listening Assessment comprises of a variety of tasks. These tasks are graded according to the length of the task and the difficulty level. Schools may download the sample tasks and reorganise them into two parts - transcripts and the marking scheme for the examiner and the worksheets for the students on which students will write answers.  

**GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS FOR STUDENTS**  

- You are not allowed to ask questions or interrupt the Examiner at any point.
- You are being tested on your Listening Skills.
- You will hear a set of recordings of the Listening Input or listen to a reading of the Listening Input.
- Each of the recordings will be played twice. In case there is an oral rendering of the Listening Input, it shall take place twice.
- You are required to answer a set of questions based on each of the Listening Inputs.
- The assessment consists of 4 sections.
- You are required to attempt all 4 sections of the assessment.
- Familiarise yourself with the questions on the worksheet. It will help you to answer them later on.
- After you have listened to the input, you will be given specified minutes to answer the questions on your worksheet.
- You may answer the questions on your worksheet while listening.
- Do not interact/comment until you have moved out of the examination room.

## SPEAKING SKILLS  

Speaking skill has acquired a very important place as a communication skill. Like listening skill, a number of sub-skills of speaking need to be consciously developed among students. Some of the sub-skills which can be assessed are as follow:
- Speaking intelligibly using appropriate word stress, sentence stress and intonation patterns.
- Narrating incidents and events, real or imaginary in a logical sequence.
- Presenting oral reports or summaries; making announcements clearly and confidently.
- Expressing and arguing a point of view clearly and effectively.
- Taking active part in group discussions, showing ability to express agreement or disagreement, summarising ideas, eliciting the views of others and presenting own ideas.
- Expressing and responding to personal feelings, opinions and attitudes.
- Participating in spontaneous spoken discourse in familiar social situations.  

**GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS**
1. The total administration time for the speaking assessment is approximately 10-12 min.
2. The speaking assessment will be conducted for two students at a time.
3. There will be a single teacher to function as the examiner.
4. The examiner should be a qualified English teacher.  

**ROLE OF EXAMINER**  

- The examiner should ensure that the room is quiet and has good acoustics.
- The tone, manner and body language of the examiner should be relaxed, encouraging and pleasant. Care should be taken to make the candidates feel at ease.
- Students must not be discouraged from making a fresh start in case they are unable to do so at the first attempt.
- The examiner needs to be flexible, sympathetic and reassuring in her/his demeanour.
- The examiner should also be a proficient user of the language in order to conduct the speaking assessment successfully. For example, the examiner should be skilled in elicitation techniques, so that questions such as... "Explain how"/"Why"..."Tell me what you think of" ... may be asked. Yes/ No questions should generally be avoided.
- Ideally, the examiner should award marks after the candidates have left the examination room. If necessary, notes can be made discreetly to be used later in the scoring, so that it does not make the candidates unduly tense and self-conscious.  

The Speaking Assessment is divided into three stages:  

**Stage 1: General Introduction**
The aim of this introductory task is to put the candidate at ease through some general, open-ended questions, thereby creating a space for the candidate to respond with personal feelings and opinions. Simple warm up questions based on the candidate's name, place of residence, leisure preferences etc are asked.  

**Stage 2: Topic Presentation**
This can take the form of PowerPoint/ OHP presentations, book reviews, debates, speech etc. The focus of this task is to assess a candidate's ability to sustain a straightforward, subjectwise description, mapped out in a fluent and cohesive style.
In this section, the examiner gives each candidate the choice to pick up a Role Card or a Cue Card with a topic written on it. The candidates are given 1 minute to prepare. The examiner should be ready with sheets of paper and a pencil. Candidates may organise their thoughts and ideas. Prior to the day of the speaking assessment, as an assurance to students to prepare for the presentations, the teacher can give a choice of 20 to 30 topics in class and students so that they can prepare presentations based on the topics and organise their ideas. Similar topics can be given in the actual assessment.
Students are not allowed to write full length answers. They may jot down points only in the sheets given by the examiner. Therefore, a pencil and sheet of paper should be provided on the table. Students are not allowed to carry pen, paper or mobile phone to the examination room. After a minute for making notes, each student will be given 2 minutes each to present his/ her ideas. In case a student is unable to speak during/ for the allotted time, the Examiner may ask some rounding off questions.  

**Stage 3: Problem Solving (Pair Interaction)**
The third section of the test is for 3 minutes. Both students are given a verbal or visual stimulus and asked to respond to it. Both the students are given a total of 3 minutes to interact. Both of them will talk together. After 2 minutes the examiner can ask questions based on their discussion.  

The closing is for 1 minute duration only. In case a student has not been able to speak or has been unable to speak owing to nervousness, the dominance of the second candidate or any other factor, then the examiner may use the 1 minute to give a fresh opportunity to that student.
