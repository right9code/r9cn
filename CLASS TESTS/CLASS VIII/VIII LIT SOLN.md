##### Section B: Short Answer Questions (6 Marks)

**Q3. Answer any two:**

**1. What does the poet compare an inch of moss to when viewed through a magnifying glass?**
When viewed through a magnifying glass, the poet compares an inch of moss to a vast forest — a rich, dense, miniature world full of intricate detail. The tiny, unremarkable patch of moss is transformed into something magnificent and expansive, revealing the hidden grandeur within the ordinary.

**2. Why is Bibha Chowdhuri considered a pioneer in the field of science in India?**
Bibha Chowdhuri is considered a pioneer because she was among the first Indian women to pursue physics at a time when women's participation in science was extremely rare. She worked alongside Sir C.V. Raman, conducted groundbreaking research on cosmic rays, and contributed to the discovery of subatomic particles. Despite facing societal barriers and receiving little recognition in her lifetime, she broke gender stereotypes and paved the way for future generations of women in science.

**3. What does the renaming of the star HD 86081 as 'Bibha' signify?**
The renaming of the star HD 86081 as 'Bibha' signifies a long-overdue recognition of Bibha Chowdhuri's extraordinary contributions to science. It symbolises her enduring legacy — just as a star shines in the universe, her work illuminates the world of physics. The naming also serves as an inspiration to scientists, especially women, across India and the world, honouring someone whose brilliance was ignored during her lifetime.

##### Section C: Detailed Questions & Answers (6 Marks)

**Q5. Answer any two:**

**1. How does the poet use visual imagery to show the transformative power of a magnifying glass? Give examples from the text.**
The poet uses vivid visual imagery throughout the poem to show how the magnifying glass transforms the ordinary into the extraordinary. A drop of water, something utterly mundane, is shown to resemble a bustling hive of bees when magnified — suggesting unseen activity and life within the tiniest things. An inch of moss becomes a dense forest, shifting scale so dramatically that the familiar becomes alien and wonderful. These images work together to create a sense of awe, showing that the world is far richer and more complex than our naked eyes perceive. The magnifying glass becomes a metaphor for curiosity and scientific inquiry — both of which reveal the marvellous within the mundane.

**2. What does Bibha Chowdhuri's nomination for a Nobel Prize by Erwin Schrödinger tell us about her scientific abilities and research?**
Erwin Schrödinger was himself a Nobel laureate and one of the greatest physicists of the twentieth century. The fact that he nominated Bibha Chowdhuri for a Nobel Prize speaks volumes about the quality and significance of her research. It tells us that her work on cosmic rays and subatomic particles was cutting-edge and internationally recognized by the greatest scientific minds of her era. That a scientist of Schrödinger's stature endorsed her work confirms that she was not merely a regional or national figure but a researcher of global calibre. Her nomination highlights how her contributions were tragically overlooked by formal awards, even though peers of the highest order acknowledged her genius.

**3. How do the roles of women in ISRO today reflect the progress and pioneering spirit of figures like Bibha Chowdhuri?**
The presence of women scientists at ISRO — participating in and leading landmark missions like Mangalyaan and Chandrayaan — is a direct continuation of the pioneering spirit embodied by Bibha Chowdhuri. She broke barriers at a time when even accessing higher education in science was an enormous challenge for women. Today's women at ISRO inherit that tradition of defying expectations and contributing at the highest levels of science and technology. Their visibility and success are a testament to decades of quiet, determined progress by individuals like Bibha Chowdhuri who persevered despite receiving little credit. In many ways, ISRO's women scientists are the living proof that pioneers like Bibha were not in vain — they lit a path that others could follow.
