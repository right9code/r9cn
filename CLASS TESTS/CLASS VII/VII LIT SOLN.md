##### Section B: Short Answer Questions (6 Marks)

**Q3. Answer any two:**

**1. Why did Rani Abbakka refuse to pay the tribute to the Portuguese envoy?**
Rani Abbakka refused to pay the tribute because she was a proud and fearless ruler who believed in the sovereignty and dignity of her kingdom, Ullal. She saw the Portuguese demand as an act of subjugation and refused to bow to foreign dominance, choosing to fight for the independence and self-respect of her people.

**2. What does the support of the people of Ullal for Rani Abbakka tell us about their mindset?**
The wholehearted support of the people of Ullal tells us that they were patriotic, brave, and deeply loyal to their queen. They valued freedom over submission and were willing to stand and fight alongside their ruler against a powerful foreign force. It reflects their spirit of unity and resistance against oppression.

**3. Why is the line "Prime of your youth given to the nation!!" significant with reference to the soldiers' service?**
The line is significant because it highlights the great personal sacrifice soldiers make. Instead of pursuing personal ambitions or comforts during the best years of their lives, they dedicate their youth — their most energetic and vital years — to safeguarding the nation. The exclamation marks emphasise the poet's deep admiration and emotion for this selfless commitment.

##### Section C: Detailed Questions & Answers (6 Marks)

**Q5. Answer any two:**

**1. How does Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam highlight the constant commitment and vigilance of the soldiers in the poem? Give examples.**
In *My Dear Soldiers*, Dr. Kalam vividly portrays soldiers as ever-watchful guardians who stand guard in extreme conditions — scorching heat, biting cold, and stormy weather — without complaint. He describes how they keep vigil through the night while the rest of the nation sleeps in peace. Lines referencing sleepless nights and harsh terrains emphasise that their duty knows no holiday, no season, and no personal convenience. By comparing their lives to burning candles, Kalam shows that soldiers give of themselves continuously — their very existence is an act of selfless service. This commitment, the poet suggests, is not just physical but emotional and spiritual, driven by love for the motherland.

**2. Why do you think Rani Abbakka's victory against the Portuguese would have inspired other regional rulers to stand up against a foreign power?**
Rani Abbakka's victory was historically significant because it demonstrated that a determined leader, even from a small coastal kingdom, could successfully resist the might of the Portuguese naval empire. Her victory shattered the myth of Portuguese invincibility. For other regional rulers watching, it was proof that unity, local knowledge, tactical bravery, and popular support could overcome superior firepower and resources. It would have given them confidence and hope, encouraging them to form alliances and resist colonial demands rather than capitulate. Her example showed that honour and sovereignty were worth fighting for, no matter the odds.

**3. Why is it important for each one of us to pray for the well-being of our soldiers, according to the poem?**
According to the poem, soldiers sacrifice everything — time, family, comfort, and even their lives — so that ordinary citizens can live in safety and peace. They stand at the borders, often unrecognized and unseen, enduring hardships beyond imagination. The poet urges us to remember that every peaceful moment we enjoy is bought by their sacrifice. Praying for soldiers is therefore not merely a religious act but a moral duty — an acknowledgment of their service and a way of connecting the nation's heart to its protectors. It keeps alive a sense of gratitude and national belonging.
