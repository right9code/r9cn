# Kinds of Phrases  

You have learnt that a group of words that makes complete sense is called a **sentence**.  

**Which of these groups of words are complete sentences?**  
- The boy in the blue coat is the captain of the team.  
- I hit the ball.  
- in the blue coat  
- The children are playing in the backyard.  
- in the backyard  
- In a flash, the frog caught the insect.  
- in a flash  

Did you notice that the groups of words which sound incomplete do not have a verb?  

A **phrase** is group of words that makes complete sense only when it is a part of a sentence. A phrase does not contain a finite verb.

There are three kinds of phrases. They are:  
- noun phrase  
- adjective phrase  
- adverb phrase  

**Noun Phrase**  

Read these sentences.  
- **To win the competition** is my dearest wish.  
- I tried **to get it right**.  
- **Your warning him** was necessary.  

Sometimes, we need a group of words to speak or write about a person or a thing.  

When a group of words does the work of a noun in a sentence, we call it the **noun phrase**.  

**NOTE**:  
It is possible to replace noun phrases with pronouns.  

A noun phrase can act as the subject or the object of a verb. For example:  
- **Standing in the rain** is not a good idea. **(subject)**  
- He dislikes **being surprised**. **(object)**

**Adjective Phrase**  
Read these sentences. Note that the highlighted groups of words qualify the nouns.  
- The boy **with blue eyes** is the most intelligent boy in this class.  
- Dr Kapoor is a doctor **of great fame**.  

When a group of words does the work of an adjective in a sentence, we call it the **adjective phrase**.  

It is sometimes possible to replace an adjective phrase in a sentence with an adjective without altering the meaning of the sentence. For example:  
- He admires men **without fear**.  
    - He admires **fearless** men.
- She was wearing a necklace **with many diamonds**.  
    - She was wearing a **diamond-studded** necklace.

**Adverb Phrase**  
Read these sentences. Note that the highlighted groups of words modify or tell us more about the verbs in each case.  
- The teacher spoke **in a clear voice**. **(manner)**  
- **At that time**, I was in Delhi. **(time)**  
- I searched for the keys **in every possible place**. **(place)**  

When a group of words does the work of an adverb in a sentence, we call it the **adverb phrase**.  

It is sometimes possible to replace the adverb phrase in a sentence with an adverb. For example:  
- I shall support this cause **with all my heart**.  
    - I shall support this cause **whole-heartedly**.
- I went through the documents **again and again**.  
    - I went through the documents **repeatedly**.
