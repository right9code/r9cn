# Diary Writing  

**Read this extract from the 1864 science-fiction novel by Jules Verne (1828-1905) called *the Journey to the Interior of the Earth (also called Journey to the Centre of the Earth)*.**  

***Monday, August 17**: I am trying to recall the peculiar instincts of the monsters of the pre-adamite world, who, coming next in succession after the molluscs, the crustaceans and the fishes, preceded the animals of mammalian race upon the earth. The world then belonged to reptiles. Those monsters held the mastery in the seas of the secondary period. They possessed a perfect organisation, gigantic proportions, prodigious strength. The saurians of our day, the alligators and the crocodiles, are but feeble reproductions of their forefathers of primitive ages.*  
*I shudder as I recall these monsters to my remembrance. No human eye has ever beheld them living. I saw at the Hamburg museum the skeleton of one of these creatures thirty feet in length. Am I then fated—I, a denizen of earth—to be placed face to face with these representatives of long extinct families? No; surely it cannot be! Yet the deep marks of conical teeth upon the iron pick are certainly those of the crocodile.*  
*My eyes are fearfully bent upon the sea. I dread to see one of these monsters darting forth from its submarine caverns. I suppose Professor Liedenbrock was of my opinion too, and even shared my fears, for after having examined the pick, his eyes traversed the ocean from side to side. What a very bad notion that was of his, I thought to myself, to take soundings just here! He has disturbed some monstrous beast in its remote den, and if we are not attacked on our voyage.*  
*I look at our guns and see that they are all right. My uncle notices it, and looks on approvingly.*  
*Already widely disturbed regions on the surface of the water indicate some commotion below. The danger is approaching. We must be on the lookout.*  

What you have just read is a very different form of writing which is written in the first person.  
This form of writing is called **diary writing**. This is yet another form of creative writing and it does have some special features. Let us list them.  
- It is written in the first person and the day and date are carefully recorded.  
- It is not only a chronicle of the events that have taken place on that day and date, but also a spontaneous expression of emotions.  
- Sometimes the diary is given a name. Anne Frank called her diary, 'Kitty'.  
- Usually, a diary is maintained by an individual and is not meant for public viewing. So, one can really be honest to the diary and write without inhibitions. However, in a classroom situation, perhaps one has to be diplomatic.  
- When writing in a diary, you should keep in mind the normal rules of good writing. Be careful about sentence constructions, paragraphs, ornamentations in language, etc.  

**NOTE**:  
**Mind the format!** Every entry must begin with the date and day of the week. Diary entries may also be signed off like letters. You have a wide choice of tenses to use. You may start with the past as you are trying to recall the incidents of the day and end with the present or the past when you are trying to sum up your emotions.  

**Now, attempt writing diary entries for the following situations.**  
1. Suppose your parents have left town and you are alone at home for a week. Write a diary entry for each day of the week describing what you did and how you managed.  
2. Imagine your plane has crashed in the Amazonian rainforest. You are the lone survivor. In the form of a diary, narrate how you spent your days (at least seven) in the wilderness before you were rescued.  
3. Imagine you have been selected to represent the school at some event (it could be a sports meet, a debate or a quiz). Write three diary entries—the first describing how you prepared for the selection process, the second describing how you felt after you were selected and the third describing what happened when you participated in the competition.

**1.**  

Monday, 10 October 20XX  
9:30 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
My parents left for Mumbai early this morning to attend an official seminar, leaving me in charge of the house for an entire week. At first, I felt a surge of excitement thinking about the freedom of having the house to myself. However, as evening approached, the silence in the living room felt quite unusual. I cooked simple noodles for dinner and made sure all the doors were locked securely. It is going to be a true test of my independence, but I am determined to manage everything responsibly!  

Tuesday, 11 October 20XX  
10:00 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
Today was my second day on my own, and it went fairly smoothly. I woke up early on my own alarm, made myself breakfast, and packed my school bag without any reminders. After returning from school, I watered the garden plants and completed my homework before heating up the meal mom had stored in the refrigerator. Managing daily chores alongside studies is definitely harder than it looks, but I am learning to organize my time efficiently.  

Wednesday, 12 October 20XX  
9:45 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
A minor challenge arose this evening when the kitchen tap started leaking after dinner! For a moment, I panicked seeing water pool on the sink counter. Remembering dad's advice, I located the main valve under the sink and turned it off safely. I then called our neighborhood plumber to fix it. Resolving the issue independently gave me a huge boost of confidence. I spoke to mom on the phone later and she was very proud of how I handled it.  

Thursday, 13 October 20XX  
10:15 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
Today was a busy school day, but keeping the house tidy has become second nature now. I swept the rooms in the afternoon and kept my study table neatly organized. To avoid feeling lonely in the evening, I played my acoustic guitar for an hour and read a chapter of my adventure novel. Being alone has given me time for quiet self-reflection, and I am enjoying this newly discovered self-reliance.  

Friday, 14 October 20XX  
9:15 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
With the weekend approaching, I decided to finish all my pending homework tonight so that I could relax tomorrow. I prepared a simple vegetable stir-fry for dinner, which surprisingly turned out quite delicious! My aunt called in the evening to check on me and invited me over for lunch tomorrow, but I politely declined because I want to complete this week entirely on my own strength.  

Saturday, 15 October 20XX  
10:30 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
What a productive Saturday! I spent the morning doing laundry and cleaning out the refrigerator to make sure everything was fresh. In the afternoon, my friend Rohan visited for a couple of hours, and we played board games in the living room. Handling all household responsibilities while keeping the house spotless has given me a great sense of accomplishment.  

Sunday, 16 October 20XX  
9:00 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
My parents finally returned home this evening! They were pleasantly surprised to find the house neat, clean, and well-managed. Mom gave me a warm hug and praised my maturity, while dad gave me an approving pat on the back. This week has been an incredible learning experience. I proved to myself that I can be responsible, self-sufficient, and dependable in any situation.  

**2.**  

Monday, 4 June 20XX  
8:00 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
I am writing this with trembling hands by the dim light of a small campfire. Our light aircraft suffered engine failure during a sudden storm and crashed deep inside the Amazon rainforest. Miraculously, I survived with only minor scrapes, but the plane is destroyed and there is no trace of the pilots. I managed to salvage a pocket knife, a flashlight, and a small water bottle from the wreckage. Fear surrounds me in this dense, dark jungle, but I must stay strong to survive.  

Tuesday, 5 June 20XX  
7:30 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
My second day in the wilderness was spent securing basic necessities. I built a temporary shelter using fallen palm fronds and branches near the crash site so that rescue teams can spot me easily from the air. Drinking water was my biggest concern, but I managed to collect fresh rainwater using large leaves. The endless calls of unseen birds and insects echo through the trees, keeping me awake, but I am holding onto hope.  

Wednesday, 6 June 20XX  
8:15 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
Hunger set in severely today. Remembering survival documentaries, I carefully avoided bright berries and instead searched for wild bananas and edible palm shoots near a nearby stream. Navigating the thick foliage was exhausting, and I had to be constantly vigilant against snakes and insects. I maintained a smoky fire near my shelter throughout the day, hoping the smoke signal would attract searching aircraft.  

Thursday, 7 June 20XX  
7:45 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
A heavy tropical downpour battered the rainforest for hours today, dampening my shelter and putting out my fire. It was a miserable, shivering day, but I managed to protect my notebook and matches inside a waterproof pouch from my backpack. Rebuilding the fire after the rain stopped was tough, but seeing the flames flicker back to life restored my spirits. I will not give up.  

Friday, 8 June 20XX  
8:30 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
Today I heard the distant rumble of a helicopter engine in the afternoon! I rushed to add damp leaves to my fire to create thick white smoke, but the aircraft moved away before spotting me. Though disappointed, hearing search teams gave me immense comfort—it means people are actively looking for me! I constructed a giant 'SOS' sign on a clear patch of ground using light-colored rocks and tree trunks.  

Saturday, 9 June 20XX  
8:00 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
Physical fatigue is taking a heavy toll on my body, but my mind remains resolute. I spent the day reinforcing my shelter and gathering firewood to keep the wild animals at bay during the night. Looking up at the tiny patches of night sky visible through the thick jungle canopy, I prayed dearly for my family. I know rescue is near; I just have to endure a little longer.  

Sunday, 10 June 20XX  
6:00 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
I am writing this from inside a rescue helicopter! Late this afternoon, a search and rescue team spotted my 'SOS' sign and smoke signal while patrolling the sector. A rescue swimmer descended via cable, lifted me to safety, and wrapped me in warm blankets. As the helicopter rose above the green canopy of the Amazon, tears of overwhelming gratitude filled my eyes. I survived seven long days in the wild, and I am finally going home!

Dear Diary,  
I can hardly contain my joy—I have been selected to represent our school at the State Debate Championship! When the teacher announced my name as the official delegate this afternoon, I felt an overwhelming wave of relief and happiness. My classmates cheered warmly, and the teacher commended my strong arguments and clear voice modulation during the trial. Carrying the responsibility of representing my institution is both thrilling and humbling. I am determined to work even harder now to bring home the trophy!  

Wednesday, 17 August 20XX  
9:30 p.m.  

Dear Diary,  
What an unforgettable day! The State Championship took place today at the city auditorium, featuring brilliant speakers from over thirty schools. When my turn came, I stepped up to the podium, took a deep breath, and delivered my speech with utmost conviction and poise. The rebuttal round was intense, but I countered the opposing arguments effectively. To my immense delight, our school won the First Runner-Up trophy! Holding the glittering trophy on stage alongside my mentor teacher was a moment of supreme pride that I will cherish forever.
