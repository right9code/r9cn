# Articles  

You have learnt that there are three articles—**a, an** and **the**.  

**Indefinite Articles**  
The articles a and an are called **indefinite articles** because they do not point to a particular person or thing. Let us look at the uses of the indefinite articles.  

- We use **a** and **an** before countable nouns in the singular number. For example,  
    - a desk
    - an orange  

**NOTE**:  
We do not say a milk or a lemonade because they are uncountable nouns. However, we can say a cup of milk or a glass of lemonade.  

- We use **a** before words that begin with a **consonant sound**. We use an before words that begin with a **vowel sound**. For example,  
    - **a** thirsty crow
    - **a** good friend
    - **an** empty bottle
    - **an** independent state  

- We use **a** before words that begin with **e** and **u** because they have an initial '**y**' sound. For example,  
    - **a** **u**niversity
    - **a** **E**uropean  

- We use **a** before words that begin with o but sound like '**w**'. For example,  
    - **a** **o**ne-storey building  

- We use **an** before words that begin with a silent **h**. For example,  
    - **an** **h**eir
    - **an** **h**onest politician  

- We use **an** before abbreviations, if consonants begin with a vowel sound. For example,  
    - **an** **MA**
    - **an** **MP**  

- We use **a** or **an** before a singular countable noun which is used to represent a class of things. For example,  
    - **A** cow is a useful animal.  

- We use **a** or **an** to indicate one. For example,  
    - Not **a** bird could be heard.  

- We use **a** or **an** to make a common noun of a proper noun. For example,  
    - We have **a** Daniel in our midst!  

- We use **a** or **an** when we speak of a profession. For example,  
    - He is **a** doctor.  

- We use a before words like **dozen, quarter, thousand, few, little** and **lot of**. For example,  
    - Can you give me **a** little sugar?
    - He bought **a** few mangoes.
    - It is a quarter of **a** kilometre to the school.

**Definite Articles**  
The is called the definite article because it points to a particular person or a specific thing. Let us learn the uses of the definite article.  

We use the when we speak of a particular person or thing, or one that has already been referred to. For example,  
- A thirsty crow found a pitcher that contained some water. The clever crow picked up some pebbles in his beak and dropped them one by one into the pitcher.  

We use the when a singular common noun is used to indicate a whole class. For example,  
- The lion is the king of the jungle.  

We use the with superlatives. For example,  
- Sachin is one of the most popular sportsmen in the world.  

We use the with objects that are unique. For example,  
- the sun  
- the moon  

We use the before phrases like first, last, only, next, following and previous. For example,  
- She is sitting in the last row.  

We use the before ordinals. For example,  
- Her examination starts on the third of March.  

We use the when we speak of the population of a nation. For example,  
- The Japanese are warm-hearted people.  

We use the before the names of trains, planes and ships. For example,  
- the Titanic  
- the Golden Temple Express  

We use the before the names of well-known buildings and institutions. For example,  
- the Parliament House  
- the Red Fort  

We use the before the names of newspapers and magazines. For example,  
- the Deccan Herald  
- the Hindu  

We use the with adjectives, when we use them to refer to a whole group of people. For example,  
- We should help the poor. (poor people)  

We use the with the names of rivers, seas, oceans, mountains and deserts. For example,  
- the Ganges  
- the Indian Ocean  
- the Arabian Sea  
- the Sahara Desert  

We use the before points of the compass that are used as geographic areas. For example,  
- the Middle East  
- the West  

We use the before points on the globe. For example,  
- the equator  
- the south pole  

**Omission of Articles**  
We do not use articles—  
- before expressions like last night, last week, last year, next week and next month.  
- before proper nouns. For example,  
  - I am going to Australia.
  - Have you spoken to Hema?
- before names of languages. For example,  
  - They speak German quite well.
- before plural countable nouns that refer to the entire class. For example,  
  - I do not like bananas.
- before material nouns. For example,  
  - Platinum is an expensive metal.
- before abstract nouns when used in a general way. For example,  
  - Honesty is a virtue.
- before the names of games and sports. For example,  
  - Golf is gaining in popularity in India of late.
  - Rakesh loves playing hockey.
- before the names of meals. For example,  
  - I ate breakfast quite late today.
- before expressions such as all day, all night, by air and by sea. For example,  
  - The contingent for the Asian Games will travel by air.  

**NOTE**:  
In certain cases, there is a need to repeat an article to make the meaning clear. Look at these sentences carefully.  
- The Trustee and Secretary arrived.  
- The Trustee and the Secretary arrived.  

The first sentence implies that only one person who was both Trustee and Secretary arrived.  
The second sentence states that two persons arrived—the Trustee and the Secretary.
