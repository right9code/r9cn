# Interjections  

Read these sentences.  
- **Ah!** I damaged the vase.  
- **Hello!** What's the matter?  
- **Hush!** Be quiet.  
- **Hurrah!** We won the tournament.  
- **Oh!** How did that happen?  
- **Alas!** There is little hope of her winning.  

The highlighted words—**Ah!, Hello!, Hush!, Hurrah!, Oh!** and **Alas!**—express sudden feelings or emotions. They have no grammatical relation with any of the other words of the sentence. Such words are called **interjections**.  

An **interjection** is a word or a group of words that expresses some sudden feeling or emotion of the speaker.  

The interjection is placed before a sentence begins. A mark of exclamation (!) follows an interjection.  

Some groups of words or phrases that act as interjections are—  
**Dear me!, Hold on!, Well done!, Good grief!, Good gracious!** and **Good heavens!**.  

Study this list to learn which interjections can be used to express various emotions.  
- **Joy:** Hurrah! Ha ha!  
- **Surprise:** Ha! What! Oh! Good heavens!  
- **Drawing attention:** Listen! Behold! Look! Hello! Ho! Hi!  
- **Making/becoming silent:** Hush! Shh!  
- **Appreciation:** Bravo! Well done!  
- **Contempt:** Pooh! What a shame!  
- **Sorrow:** Alas! Ah! Oh!
