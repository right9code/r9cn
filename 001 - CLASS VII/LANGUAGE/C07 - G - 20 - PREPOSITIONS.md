# Prepositions  

You have learnt that a preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or a pronoun and some other word in a sentence.  

Let us take a look at a few examples to understand this better. The highlighted prepositions in the sentences given below show the relation between the underlined words.  
- The **baby** is **on** the **bed**.  
- **I** am scared **of** **spiders**.  
- The **diver** jumped **into** the **pool**.  

Did you notice that in each of the three sentences, the preposition comes before a noun? In each case, the noun is the object of that preposition.  

You have also learnt that prepositions may be used to express a number of relationships.  

Look at these sentences.  
- School reopened **on** Monday.  
- We were **on** holiday **for** a month **before** that.  
- Getting to school **by** 8 a.m. **on** that first Monday was not easy.  

The highlighted prepositions in these sentences have been used to denote time so they are known as **prepositions of time**.  

Some prepositions that are commonly used to denote time are **before, after, during, from, since, for, within, by, till, at, in** and **on**.  

Now, read these sentences.  
- The students are waiting **in** the assembly hall.  
- A few **among** the younger students are very excited.  
- The prefects are standing **in** two rows **at** the back.  

The highlighted prepositions in these sentences have been used to indicate position so they are called **prepositions of position or place**.  

Some prepositions that are commonly used to denote position are **at, on, in, beside, behind, beyond, above, over, under, below, near, among** and **between**.  

Take a look at these sentences.  
- As soon as the bell rang, the students ran **to** the playground.  
- The teachers asked the students to walk **down** the stairs quietly.  
- The students could not wait to get **out of** the building.  

**NOTE**:  
The words **at, on** and **in** can be used both as prepositions of time and prepositions of position.  

The highlighted prepositions in the above sentences have been used to indicate direction related to movement so they are known as **prepositions of motion**.  

Some prepositions that are commonly used to denote direction or movement are **to, towards, out of, into, at, over, across, up, down, around, along, against, through, from, off** and **for**.  

Prepositions can also be classified as—  
- **simple prepositions** that only consist of one word like most of the ones listed above.  
- **compound or phrase prepositions** that are made up of more than one word. For example, **in place of, away from, in case of, by means of, because of, in front of**.  

**NOTE**:  
The word **at** can also be used as a preposition of motion. For example,  
- The boy threw a stone **at** the dog.  
Phrase prepositions always end with a simple preposition.

Look at these sentences showing the correct use of some prepositions that are often confused.  
- **Beside** means by the side of while **besides** means in addition to.  
    - The cat slept on the bed **beside** the little boy.
    - **Besides** this cat, he has two other pets.
- **Of** may be used to indicate origin, cause, material, etc. However, **off** usually means from, away or not on.  
    - The dress is made **of** silk.
    - The toddler fell **off** the chair.
- **Since** is used to indicate a point of time in the past when an action started while **from** can be used to indicate an action in the past, present or future.  
    - The child has been ill **since** Friday.
    - My son begins school **from** today.
    - He will begin to repay the loan **from** September 2015.
- **Between** is used when speaking of two things or people while **among** refers to more than two people or things.  
    - Meghna is standing **between** Mohana and Srija.
    - The cadets distributed the fruits **among** the children.
- **With** relates to the instrument used to do something while **by** refers to the agent or the doer.  
  - I prefer to write **with** a fountain pen rather than a ballpoint pen.
  - This chart was drawn **by** me **with** water colours.  

**NOTE**:  
The word **from** can indicate source or origin as well as reason and motion.  
- We get heat **from** the sun.  
- The poor man suffers **from** asthma terribly.  
- We walked **from** the market to our house.
