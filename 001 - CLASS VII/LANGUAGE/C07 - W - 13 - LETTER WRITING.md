# Letter Writing  

You have learnt that we write informal letters to friends and relatives and formal letters to principals of school and colleges and to official bodies.  

**Here is a sample of an informal letter.**  

Top Sea View  
47 Bandra Point  
Mumbai 400 007  
26 February 20XX  

Dear Mona  

I have just returned from my holiday in Shimla. I thought I would write to you immediately and tell you all about my trip.  
Although I was most reluctant to be away from home for a week, the trip turned out to be most memorable. It was quite a long train journey from Mumbai to Shimla, but since I slept most of the way, time passed by quickly. Two of my cousins in Shimla were my constant companions. What I liked most during my stay there was the location of my aunt's house where we had put up. Their home was on a slope overlooking a pine forest. Coming from the congested city of Mumbai, breathing the pure fresh air of the hills was an extremely welcome change.  
Another thing that I loved about the place were the small tracks built all over the area because of which we could go on long walks and be surrounded by nature. It was an extremely enjoyable feeling to take an average of three walks a day with my cousins.  
I recommend Shimla as a vacation destination especially for someone who loves nature as much as you do, because of its clean surroundings, its beauty and the friendly people. For city folks like you and me, such a trip is essential to realise that we are missing a lot by living in Mumbai and Kolkata.  
I would be happy to make a trip with you in the near future.  

Yours truly  
Anita  

You have learnt what the parts of an informal letter are. It is very important to remember each of these. In your exams, marks will be allotted for the layout of the letter as well as the content and, of course, the language. Look at the marks that will be allotted to each of these parts in the exam.  

- Layout of the letter:  
    - sender's address: 1½ marks
    - date: ½ mark
    - salutation: ½ mark
    - subscription: ½ mark
    - signature: ½ mark
- Content of the letter: *(in the context of the sample letter)*  
    - what you found most exciting: ½ mark
    - two reasons explaining why you were excited: 2 marks
- Language and style: 5 marks  

**A. Now, attempt these informal letters in your notebook.**  
1. You have got the address of someone in Japan who is looking for a pen friend. Fortunately, he/she can read and write English. Write your first letter to him/her saying that you would like to be his/her pen friend. In your letter, you are expected to give your interests and other relevant details.  
2. Write a letter to your friend in Delhi describing to him/her how two of your school friends and you got together and watched the FIFA World Cup Finals. Tell him/her how much you missed his/her company and the things that you could have done had he/she been present.  
3. A friend from abroad is coming to visit you. Write a short letter giving him/her directions to enable him/her to reach your house.  
4. Write a letter to your friend wishing him for his birthday and explaining why you cannot attend his party.  
5. A friend of yours is a very good actor on stage. You have organised a Harry Potter theme party for some underprivileged children. Write a letter to your friend requesting him/her to impersonate any character from any one of the novels in order to entertain the children. Give him/her other details of the programme which he/she requires.  
6. You have just celebrated Teacher's Day in school. Write a letter to your friend in another city about the things you did on that day to make your teachers feel special.

You have learnt what the parts of a formal letter are. It is very important to remember each of these. Look at the marks that will be allotted to each of these parts in the exam.  

- Layout of the letter:  
  - writer's address and date: 1 mark
  - name and address of the receiver: ½ mark
  - subject and salutation: ½ mark
  - subscription: ½ mark
  - signature: ½ mark
- Content of the letter: *(in the context of the sample letter)*  
  - date of the competition: ½ mark
  - venue and time: ½ mark
  - reason for not attending: 1 mark
- Language and style: 5 marks  

**B. Now, attempt these formal letters in your notebook.**  
1. Write a letter to the local police complaining about a theft that took place the previous night at your neighbour's flat.  
2. You are the Secretary of the Photography Club of your school. You plan to organise an exhibition of some of the activities of your club during the year. Write a letter to the principal of your school inviting him/her to inaugurate the exhibition, stating some of the items that will be displayed.  
3. You are a budding tennis star but there aren't enough facilities for the improvement of your genius. Write a letter to the Secretary of the Lawn Tennis Association of your state bringing to his notice the lack of facilities and suggesting what could be done to encourage young tennis players like you, stating some of the incentives that could be provided.  
4. You have recently purchased a book from a famous bookstore. On going home, you discover you have been given a different book. Write a letter to the bookstore complaining about their services and requesting them to change the book at the earliest.  
5. Write a letter to the municipal authorities of your town objecting to the construction of a supermarket close to your house, giving reasons as to why the construction work should be abandoned.
