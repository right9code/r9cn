# Simple, Compound and Complex Sentences  

You have already learnt that a sentence is a group of words that makes absolute sense.  

Sentences can be categorised into three types, based on their grammatical structure.  
- simple sentence  
- compound sentence  
- complex sentence  

**Simple Sentence**  
Read these sentences.  
- He **lives** in this building.  
- Sammy **likes** sweets.  

Each of these sentences is a clause. We can say so because each has a subject and a verb. And these sentences can also be divided into subject and predicate.  

A sentence having only one clause and therefore, only a single finite verb, is called a **simple sentence**.  

**Compound Sentence**  

Read these sentences.  
- **The rain arrived** and **the peacocks began to dance**.  
- **I was disappointed** but **I kept quiet**.  

The highlighted parts in the above sentences are independent clauses that can stand on their own. These independent clauses have been joined by coordinating conjunctions like **and** and **but**.  

A sentence that contains two independent clauses that are joined by a coordinating conjunction is called a **compound sentence**.  

**NOTE**:  
If a compound sentence is made up of two clauses and if the subject of the second clause is the same as the subject of the first, the second subject may be omitted.  
- Monica reached home and cooked dinner.  
Since both the clauses are of the same rank, we refer to them as coordinate clauses. Each clause can be divided into subject and predicate and has a finite verb.  

**Complex Sentence**  
Now, read these sentences. Note that in each sentence, the underlined clause depends on the highlighted clause to complete its meaning.  
- **This is the girl** *who is my neighbour*.  
- **I left the house** *when my mother arrived*.  

These sentences comprise a main clause and a subordinate clause.  
The subordinate clause in each case is dependent on the main clause to make its meaning clear.  

A sentence having one main clause and at least one subordinate clause is called a **complex sentence**.  

**NOTE**:  
A complex sentence can have many subordinate clauses. But there must be at least one main clause that is absolutely independent and makes complete sense. For example,  
- (As the sun set) we walked towards our tent (which had been pitched near a river) (that was almost overflowing).  
The highlighted part is the main clause. There are three subordinate clauses in this sentence. They have been put within brackets. The main clause, as well as each of the subordinate clauses, has a finite verb.

1. Although Porus was defeated, he never lost courage.  
2. This violin, bought from an antique shop, belonged to a man living in a small town in the hills.

4. Many people live in small huts by the river flowing down from the mountains in the north.  
5. If you hope to succeed, you should put in more effort.  
6. The Town Hall was destroyed by the last earthquake, but the War Memorial was not damaged.  
7. Tired of waiting, we started walking in the direction of the station, hoping to catch the last train to the city.  
8. You must not go to work today as you are unwell.  
9. We were all delighted by his achievements, yet we could not reveal our true feelings.  
10. I must leave now, else I shall miss the train.  
11. The prince wandered through the forests of the region searching for the mythical animal.  
12. Neither the colour nor the design of this dress appeals to me.  
13. Yesterday, I met Rita who is now in Delhi where she lives with her brother who is an architect.  
14. I have always wondered how he manages to do so well.  
15. When I walked through that door, I was feeling nervous.  
16. As soon as the teacher walked into the classroom, the students stopped shouting.  
17. The book was published in the month of May prior to his departure for a country on the west coast of Africa.  
18. I have visited many continents, but I have not toured India.  
19. A great commotion at the station drew my attention.  
20. Neither he nor I was prepared for the sudden turn of events.
