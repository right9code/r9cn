# Punctuation  

1. **Read the sentences. Notice the punctuation marks at the end of each.**  
    - We are going to the zoo.
    - We are going to the zoo!
    - We are going to the zoo?  

The punctuation marks in the three sentences above suggest three different ideas.  

You must have noticed that when we speak, there are times when our voice is raised and sometimes it is lowered. Then, we pause and our pauses are not of the same duration. Some pauses are short, while some are long. Marks of punctuation in writing carry out the same function as pauses and stresses in speech.  

The most commonly used punctuation marks are:  
- full stop (.)  
- comma (.)  
- mark of interrogation (?)  
- mark of exclamation (!)  
- quotation marks or inverted commas (“...)  

**Full Stop**  
The **full stop**, or **period**, represents the longest pause. It is used—  
- to mark the end of an assertive or an imperative sentence. For example,  
    - I will go to the market.  

**Comma**  
The **comma** is used to—  
-  set off a noun of address. For example,  
    - Rahul, come here.
- separate each pair of words of the same class or kind. For example,  
    - Rich and poor, young and old, men and women, took part in the celebrations.
- separate parts of a date from the other words following the date. For example,  
    - On 12 May 2009, we left for Mumbai.
- separate words in a series. For example,  
    - We bought some pencils, pens, erasers and books.
- separate a statement from a question tag. For example,  
    - You didn't meet him, did you?
- show a brief pause that is needed to give clarity to our writing. For example,  
    - It is, as far as I have understood, a novel plan.
- separate expressions like yes, no, oh and well from the rest of the sentence. For example,  
    - Yes, you will be in charge of the team.
    - Well, I think so too.
- separate parts of an address. For example,  
    - 4, Chowringhee Square
- separate the words spoken by the speaker from the rest of the sentence. For example,  
    - She said, 'I want to go home.'
- separate words, phrases or clauses inserted into the body of a sentence. For example,  
    - Health is, after all, of prime concern.  

**Capital Letters**  
You have also learnt about the importance of capital letters in writing. There are rules for using capital letters as well.  
A **capital letter** is used—  
- to begin a sentence. For example,  
    - He is a good person.
- to begin each fresh line of poetry. For example,  
    - Where the voice of the wind calls our wandering feet, Through echoing forest and echoing street, With lutes in our hands ever-singing we roam, All men are our kindred, the world is our home.
- for all proper nouns and adjectives formed from them. For example, May, Friday, Delhi, Japan, British, French and Wendy.  
- to write the pronoun I and the interjections O and Oh.

**Mark of Interrogation**  
**The mark of interrogation** or **the question mark** is used at the end of an interrogative sentence. For example,  
- Would you like to have some coffee?  
- Can I visit you today?  

**Mark of Exclamation**  
The mark of exclamation is placed at the end of an exclamatory sentence or after an interjection. For example,  
- What a relief!  
- Hurrah! We have won the match.

**Inverted Commas or Quotation Marks**  
Quotation marks or inverted commas are used to mark the exact words of a speaker. For example,  
- The lady said, 'I am tired.'  
- Warren Buffet said, 'Someone's sitting in the shade today because someone planted a tree a long time ago.'  

**NOTE**:  
Inverted commas are also sometimes used when we are mentioning the name of a book or an article in a sentence. For example,  
I read 'Alice in Wonderland' as a child.
