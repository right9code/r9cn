# Nouns  

You have learnt that a noun is a word that names a person, place, animal, action, thing, quality or feeling.  

**Kinds of Nouns**  
Nouns are usually classified into four kinds.  
- A **proper noun** denotes a particular person, place or thing. For example, **Italy, Mount Everest, Mohit, Taj Mahal**.  
- A **common noun** denotes a person, place or thing in general. For example, **city, mountain, boy, monument**.  
- A **collective noun** denotes a collection of people, animals or things. For example, **army, herd, bunch**.  
- An **abstract noun** denotes a quality, an action or a state of being. For example, **wisdom, honesty, wickedness**.  

There is a fifth kind of noun which names the material of which things are made.  

A **material noun** denotes the matter or substance of which things are made. For example, **jute, copper, paper**.  

**NOTE**:  
Proper nouns, collective nouns, abstract nouns and material nouns can also be used as common nouns. They are then used in the plural number or have an article placed before them.  
- **Mother Teresa** served the poor of India. (**proper noun**)  
    - She is **the Mother Teresa** of my country. (common noun)
- **Death** is a great leveller. (abstract noun)  
    - The media reported many **deaths**. (common noun)

**Gender**  

Nouns may be in the **masculine**, **feminine**, **neuter** or **common** gender.  

A noun is said to be in the **masculine gender** if it refers to a male. For example, **man, tiger, uncle**.  

A noun is said to be in the **feminine gender** if it refers to a female. For example, **woman, tigress, aunt**.  

A noun is said to be in the **neuter gender** if it refers to things that are neither male nor female. Nouns referring to non-living things are of the neuter gender. For example, **stone, grass, sand**.  

A noun is said to be in the **common gender** if what it refers to can be a male or a female. For example, **student, orphan, baby**.  

**NOTE**:  
Objects associated with strength and power are sometimes regarded as male. For example, Sun, Death. Objects associated with beauty and gentleness are usually regarded as female. For example, **Nature, Earth**.

**Number**  
In English, a noun may either be singular or plural in number.  

When we speak about one person, one animal or one thing, we use the noun in the **singular** form. For example, **boy, tiger, box**.  

When we speak about two or more people, animals or things, we use the noun in the **plural** form. For example, **boys, tigers, boxes**.  

Singular nouns can be changed to plural in various ways. Look at this table and revise the rules that you have studied before.  

| Rule | Singular | Plural |  
|------|----------|--------|  
| By adding -s to the singular form | garden, stone | gardens, stones |  
| By adding -es to nouns ending in -x, -ch, -s, -sh | tax, watch, gas, dish | taxes, watches, gases, dishes |  
| In nouns ending in -y, by changing -y into -i and adding -es | duty, story | duties, stories |  
| In nouns ending in -y but preceded by a vowel, by adding s | day, monkey | days, monkeys |  
| In nouns ending in -o preceded by a consonant, by adding -es | mosquito, volcano, hero, buffalo | mosquitoes, volcanoes, heroes, buffaloes |  
| In nouns ending in -ie, -oo or -eo and in some words ending in -o preceded by a consonant, by adding -s | tie, cuckoo, video, piano | ties, cuckoos, videos, pianos |  
| In nouns ending in -f or -fe, by changing -f or -fe into -v and adding -es | calf, knife | calves, knives |  
| In some nouns ending in -f, by adding -s | gulf, roof, cliff | gulfs, roofs, cliffs |  
| Some nouns do not follow any of the above rules | hoof, dwarf | hoofs/hooves, dwarfs/dwarves |  
| Some nouns have the same singular and plural forms | bison, deer, moose, aircraft | bison, deer, moose, aircraft |

Now, read some more rules.  
- Compound nouns generally form their plurals by adding -s to the main noun. For example,  
    - father-in-law—fathers-in-law
    - step-daughter—stepdaughters  

- Some words are generally used in the singular form. For example, **poetry, information, furniture, scenery, mathematics, politics, advice, machinery, alms, innings**.  

-  Nouns that describe things having two similar parts are considered to be plural nouns. For example, **jeans, glasses, tongs, socks, trousers, scissors**.  

- Some nouns denoting a specific quantity or amount are considered to be singular. For example, **three dozen apples, two hundred miles**.  

- Some words in the English language have been borrowed from Latin. Some nouns of Latin origin that end in **-us** form their plurals by adding **-es**. For example, **circuses, campuses, viruses**.
