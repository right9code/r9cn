# Report Writing  

In this chapter, we are going to discuss how to write a newspaper report covering an event. A **report** is a brief, factual piece of writing which has no scope for expressing personal opinions or emotions.  

**Look at this sample of a newspaper report.**  

**Pulse Polio Campaign**  
by Sudeep Das  
Kolkata, 14 May, 2013  
A pulse polio campaign was organised in a village of Burdwan District, called Govardhanpur, in West Bengal. It was organised by the World Health Organisation (WHO), a wing of the United Nations. There were roughly four hundred children present at the camp.  
A number of tents had been set up in the camp. In each tent, there was a team of two doctors, and a nurse aided by two people who were handling the supplies. The  
helpers were volunteers from among the villagers. There were only two hundred and fifty polio units in stock, however, and the additional units were brought in by a helicopter in the evening.  
The villagers have appreciated this campaign and are very happy to see the genuine concern of WHO. One of them, Mr Ashish Banerjee, commented, 'This campaign is a boon for poor villagers like us. Polio is a crippling disease and our children are lucky that they are being immunised against it.'  

**Look at these guidelines to be kept in mind for writing a report.**  
- A newspaper report must be factual.  
- It should be short and precise (not more than 100–150 words).  
- All the relevant points should be narrated in a proper sequence.  
- Nothing should be left to guesswork.  
- An account by an eyewitness should be included.  
- Start a report directly with the topic. It does not provide any scope for an elaborate introduction.  
- Write in indirect speech.  
- Use the passive voice.  
- Eliminate the use of the personal pronoun, 'I'. If necessary, the phrase 'the reporter' should be used.  
- No personal opinion should be given unless specifically asked for.  
- The language used in a report should be matter of fact and not ornamented with similes or metaphors.  
- Always keep in mind the reader you are writing for.  

**Format of a report**  
- A short heading in the centre. The heading should not be so creative or far fetched that it has no connection with the report. It is best to convert the question into the heading.  
- The heading may be followed by the name of the person who is writing the report.  
- The report must begin with the name of the place where the event has occurred followed by the date of reporting.  
- Word limit: 100–150 words.  
- A report is a short essay and is normally marked out of 20. The mark division is 15 for the content and 5 for the format. The format includes the heading, designation of the reporter, name of place, date and the use of paragraphs.  
- In a report writing question, some guidelines will always be given. It is imperative to touch upon all the points and to maintain the sequence of events. Any digression from these rules may result in loss of marks for the format.  

**A. Now, write newspaper reports of about 150 words for each of the following topics or situations. You can use the guidelines provided.**  
1. **Topic: The devastating cyclone**  
- Guidelines: name of the cyclone...date...time...areas affected...people marooned...eyewitness account...immediate and subsequent rescue work...evacuation...chances of widespread disease and sickness...response of the whole world...sympathy and help for the suffering  
2. **Topic: The inter-school soccer match held in your school**  
- Guidelines: date...time...venue...schools participating...objective...a festive mood...rules of the game...a brief description...winning team...prize distribution...comments from a spectator  
3. **Situation: A famous actor has come to your school to conduct auditions for his forthcoming production. On popular demand he has even addressed the students of the school-leaving batch**  
- Guidelines: date...venue...number of days that he visited...number of students who turned up for the auditions...arrangements for the same...brief description of the auditions...results...a brief on the actor's address... impact on students  
4. **Situation: As the head boy/girl, prepare a report on the pet show recently organised by the nursery department of your school.**  
- Guidelines: date...venue...time...aim and objective... number of pets...breed, species...classes that visited... schools invited...brief description of the programme... security arrangements...impact on students  
5. **Situation**: As an eyewitness, prepare a report on the one-act theatre festival held among the schools in your city, which was organised by the British Council, in collaboration with the Central School of Speech and Drama, London.  
- Guidelines: date...venue...time...organisers...schools participating... sponsors...rules...judges...brief description of the programme ...results...audience response  

**B. Write reports of about 150 words on these topics or situations.**  
1. You are Akshit, secretary of the Science Club in your school. There was a science quiz held in your school recently and many other schools participated. Write are report on the event.  
2. You are a newspaper reporter and have just witnessed a hit-and-run road accident. Take the account of the eyewitnesses and the people involved and write a report on the accident.  
3. There has been a sensational kidnapping of the CEO of an MNC. Find out details about it from the family, the police and the eyewitnesses, and write a report.  
4. There has been a Diwali celebration in your society recently. Write a report on it for your society magazine.  
5. You are Ragini/Rahul, a reporter with a newspaper. Write a report on the increasing crime rate in your city and the probable causes for it.  
6. You are Pratibha, a reporter with The Indian Times. Write a report on the polio camps held in the city and their importance in India's progress as a healthy nation.

**Devastating Cyclone Strikes Coastal Belt**  
by Special Correspondent  
Balasore, 12 October, 20XX  

A furious severe cyclonic storm named 'Cyclone Phailin' slammed into the coastal region of Balasore yesterday evening at around 7:30 p.m. Torrential rains accompanied by violent winds exceeding 160 km/h uprooted hundreds of trees, destroyed electric poles, and flattened mud houses. Low-lying coastal villages were submerged, leaving over ten thousand people marooned without food or clean water.  

An eyewitness, Mr. Ramesh Jena, reported, 'The roar of the wind was terrifying, and floodwaters rose to our waist within minutes.' Emergency rescue operations were immediately launched by the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and local authorities. Armed forces helicopters were deployed for air-dropping food packets and evacuating stranded villagers to safer relief shelters. Health officials have warned of the high risk of widespread waterborne diseases. Sympathy and humanitarian aid have poured in from governments and aid organisations worldwide to support relief efforts.
