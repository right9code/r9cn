# Direct and Indirect Speech  

Read these sets of sentences.  
- She said, 'I am going to Paris.'  
- She **told** me that she was going to Paris.  

In the first sentence, the exact words of the speaker are reproduced.  
The entire speech is put within inverted commas.  

When a sentence reports the exact words of the speaker, it is said to be in **direct speech**.  

In the second sentence, the words of the speaker are reproduced in the past tense and are not within inverted commas.  

In **indirect speech**, we report what the speaker has said without quoting his or her exact words.  

In a sentence containing direct speech, there is a verb of speaking. In the above example, the verb is **said**. The verb said is called the **reporting verb**.  
Usually, a comma or a colon separates the words of the speaker from the reporting verb. The first word in the quoted section will always begin with a capital letter.  

**NOTE**:  
Two of the most commonly used reporting verbs are said and told.  
When we need to mention to whom something is said, we use told.  
When we either do not know or do not want to mention to whom something has been said, we use **said**. For example,  
- Rahul **said**, 'I am very tired.'  
- Rahul **said** that he was very tired.  
- Rahul **said** to me, 'I am very tired.'  
- Rahul **told** me that he was very tired.  
Some other words that can be used in place of said are **add, complain, promise, remind, remark** and **point out**.  

A sentence in direct speech can thus be divided into two—  
- the main part of the sentence that contains the reporting verb  
- the quoted section which is the object of the reporting verb  

When we are asked to change direct speech into indirect speech, we have to make certain changes.  

Now, the changes will depend on various things—  
- The manner in which the speaker utters the words—is he merely making a statement or is he giving an order or is he perhaps making a request?  
- Is the speaker asking a question?  
- Is the speaker happy, sad, excited or furious?  
- We will also notice that when we are changing from direct to indirect speech, the main change takes place in that part of the sentence that contains what has been said.  

Now, take a look at the following sentences in direct speech. Also notice the changes made while transforming them into indirect speech.  
- The teacher **said** to the naughty child, 'Be quiet.'  
- The teacher **ordered** the child to be quiet.  
The reporting verb **ordered** has replaced **said**. We have taken into **account** what the speaker is saying is saying and how he is saying it.  
- Our coach **said** to us, 'Drink fluids during practice sessions.'  
- Our coach **advised** us to drink fluids during practice sessions.  
The word **advised** is appropriate because that is what the coach is doing.  
- My mother **said**, 'My car has been giving me a lot of trouble.'  
- My mother **complained** that her car had been giving her a lot of trouble.  

The word **complained** reflects the mood of the speaker.  

So, when we change from direct speech to indirect speech, we do need to take into account not only what is being said but how it is being said. The mood and sentiment of the speaker will play a crucial role.  

Here are some rules that should be followed while changing sentences from direct into indirect, or reported speech.  
- If the reporting verb is in the present tense or the future tense, the tense of the verb in the reported speech remains unchanged. For example,  
    - The lady **says**, 'I am a good cook.'
    - The lady **says** that she is a good cook.
    - The trainer **will say**, 'The children are ready for the tournament.'
    - The trainer **will say** that the children are ready for the tournament.  

Study this table to understand how tenses change when we convert direct speech into indirect speech.  

| Direct Speech                                                    | Indirect Speech                                                       |  
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |  
| **simple present** – The man said, 'I like flying kites.'        | **simple past** – The man said that he liked flying kites.            |  
| **present continuous** – Rita said, 'My aunt is cooking dinner.' | **past continuous** – Rita said that her aunt was cooking dinner.     |  
| **present perfect** – Rana said, 'I have taken a bath.'          | **past perfect** – Rana said that he had taken a bath.                |  
| **simple past** – He said, 'I bought a watch for my mother.'     | **past perfect** – He said that he had bought a watch for his mother. |  
| **past perfect** – Ronny said, 'Rahul had done this before.'     | **past perfect** – Ronny said that Rahul had done that before.        |  

Here are some exceptions to the above rules.  
- If the reported speech expresses a universal truth or an established/habitual fact, its tenses remain unchanged.  
    - The teacher told the class, 'The sun is a star.'
    - The teacher told the class that the sun is a star.  

**NOTE**:  

Modal verb forms also change sometimes.  
- must becomes had to  
- will/shall become would  
- shall sometimes changes to should  
- can becomes could  

- If the reported speech describes a state of affairs which still exists when the speech is reported, its tenses remain unchanged.  
    - The President said, 'The people of this country are very intelligent.'
    - The President said that the people of this country are very intelligent.  

- If the reported speech contains a time clause, its tense remains unchanged.  
    - She said, 'When I was an actor, I lived in Mumbai.'
    - She said that when she was an actor, she lived in Mumbai.  

- Sometimes the gender of the person speaking remains unclear. In that case, we usually use the masculine gender.  
    - My teacher said to me, 'I will speak to you on Monday.' 
    - My teacher told me that he would speak to me on Monday.

**Statements: From Direct Speech to Indirect Speech**  
Pronouns and possessive adjectives in the direct speech are changed, wherever necessary. For example,  
- Pronouns and possessive adjectives of the first person are changed according to the subject of the reporting verb.  
    - He said, '**I** want to give my sister a present.'
    - He said that **he** wanted to give his sister a present.  

- Pronouns and possessive adjectives of the second person are changed according to the object of the reporting verb. For example,  
    - Mahi said to **me**, 'You have not done your work.'
    - Mahi told **me** that I had not done my work.  

- Pronouns and possessive adjectives in the third person remain unchanged. For example,  
    - She said, '**He** wants to give his sister a present.'
    - She said that **he** wanted to give his sister a present.  

- If the person addressed reports the speech himself/herself, then the second person is changed into first person. For example,  
    - She said to me, '**You** appear tired.'
    - She told me that **I** appeared tired.  

- The nominative of address in direct speech becomes the person spoken to in indirect speech. For example,  
    -  She said, '**Sam**, I am going to sleep.'
    - She told **Sam** that she was going to sleep.  

- Certain words expressing nearness are changed into words expressing distance.  
    - now → then
    - this → that
    - these → those
    - here → there
    - thus → so
    - ago → before
    - today → that day
    - tomorrow → the next day
    - yesterday → the day before; the previous day
    - last night → the night before; the previous night
    - last week/month/year, etc. → the week/month/year, etc. before; the previous week/month/year, etc.

**Questions: From Direct Speech to Indirect Speech**  
Now, let us see how we should change questions from direct to indirect speech.  
- The reporting verb must be changed to **asked, enquired**.  
- Words like **demanded, wondered, wanted to know**, may also be used.  
- The question mark is removed. A statement is framed.  
- No conjunction is used if the question begins with words like **who, whom, whose, which, what, why**. For example,  
    - Rita said to me, 'What do you wish to buy?' 
    - Rita asked me what I wished to buy.
- When the question is not introduced by a question word, the reporting verb is followed by whether or if. For example,  
    - I asked him, 'Will you be late?'
    - I asked him whether he would be late.
- Do remember that we need to keep in mind the tone of the question and the sentiment of the speaker when we change from direct to indirect speech. For example,  
    - My friend said, 'Would you like to go for a movie?' I said, 'No.'
    - My friend asked me whether I would like to go for a movie. I replied that I would not.

**Imperatives: From Direct Speech to Indirect Speech**  
Let us now take a look at the rules for changing commands, requests, advice, etc. into indirect speech.  

- The reporting verb will have to be changed to **ordered, requested,** or **advised**. Other words that may be used are **asked, invited, encouraged, reminded, urged, warned, recommended,** etc. The reporting verb must be chosen according to who said what and in what way. The verb that is to be reported is changed into an infinitive. For example,  
    - I **said** to my friend, 'Could you **make** me a cup of coffee?' 
        - I **requested** my friend **to make** me a cup of coffee.
    - The teacher **said** to the pupils, 'You must **complete** the work right now.'
        - The teacher **ordered** the pupils **to complete** their work right then.  

**Exclamatory Sentences: From Direct Speech to Indirect Speech**  
Let us now take a look at the rules for changing exclamations and wishes into indirect speech.  
- The reporting verb is changed into **wished, prayed, cried, exclaimed,** etc.  
- Words such as **hurrah, alas, oh,** etc., are omitted. However, the feeling expressed must be conveyed appropriately by using expressions such as exclaimed **with joy**.  
- The sign of exclamation is omitted. For example,  
- 'Alas! The elephant is dead,' he said.  
    - He exclaimed sadly that the elephant was dead.

**From Indirect Speech to Direct Speech**  

Let us now study how to change sentences in indirect speech into direct speech.  
- We know that indirect speech is a means of reporting what somebody has said without using the actual words of the speaker.  
- The best way to change from indirect to direct speech is to mentally identify the two parts of the sentence.  
- The main part will contain a reporting verb like, **said, tell, suggested, requested,** etc.  
- The other part will contain what is being reported. This part will be the quoted section in direct speech.  
- The part that will become the quote, once we have made the transformation, is the object of the reporting verb.  
    - He told me that my letter had reached him the previous week. 
        - He said to me, 'Your letter reached me last week.'
    - The teacher asked the boy whether he had read that book.
        - The teacher said to the boy, 'Have you read this book?'
    - His mother urged him to pursue a career in medicine.
        - His mother said to him, 'You should pursue a career in medicine.'
    - He exclaimed that it was a violent storm.
        - 'What a violent storm it is!' said he.
