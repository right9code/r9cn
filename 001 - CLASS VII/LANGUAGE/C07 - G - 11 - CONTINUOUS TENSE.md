# Continuous Tense  

Verbs in the continuous tense will denote actions that have not been completed.  

You have learnt how the form of the verb changes in the continuous tense, depending on the time. Take a look at the chart given below and revise how the verb forms change.  

| Tense | First person | Second person | Third person |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| Present continuous | I am watching TV. <br> We are watching TV. | You are watching TV. | He is watching TV. <br> They are watching TV. |  
| Past continuous | I was watching TV. <br> We were watching TV. | You were watching TV. | He was watching TV. <br> They were watching TV. |  
| Future continuous | I shall be watching TV. <br> We shall be watching TV. | You will be watching TV. | He will be watching TV. <br> They will be watching TV. |  

**Present Continuous Tense**  
The present continuous tense is used—  
- to describe an action that is happening now, at the time of speaking. For example,  
      - He **is reading** a book now.
      - A nightingale **is singing** in the woods.
- to describe an action that has been going on over a period of time, including the present, but may not be happening at the time of speaking. For example,  
    - I **am learning** Arabic.
    - Swami **is attending** classes regularly.
- for an action that is going to take place in the near future. For example,  
    - I **am going** to listen to music.
    - Wait, we **are reaching** in a few minutes.  

**NOTE**:  
The present continuous tense draws attention to the continuity of action. It is usually associated with adverbs or phrases of present time such as **now**, **at this moment**, etc.  

**Past Continuous Tense**  
The past continuous tense is used—  
- to describe an action going on at a point of time or over a period of time in the past. For example,  
    - We **were strolling** all evening.
    - I **was sleeping**.
- to describe an action that was in progress when another action took place. For example,  
  - They **were working** in the laboratory when I arrived.
  - When I met him, he **was baking** cakes.  

In these two sentences, we see that the verbs in the past continuous tense are describing longer actions, whereas the verbs in the simple past are describing shorter actions.  

**Future Continuous Tense**  
The future continuous is used—  
- to denote an action that will be in progress at some point in the future. For example,  
    - Grandfather **will be eating** at that time.
- to denote future events that are planned. For example,  
    - The Cabinet **will be announcing** the next plan.
