# Synthesis of Sentences  

Based on the structure of sentences, we can divide them into three kinds. Look at these examples of the three types of sentences.  
- I know him well. **(simple)**  
- I want to work, but I do not want to work here. **(compound)**  
- I like the dress that you bought. **(complex)**  

Did you know that it is possible to join two or more simple sentences and form compound or complex sentences?  

**Synthesis** means combining two or more simple sentences to form compound or complex sentences.  

You have learnt that compound sentences are made up of two independent clauses that are joined with the help of conjunctions. We can join two or more simple sentences to form compound sentences. Let us learn the ways of combining simple sentences into compound sentences.  

- We can combine two statements having similar ideas using **and, both...and, as well as** and **not only...but also**. For example,  
    - Pawan got up. He went to the market.
        - Pawan got up and went to the market.
    - We learn English. We learn Sanskrit. 
        - We learn English as well as Sanskrit.
We can combine two statements that express contrasting ideas using **but, yet, whereas** and **nevertheless**. For example,  
- He is rich. He is unhappy.  
    - He is rich, but he is unhappy.
- He is sick. He is cheerful.  
    - He is sick, yet he is cheerful.  

- We can combine two statements that express a choice between two alternatives using **or, either...or, neither...nor** and **otherwise**. For example,  
    - Are you at home? Are you outside? 
        - Are you at home or outside?
    - We can make pizza. We can make pasta.
        - We can either make pizza or pasta.
    - He is not a cheat. He is not a liar. 
        - He is neither a cheat nor a liar.  

- We can combine two statements that express cause and effect using **for, so** and **therefore**. For example,  
    - He was sick. He went home early. 
        - He was sick **so** he went home early.
    - I was late. I missed the bus.
        - I was late **therefore** I missed the bus.

You have learnt that complex sentences are made up of one main clause and one or more subordinate clauses. We can join two or more simple sentences using noun clauses, adjective clauses or adverb clauses and form complex sentences.  

We can join two or more simple sentences using a noun clause. For example,  
- He is a talented artist. Everybody knows this.  
    - Everybody knows that he is a talented artist.
- We lost the match. It was the consequence of his carelessness.  
    - Because of his carelessness, we lost the match.  

- We can join two or more simple sentences with an adjective clause using relative pronouns like **who, which** or **that** and adverbs like **where, when** and **why**. For example,  
    - I met a girl. She works at the emporium.
        - I met a girl who works at the emporium.
    - This is the house. The theft was committed here.
        - This is the house where the theft was committed.  

- We can join two or more simple sentences with an adverb clause using conjunctions like **when, as, that, since, though, although, whether, whereas, unless** and **if**. For example,  
    -  He worked hard. His objective was to win a scholarship.
        - He worked hard that he might win a scholarship.
    - He began late. He finished first.
        - Although he began late, he finished first.

# Answers – Synthesis of Sentences

1. Is this true or false?  
2. He was angry, but he kept quiet.  
3. He was tired, yet he kept working.

5. He lost not only his suitcase but also his money.  
6. Will you watch a movie or read a book?

8. He is unwell, so he cannot attend the meeting.  
9. It rained heavily, but there was no water-logging.  
10. She was very kind to us, so we did not mind at all.  
11. He read the book and remembered every detail.

13. His father is neither a teacher nor an artist.  
14. He was a good student, so his parents took pride in him.  
15. She will go to school either by car or by bus.  
16. There is no light in their house, so our neighbours must be asleep.
