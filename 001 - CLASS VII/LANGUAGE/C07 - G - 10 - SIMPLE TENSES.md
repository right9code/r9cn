# Simple Tenses  

You have learnt that in a sentence, the form of the verb indicates the time when the action is taking place. Therefore, we say that there are three tenses of a verb—**the present** (the time now), **the past** (the time then) and **the future** (the time to come). These tenses are further subdivided. Let us now look at the simple tenses—**simple present, simple past and simple future**.  

This chart gives us an idea about the verb forms of these three tenses.  

| Tense | First person | Second person | Third person |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| Simple present | I drive cars. <br> We drive cars. | You drive cars. | He/She drives cars. <br> They drive cars. |  
| Simple past | I drove cars. <br> We drove cars. | You drove cars. | He/She drove cars. <br> They drove cars. |  
| Simple future | I shall drive cars. <br> We shall drive cars. | You will drive cars. | He/She will drive cars. <br> They will drive cars. |  

**Simple Present Tense**  
We use the simple present tense—  
- to say something that is always or generally true. For example,  
    - The earth **is** round.
    - The sun **sets** in the west.
- to describe something that has been happening for a long period of time and is still continuing. For example,  
    - We **live** in Delhi.
    - He **is** a teacher.
- to refer to something that happens regularly or to something that somebody does habitually. For example,  
    - A dog **barks**.
    - He **travels** a lot.  

**Simple Past Tense**  
We use the simple past tense—  
- to refer to an action that has already been completed in the past. For example,  
    - It **rained** yesterday.
    - She **exercised** last evening.
- to indicate a habitual action in the past. For example,  
    - My father always **played** golf on Sundays.
    - As a young man, I **trekked** a lot.  

**NOTE**:  
Adverbs and phrases like **yesterday**, **a week ago**, **four months ago**, etc. are usually added to sentences that describe past action.  

**Simple Future Tense**  
We use the simple future tense—  
- in order to say what we think or know will happen in the future. For example,  
    - I **shall swim** tomorrow.
    - Suman **will visit** next week.
- in order to state what will happen or will be done as a matter of habit or course. For example,  
  - During the lunar eclipse, the river **will be** turbulent.

# Answers – Simple Tenses  


**A. Fill in the blanks according to the instructions.**  

1. As a child, I **swam** regularly, but now I **exercise** daily.  
2. I **read** the news with interest and **resolved** to learn more about the elections.  
3. Horrible accidents **take** place because of fire-crackers.  
4. He **extended** his full support to the team and **promised** to do so in the future.  
5. I **shall cook** for my colleagues next Monday.  
6. They **will enjoy** themselves at the summer camp.  
7. We are very sorry and **understand** how you **feel**.  
8. Hari **will** not **do** anything without his father's permission.  
9. **Will** you **find** time to go through the draft? We **shall submit** it to the council tomorrow.  
10. He **studies** at night, **works** during the day and **sleeps** for four hours in the afternoon.  
11. I usually **get** up early in the morning, but tomorrow I **shall sleep** till nine in the morning.  
12. He **drinks** only a glass of milk every morning. However, from tomorrow, he **will eat** an apple.
