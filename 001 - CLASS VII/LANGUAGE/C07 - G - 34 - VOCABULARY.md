# Vocabulary  

**Words Easily Confused**  

**Read these sentences to understand the difference in meaning between these words that are often confused with each other.**  

- **adoptive**: Mr and Mrs Chaki are the **adoptive** parents of Rakesh.  
- **adopted**: Rakesh was **adopted** at birth by Mr and Mrs Chaki.  
- **adverse**: The flight took off despite **adverse** weather conditions.  
- **averse**: Mahatma Gandhi was **averse** to all forms of violence.  
- **advice**: You should listen to the **advice** of your teachers.  
- **advise**: I **advise** you to practise these maths problems.  
- **allusion**: I saw a movie recently that had a clear **allusion** to the story of the great Indian epic, The Mahabharata.  
- **illusion**: It looked as though the magician was sawing the woman in half, but it was actually just a clever **illusion**.  
- **altar**: The Aztecs used to offer terrible sacrifices on their **altars**.  
- **alter**: I cannot wear this dress unless I **alter** it, it is too short for me.  
- **award**: The children who receive **awards** for bravery from the President are truly admirable.  
- **reward**: Nita has lost her pet dog and is offering a huge **reward** to anyone who can find him.  
- **canvas**: The artist destroyed the **canvas** because he was not happy with the painting he had made.  
- **canvass**: Politicians have to **canvass** for many votes before the elections.  
- **defuse**: It takes a lot of knowledge and courage to **defuse** a bomb.  
- **diffuse**: The rich smell of chocolate began to **diffuse** through the room as the cake was baked in the oven.  
- **draft**: Each class had to submit a **draft** of their script to the judges who would select the best play for the inter-school competition.  
- **draught**: There was a cold **draught** coming through a crack in the window all night, and in the morning I had caught a cold.  
- **imply**: The speaker in the debate attempted to **imply** that the opposition was lying.  
- **infer**: We could **infer** that our class teacher was annoyed by the cool tone of her voice.  
- **incidence**: There has been an increasing **incidence** of crimes in that part of town over the last three months.  
- **incidents**: The minister's speech was disrupted by three separate **incidents** in which the members of the Opposition started shouting.  
- **incite**: The naughty girl tried to **incite** her classmates to be rowdy, but they refused to listen to her.  
- **insight**: I read a fascinating book that gave me an **insight** into life in my grandparents' time.  
- **insure**: You should **insure** your car against theft with an insurance policy.  
- **ensure**: Including air bags in cars will **ensure** that you are protected if your car crashes.  
- **precede**: The paragraph you are looking for is in the **preceding** chapter of that book.  
- **proceed**: After you solve all the sums in the section, you can **proceed** to the next chapter.  
- **regretful**: Ronita was **regretful** when her parents explained the consequences of her misdeeds to her.  
- **regrettable**: It is **regrettable** that Aishwarya does not learn from her mistakes.

**Idiomatic Expressions**  

An **idiomatic expression** is a group of words which when taken together imply a meaning that is different from the dictionary definitions of the individual words.
