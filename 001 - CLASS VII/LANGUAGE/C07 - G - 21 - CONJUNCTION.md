# Conjunction  

Read the following sentences.  
- I was late **but** I still managed to finish the work.  
- Gita bought a pen **and** I bought a book.  
- I will call you **when** I reach home.  

The highlighted words are **conjunctions**.  

A **conjunction** is a word used to join words, groups of words or sentences.  

There are two types of conjunctions.  
- coordinating conjunctions  
- subordinating conjunctions  

**Coordinating Conjunctions**  

Read these sentences.  
- I went to the market **and** bought a pair of trousers.  
- He, **as well as** his father, has left for Delhi.  
- I was tired, **still** I went to the party.  
- It rained heavily, **but** there was no water-logging.  
- The man is very rich, **yet** he is unhappy.  
- **Either** visit your aunt **or** call her up right now.  
- **Neither** is he a loyal person **nor** is he truthful.  
- He will do well in life **for** he is focussed.  

These highlighted conjunctions help to join two statements.  

A conjunction that joins two independent statements or two statements of equal importance is called a **coordinating conjunction**.  

Most commonly used coordinating conjunctions are **and, but, for, or, either...or, neither...nor, yet,** and **still**. **Either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also, both...and, no less...than** are used in pairs.  

**NOTE**:  
We should choose a conjunction carefully, else the meaning will change.  
Read these sentences.  
- Kate gave me textbooks. Kate gave me compact discs.  
- Kate gave me textbooks **and** compact discs.  
- Kate gave me **both** textbooks **and** compact discs.  
The meaning here is that not only did Kate give textbooks but also provided the compact discs.  
By using the conjunction **and**, the sense is not conveyed completely.  
- I was tired. I went to the party.  
- I was tired **and** I went to the party.  
- I was tired **so** I went to the party.  
- I was tired, **yet** I went to the party.  
By using the coordinating conjunctions **so** and **and**, the idea has not been conveyed correctly.

**Subordinating Conjunctions**  

Read these sentences.  
- I returned **after** the sun had set.  
- Do not leave the premises **till** the guards arrive.  
- The fire brigade reached the spot **as soon as** they could.  
- I will inform you **whenever** I meet him.  

The highlighted parts of these sentences do not convey complete sense. It is only when these parts are added to the rest of the sentence with conjunctions (the underlined words) that they make sense.  

A conjunction that joins two statements, one of which is dependent on the other, is called a **subordinating conjunction**.  

Subordinating conjunctions express contrast between ideas or show the cause and effect relationship between ideas. For example,  
- I left for the studio **when** the rain stopped. (cause and effect)  
- I dislike him **because** he does not keep his word. (cause and effect)  
- I could not visit you **as** I had fallen ill. (cause and effect)  
- The train was **so** crowded **that** we could not reach our seats. (cause and effect)  
- She has not lost her will **though** she has suffered much. (contrast)  
- **Although** he is poor, he is an honest man. (contrast)

These conjunctions convey their full sense only when they are used in pairs.  

**either...or**  
- You must **either** apologise **or** accept punishment.  
- She will **either** visit you today **or** call you tomorrow.  

**neither...nor**  
- **Neither** he **nor** I can do this.  
- **Neither** Rekha **nor** Rama was present.  

**both...and**  
- The athlete was **both** fined **and** expelled.  
- The trainer was **both** happy **and** relieved to see him.  

**so...that**  
- He worked **so** intently **that** he completed the task before time.  
- He ran **so** fast **that** he was out of breath.  

**no sooner...than**  
- **No sooner** had I entered the room **than** I heard a loud explosion.  
- **No sooner** had the child entered the room **than** he started crying.  

**not only...but also**  
- The hailstorm **not only** destroyed the standing crops **but also** damaged the fruit trees.  
- My teacher **not only** helped me **but also** promised to lend me some books.  

**as...so**  
- **As** the morning is, **so** is the day.  
- **As** you sow, **so** shall you reap.
