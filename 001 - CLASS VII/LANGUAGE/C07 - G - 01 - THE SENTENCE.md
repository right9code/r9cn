You have learnt that a group of words that makes complete sense is called a **sentence**. A sentence always begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark. A sentence must always contain a verb.  

**Subject and Predicate**  

Every sentence consists of two parts—the subject and the predicate.  

The part which names the person or thing we are talking about is called the subject of the sentence. The subject usually comes at the beginning of the sentence.  

The part which tells us more about the person or thing, or what they do, forms the predicate of a sentence. The verb is always a part of the predicate.

**NOTE**: The subject, however, need not always come at the beginning of the sentence. For example,  
- Down dropped **the sail**.  
- How beautiful **the rose** is!  
- Did **you** see my watch?  

Sometimes, the subject of a sentence can consist of more than one word. For example,  
- **Luv and his friend** are jogging in the park.  
- **Books, magazines and newspapers** are sold here.  

In some sentences, the subject may consist of several words, but there may be one word which is more important than the other words. This is called the **subject-word**. The subject-word is always a noun, or a group of words that does the work of a noun. The subject is usually qualified by an adjective or a group of words that does the work of an adjective. This adjective or adjective phrase is called the **attribute**. For example,  
- **The warm *weather*** will not last.  
- **The *house* in the woods** belongs to my uncle.  

**NOTE**:  
The predicate, like the subject, may also consist of several words. When the predicate consists of a single word, it is always a verb. For example,  
- Dogs **bark**.  
- Bees **sting**.

**Kinds of Sentences**  
A group of words that makes complete sense is called a sentence. Sentences are of four kinds. Let us learn about them.  

Read these sentences.  
- Wolves live in forests.  
- The sun rises in the east.  

These sentences state or declare something. They are called **assertive sentences** and they end with a full stop (.)  

Assertive sentences can be **affirmative** (positive) or **negative**. For example,  
- It's warm today. (**affirmative**)  
- It's not warm today. (**negative**)  

**NOTE**:  
Sentences that contain not or no are called negative sentences.  

Now, read these sentences.  
- Do you like tea?  
- Who will do the work?  

These sentences ask questions and end with a question mark (?). Such sentences are called **interrogative sentences**.  

Now, read these sentences.  
- Leave the room immediately. (**order**)  
- Please lend me some money. (**request**)  
- Study hard if you want to succeed. (**advice**)  

**These sentences give orders, commands or advice, or make requests and are called imperative sentences.**  

Look at these sentences.  
- Alas! We lost the match.  
- How beautiful is the rose!  

These sentences express strong or sudden feelings and are called **exclamatory sentences**. They end in an exclamation mark (!).
