# Adjectives  

You have learnt that an **adjective** is a word used to qualify or describe a noun or a pronoun.  

**Can you say which of the highlighted words below have been used as adjectives? Try to identify the different types of adjectives used.**  
Kolkata is **my** city. Kolkata is a **big** city. It stands on the **left** bank of the **beautiful** river Hooghly. **This** river is a tributary of the Ganges. The river usually has **enough** water throughout the year. Kolkata has two railway stations. There are **many** monuments in the city. **Each** monument is quite stunning. **Which** monument is my favourite? The Victoria Memorial, of course!  

All the highlighted words above have been used to describe or qualify nouns. Let us revise what you have learnt about the different kinds of adjectives.  

**Adjectives of quality** describe the kind or quality of a person or thing. For example, **big** city, **beautiful** river.  

**Adjectives of quantity** indicate how much of a thing is meant. For example, **little** rain, **enough** water.  

**Adjectives of number** indicate how many of a thing is meant. For example, **two** stations, **six** fairies.  

**NOTE**:  
Adjectives of number are of three kinds.  
- Definite numeral adjectives state the exact number of people or things. They are of two kinds.  
    - **Cardinals** tell us the number of people or things. For example, **thirty** years, **twelve** days.
    - **Ordinals** tell us the order of people or things in a series. For example, **second** example, **sixth** month of the year.
- **Indefinite numeral adjectives** do not indicate an exact number. For example, **few** children, **many** people.  
- **Distributive numeral adjectives** refer to each one of a number. For example, **every** child, **each** person.  

**Demonstrative** adjectives indicate which person or thing is meant. For example, **this** river, **those** girls.  

**NOTE**:  
The words this, that, these and those can be used both as adjectives and as pronouns. It is important to distinguish between the two functions. The easiest way to determine the part of speech is to examine whether the word qualifies a noun or points to a noun. **Demonstrative adjectives** will always be followed by a noun. For example,  
- This is a charming old city. (pronoun, pointing to the noun, city)  
- This book is very interesting. (adjective, qualifying the noun, book)  

**Interrogative adjectives** are used with nouns to ask questions. For example which city, whose pen.  

**Possessive adjectives** show belonging or possession. For example, my city, their house.  

**NOTE**:  
It is important to understand the difference between possessive adjectives and possessive pronouns. Look at these sentences.  
- **My** aunt wrote me a letter. This aunt of mine lives in Canada.  
- Jasbeer spoke to a friend of his about this problem. **His** friend has promised to help.
