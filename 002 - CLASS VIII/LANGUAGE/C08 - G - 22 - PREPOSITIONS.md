# Prepositions  

By now, you have understood that appropriate use of prepositions plays a vital role in speaking and writing English correctly.  

Prepositions are usually very small words. Yet, they are very important as they establish a relationship between the different parts of a sentence.  

Take a look at these sentences.  

- Put the box **on** the table.  
    - (relation between two nouns—**box** and **table**)
- South Africa is famous **for** its diamonds.  
    - (relation between the adjective **famous** and the noun **diamonds**)
- The king rules **over** a vast kingdom.  
    - (relation between a verb and a noun—**rules** and **kingdom**)  

These examples show that a **preposition** is a word that is placed before a noun (or a pronoun) to show the relation that the noun or the pronoun has with some other word in the sentence.  

The noun or pronoun that is used with a preposition is called its **object**.  
A preposition can have more than one object.

Let us take a look at the use of three frequently used prepositions in the English language—**at, on, in**.  

All three can be used as **prepositions of time** as well as **prepositions of place**.  

**Prepositions of Time**  
The prepositions **at, on** and **in** can be used to indicate time. Read these sentences and notice how the highlighted words have been used in these sentences.  
- Please meet me **at** 8 o'clock. (indicates **exact point of time**)  
- This year, Durga Puja falls **in** September. (indicates **a longer period of time**)  
- You can expect me **in** a few days. (means **at the end of**)  
- The city museum is closed **on** Sundays. (indicates **day**)  
- John's birthday is **on** 20 October. (indicates **date**)  

Time can be indicated by a few other prepositions as well. For example,  
- **after** his departure  
- **within** a month  
- arrived **before** him  
- **till** the evening  
- **by** three o'clock  
- **since** Sunday  
- **from** 1 January  
- **for** ten years  

**Prepositions of Place**  
Look at these sentences where the prepositions **at, on** and **in** have been used as prepositions of place.  
- The cupboard is **in** my room. (to show an **enclosed space**)  
- Mr Sinha lives in Canada. (to show **position within land areas**)  
- I placed my bag **on** the table. (to show **position on a horizontal** or **vertical surface**)  
- I lived **at** 56, MG Road. (to show **specific place** or **position**)  
- Someone is **at** the doorstep. (to show **specific place** or **position**)  

Place can be indicated by a few other prepositions as well. For example,  
- stood **behind** the door  
- **above** the shelf  
- stand **by** my side  
- lies **near** the port  
- came **from** New York  
- lay **under** the table  
- **within** the classroom  
- lies **below** the surface  
- fell **into** a large crater  
- stood **beside** me  

**NOTE**:  
- To indicate cause, reason or purpose, we use prepositions such as of and for. Thus we say, died **of** cancer, **for** our good and took medicine **for** fever.  
- To indicate source or origin we use prepositions such as from. Thus, we say from gratitude and comes from practice.

Several words are used both as prepositions and as adverbs. Note that when a word governs a noun or a pronoun, it is a preposition. It is an adverb if it modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb. For example,  
- The wheel of the car **came off**. (**adverb**)  
- The convict jumped off **the van**. (**preposition**)  
- The caravan **moved on**. (**adverb**)  
- The cup is **on the table**. (**preposition**)  
- Has the teacher **come in**? (**adverb**)  
- The principal kept his diary in **the cupboard**. (**preposition**)

**NOTE**:  
- Prepositions that consist of one word are called **simple prepositions**. For example, **beyond, out, outside, over, past, but, by, despite, down, to, toward, during, except, between, under, failing, following, for, from, in, inside, into, before, among**.  
- Prepositions that consist of more that two words are called **compound or phrase prepositions**. For example, **according to, owing to, close to, as far as, by means of, as well as, in spite of, in place of, in front of**.  

There is no rule to decide which preposition comes after which verb or adjective. Particular words are followed by particular prepositions, although there are several prepositions that have the same meaning. Do remember, the list given in the previous exercise is merely a list of examples. There are many more combinations with prepositions and it is always important to use the correct prepositions in such expressions. Look at these examples.  
- The captain died **of** fever. (**correct**)  
    - He died **from** fever. (**incorrect**)
- He was sick **of** fever. (**incorrect**)  
    - He was sick **with** fever. (**correct**)  

**NOTE**:  
- When two or more words require the same preposition, only one preposition is retained.  
    - Mahatma Gandhi was **loved** and **respected** by all.
- Prepositions are not used before certain words and expressions. Thus, we say—  
    - We go to school every day. ('We go to school on every day' is incorrect.)
    - I will leave next month. ('I will leave in next month' is incorrect.)
