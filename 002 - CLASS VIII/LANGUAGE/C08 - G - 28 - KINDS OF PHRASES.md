# Kinds of Phrases  

You have learnt that a **phrase** is a group of words that makes complete sense only when it is a part of a sentence. A phrase does not contain a finite verb.  

There are three kinds of phrases. They are—  
- adjective phrase  
- adverb phrase  
- noun phrase  

**Adjective Phrase**  
When a group of words does the work of an adjective in a sentence, we call it an **adjective phrase**.  
An adjective phrase modifies a noun and comes immediately after the noun or pronoun it modifies. For example,  
- Kirti is the student **with the highest marks**.  
- The finalist wore a crown **made of diamonds**.  

An adjective can usually replace an adjective phrase and vice-versa. For example,  
- We were surprised to find a **deserted** village.  
- We were surprised to find a village **without inhabitants**.

**Adverb Phrase**  
When a group of words does the work of an adverb in a sentence, we call it an **adverb phrase**.  

Study the table with examples of the different kinds of adverb phrases.  

| Adverb phrase | Example sentence                           |  
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------ |  
| manner        | The fireman opened the door with a kick.   |  
| time          | Mother cleans the house in the morning.    |  
| place         | Mansi lives in a foreign country.          |  
| degree        | The dog eats its food very quickly.        |  
| frequency     | You should go to the dentist twice a year. |  
| purpose       | We play golf for fun.                      |  

It is sometimes possible to replace the adverb phrase in a sentence with an adverb and vice-versa. For example,  
- I shall support this cause **with all my heart**.  
    - I shall support this cause **whole-heartedly**.
- I went through the documents **again and again**.  
    - I went through the documents **repeatedly**.

**Noun Phrase**  
When a group of words does the work of a noun in a sentence, we call it the **noun phrase**. For example,  
- **The chairman of the board of governors** attended the convention.  
- The actor had appeared in **innumerable television comedies**.  
- Leela gave me **a piece of insightful and thought-provoking advice**.  
- **The girl in the red skirt** is my sister.  

Sometimes, a noun phrase can begin with a gerund or a participle. For example,  
- **To be a Bharatnatyam** dancer is her wish. **(participle)**  
- **Watching cricket on TV** is the nation's pastime. **(gerund)**  

In a sentence, a noun usually acts as the subject or the object of a verb. Likewise, a noun phrase too acts as the subject or the object of the verb in a sentence. For example,  
- I like **painting portraits.** **(object)**  
- **Studying music** is a wonderful experience. **(subject)**
