# Letter Writing—I  

Letters are an important means of communication, both for official and personal purposes. We write letters to describe an event, to provide information, to make a request or turn it down. Through letters, we convey emotions or share ideas or events. Letters are of two types—**formal** and **informal**.  

Informal letters are those that are written for unofficial purposes, while formal letters are written to institutions or officials. When writing any letter, you must keep the format in mind. You have learnt what the parts of an informal and formal letter are. Let us revise these formats along with the marks that will be allotted to each of these parts in the exam—  

| **Layout of an informal letter**<br> | **Layout of a formal letter**              |  
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |  
| sender's address: 1 mark             | writer's address, date: 1 mark             |  
| date: \(1 / 2\) mark                 | name & address of receiver: \(1 / 2\) mark |  
| salutation: \(1 / 2\) mark           | subject & salutation: \(1 / 2\) mark       |  
| subscription: \(1 / 2\) mark         | subscription: \(1 / 2\) mark               |  
| signature: \(1 / 2\) mark            | signature: \(1 / 2\) mark                  |  

**NOTE**:  
There will usually be two marks allotted to the content of the letter, where you will be marked on whether you have covered all the points mentioned in the question. Another five marks are for language and style, in other words, the substance of the letter.  

**Points to remember**  
- Do not use abbreviations in the letter.  
- Write the month. If June, write June and not 6.  
- Do not address your friend as 'Dear Friend'.  
- Never write 'Respected Father'.  
- Opening lines like 'How are you? I hope this letter finds you in the pink of health' are old-fashioned and should not be used.  
- The first word of the subscription must begin with a capital letter. For example, **Yours lovingly**.  
- The writer of an informal letter must use only his/her first name to sign, whereas in a formal letter if the signature is not legible, the full name must be printed below.  
- Be careful about the spacing in the body of the letter. The paragraphs must all be in line. The placement of the heading and subscription too should correspond.  

**NOTE**:  
Do not mix the formats. If you select the classical or traditional format (the indented style), continue writing it. Do not write the heading using the classical format and the left-aligned format for the subscription and the signature. The same goes for indentation. If the heading is indented, then the subscription must be indented too.  

**A. Now, write informal letters on these topics.**  
1. Your friend wishes to join your school's drama club. Write to him telling him what is expected of him in this club and that you would be willing to recommend him to the teacher in charge of the club.  
2. You celebrated your birthday recently by going to an orphanage. Write a letter to your friend describing how you celebrated your birthday there and how this event has changed the course of your life.  
3. A friend of yours has just won an All-India Essay Writing Competition. Write a letter congratulating him/her and indicate what you liked best about the essay.  
4. You are in a boarding school. Write a letter to your mother explaining to her your poor performance in your last exam.  
5. You have been selected for the bravery award for children for the year 2010. Write a letter to your friend expressing your feelings of joy and excitement and give him a brief account of the brave act for which you are going to be awarded.  
6. Write a letter to your cousin thanking her for the information that she has sent you for a project you are working on.  
7. You want to go on an educational excursion from school. Write a letter to your father requesting him for money for the same.  

**B. Now, write formal letters on these topics.**  
1. Write a letter to your principal telling him about the 'ragging' that is going on in your school. Suggest ways in which this can be stopped.  
2. You, along with your classmates, have been a victim of food poisoning. Write a letter to the manager of the school catering service, complaining about the poor quality of food and services at the cafeteria that has resulted in these cases of food poisoning.  
3. Write a letter to a famous personality asking him to inaugurate the new school auditorium, giving him details of the venue, place and time.  
4. Write a letter to the director of a television channel complaining about the quality of the programmes. Suggest ways to improve the quality and presentation of the programmes.  
5. Imagine that you bought a radio from a well-known shop, and that it broke down soon after you purchased it. You took it back to be repaired and it worked satisfactorily for a few days before breaking down again. On your next visit to the shop, the assistant was impatient and rude and said that he could not help you. Write a letter to the manager of the shop complaining about the product and the unhelpful shop assistant.  
6. You have lost your purse at a shopping mall. Write a letter to the manager of the mall requesting him to help you.  
7. While on a holiday recently, you and your family spent a week at a hotel. However, the poor facilities and the rude behaviour of the hotel staff made your stay very unpleasant. Write a letter of complaint to the manager of the hotel, expressing your feelings of annoyance, and suggesting what could be done to improve the services provided by the hotel.
