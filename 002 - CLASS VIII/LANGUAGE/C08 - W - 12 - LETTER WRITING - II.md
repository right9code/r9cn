# Letter Writing—II  

**Letters to Newspapers**  
Letters to newspapers usually involve issues that pertain to more than one person, that is, to society in general or a large group of people. The tone of such a letter is important. It has to be persuasive, but at the same time, there is no scope for rudeness. The language has to be formal.  
- These letters should always be addressed to **The Editor** and they usually end with **Yours truly** or **Yours faithfully**.  
- The form of salutation is **Sir**.  
- The letter must address the concerned issue directly without beating about the bush. There should be a suggestion as to how the problem can be tackled or resolved.  

**Read this sample of a letter to the editor of a well-known newspaper protesting against thoughtless honking.**  

6 Gurusaday Road

The Telegraph

Sir  

Noise pollution is on the rise in our city. The drivers of public vehicles do not think twice before honking. Even the private vehicles follow suit. Lack of civic sense and awareness on the part of the drivers and gross negligence of the traffic police are responsible for this menace.  

Schools on the main roads, senior citizens and patients in hospitals and nursing homes are the silent sufferers. This noise pollution even has an adverse effect on the breeding and migration of animals and birds.  

The students of a few leading schools have taken the initiative to curb noise pollution. However, the brazen attitude of certain drivers is a major hindrance to them.  

The concerned authorities must rise to the occasion and alleviate this problem at the earliest so as to provide a better environment to all concerned. Perhaps a steep fine for such thoughtlessness will help redress this issue.  

Thanking you.  

Yours truly  
Rohit Agarwal

**Email**  
Today, the email is fast replacing the art of letter writing. It saves time and reaches faster. Like letters, emails can be both personal and formal. Contrary to a letter, an email is rather brief and the format is also different.  

The email has come into vogue due to the advent of the computer. In an email, the salutation, subscription and signature are all to the left. In formal emails, the address of the person to whom the mail is being sent is written in the address box.  

Every email, whether personal or formal, should have a subject which is to be written in the line following the address box.  

**NOTE**:  
A personal e-mail is more or less similar to a personal letter. Once again the salutation, subscription and signature are to the left. The address of the writer is not necessary and the e-mail has a subject which follows the address box.
