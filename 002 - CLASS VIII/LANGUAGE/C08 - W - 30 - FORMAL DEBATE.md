# Formal Debate  

A **debate** is a persuasive speech. The objective is to influence or change someone's mind or way of thinking about a topic. It is a serious discussion or an argument about a subject on which people have different views or beliefs. The speaker speaks either for the motion or against the motion.  

You should organise your debate along the following lines.  
- Reveal your stand in a dramatic or interesting manner in the introduction.  
- Your arguments should be addressed to the Chairperson, who is chairing the debate.  
- Present effective arguments; provide statistical data, concrete facts, and incidents to support your arguments.  
- Anticipate the arguments that may be presented by your opponent speaker and develop methods to analyse them, in order to rebut your opponent's arguments.  
- In the last paragraph, clarify your stand in an effective manner once again.  

**NOTE**:  
You should understand that how you say something and how you physically present yourself is just as important as what you say. By understanding the dynamics involved in effective persuasive speaking, you will improve your overall confidence in communicating. You must speak articulately and eloquently.  

**Read this example of a debate. The speech has been made for the motion.**  

**Should Corporal Punishment Be Banned?**  
Respected Principal, respected teachers and dear friends, this, as you know is a very current topic and we are all concerned, especially after the media has highlighted certain facts and figures. In short, it has given us an opportunity to examine our notions of corporal punishment.  

I wish to speak for the motion because today the saying—'spare the rod and spoil the child' is too outmoded. Everything changes with time and so should these obsolete sayings.  

Everyone present here would agree that any form of corporal punishment is violent and humiliating. Parents and teachers have, since centuries, used punishment to discipline or reform children. Some people believe that corporal punishment is an effective deterrent in the classroom, and is essential to discipline students, as they are the future of the country.  

Nobody can or will deny the importance of discipline, but there should be alternative ways of enforcing discipline. Beating and spanking little children will hardly help them become remorseful, whereas talking to them or counselling them might just be a better idea.  

A very naughty boy who is always ready with a prank, say by throwing 'stink bombs' behind teachers, may be given a chart paper and could be asked to express his thoughts on paper. Perhaps, a psychiatrist could tell us why he does what he does, and help him. After all, a child is a child and always needs attention.

## Exercise  

1. **Impact of Television on the Lives of Teenagers (Against the Motion / Negative Impact)**  

Respected Chairperson, esteemed judges, worthy opponents, and members of the audience. Today, I stand before you to speak on the topic 'Impact of Television on the Lives of Teenagers', and I strongly argue that excessive television viewing has a detrimental impact on the lives of modern adolescents.  

In an age where health and academic focus are paramount, television acts as a major source of distraction. Studies show that teenagers spend an average of three to four hours daily glued to television screens. This sedentary lifestyle directly contributes to childhood obesity, poor posture, and severe eye strain. Furthermore, the relentless exposure to violent shows, sensationalized news, and unrealistically idealized lifestyle representations induces anxiety, lowers self-esteem, and promotes aggressive behavior.  

My opponents might argue that educational channels provide knowledge. However, passive consumption of media can never replace active learning, critical reading, and physical exercise. Teenagers lose valuable hours that could otherwise be spent engaging in sports, developing hobbies, or enjoying real-world social interactions with family and peers.  

Therefore, Mr. Chairperson, I firmly maintain that unmonitored television viewing severely hampers the physical, mental, and social growth of teenagers. It is time we encourage our youth to step away from the screen and embrace real life. Thank you.  

2. **Impact of the Internet on Young Children (For the Motion / Positive Impact)**  

Respected Chairperson, honourable jury, and my dear friends. I stand before you today to speak for the motion on the topic 'Impact of the Internet on Young Children', affirming that the internet, when used responsibly, is an indispensable tool for empowering young minds.  

We are living in a digital era where information is the gateway to growth. The internet provides children with instant access to an unparalleled ocean of knowledge, educational platforms, interactive learning tools, and virtual science museums. It fosters independent inquiry, curiosity, and problem-solving skills from an early age. Online educational games and coding tutorials nurture logical thinking and creativity in ways traditional textbooks simply cannot match.  

Opponents often cite screen addiction and unsafe content as drawbacks. But let us not blame the medium for poor supervision! With modern parental controls, cyber-safety education, and guided usage, the internet becomes a safe, rich ecosystem of learning. During global crises such as lockdowns, it was the internet that ensured uninterrupted education for millions of children worldwide.  

In conclusion, Mr. Chairperson, the internet is not a threat; it is a catalyst for cognitive development and global awareness. Embracing digital literacy today prepares our children for the challenges of tomorrow. Thank you.  

3. **Should We Explore Life on Other Planets? (For the Motion)**  

Respected Chairperson, distinguished guests, and respected opponents. I rise today to speak enthusiastically for the motion: 'Should We Explore Life on Other Planets?'  

Since the dawn of human civilization, curiosity and the drive to explore the unknown have been the engine of human progress. Space exploration and the search for extraterrestrial life are not mere luxuries; they represent the natural progression of scientific discovery. Searching for life beyond Earth helps us answer fundamental questions about our origins, the nature of the universe, and whether humanity is alone in the cosmos.  

Furthermore, space exploration drives technological innovations that directly benefit life on Earth. Advanced satellite communications, solar energy cells, water purification systems, and cutting-edge medical imagery were all by-products of space research programs! Exploring outer space also prepares humanity for long-term survival by identifying habitable worlds and potential resource reserves.  

My worthy opponents may argue that we should solve Earth's problems first. However, history proves that scientific advancement is not a zero-sum game; the technology developed for space exploration provides solutions for climate monitoring and resource management right here on Earth.  

Mr. Chairperson, to stop looking up at the stars would be to stunt human potential. We must continue to explore the cosmos with courage and vision. Thank you.  

4. **Should Testing of Life-Saving Drugs on Animals Be Banned? (Against the Motion / Opposing the Ban)**  

Respected Chairperson, esteemed judges, and worthy opponents. I stand today to speak against the motion 'Should Testing of Life-Saving Drugs on Animals Be Banned?', advocating for the continued, highly regulated use of animal testing in biomedical research.  

While everyone in this hall respects animal welfare, we must confront the biological reality: human bodies are immensely complex, interconnected systems. Computer simulations and cell cultures, though useful preliminary tools, cannot replicate how a complex drug interacts with an entire living cardiovascular, immune, or nervous system. Virtually every major medical breakthrough of the past century—including polio vaccines, insulin for diabetes, antibiotics, and life-saving cancer therapies—relied on animal testing prior to human clinical trials.  

Banning animal testing entirely would force scientists to test unproven compounds directly on human subjects, posing catastrophic ethical and health risks. Modern scientific protocols already strictly enforce the '3Rs': Replacement, Reduction, and Refinement, ensuring that animals are used only when no alternative exists and with maximum pain-relief measures.  

Mr. Chairperson, until science develops complete biological replacements, responsible animal testing remains an unfortunate but vital necessity to save millions of human lives from devastating diseases. Thank you.
