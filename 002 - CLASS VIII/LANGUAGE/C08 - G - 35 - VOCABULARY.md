# Vocabulary  

**Same Word Used as Different Parts of Speech**  
Here are some sentences using the same words in different parts of speech—  
- This small table will **break** under the weight of that trunk. (**verb**)  
    - The children have a **break** between classes at school. (**noun**)
- He drank two cans of fruit juice. (**noun**)  
    - You can do it if you try hard. (**verb**)
- He **braked** the car suddenly. (**verb**)  
    - She tried to slam the **brakes**, but failed. (**noun**)
- He doesn't want more money; he's **content** with what he has. (**adjective**)  
    - You are on a holiday—you can lie in the sun to your heart's **content**. (**noun**)
- Why did you **desert** us when we needed you the most? (**verb**)  
    - Life in a **desert** isn't easy. (**noun**)
- **Each** of the men received a reward (**pronoun**).  
    - I bought these mangoes for three rupees **each**. (**pronoun**)
    - **Each** boy was given a suitcase to carry (**adjective**).
    - I was away seven days and it rained **each** day. (**adjective**)
- A rabbit is usually **safe** in its burrow. (**adjective**)  
    - There is a small **safe** hidden behind the picture on that wall. (**noun**)
- We had a **sail** up the coast in his yacht (**noun**).  
    - Windmills have four different **sails**. (**noun**)
    - Have you ever **sailed** through the Mediterranean? (**verb**)
- There has been a **drop** in temperature. (**noun**)  
    - I'll just **drop** a note to my friend while I'm waiting. (**verb**)
    - The picture is hanging **on** the wall. **(preposition)**
    - They heaped insults **on** him. **(preposition)**
    - They moved **on**. **(adverb)**
    - Is the party **on** tonight? **(adjective)**
- He played a nasty trick **on** me. **(verb)**  
    - At the start **of** today's play, England scored an easy goal. **(noun)**
- I hurt myself, but it's only a **scratch**. **(noun)**  
    - How did you **scratch** your leg? **(verb)**
- It was nearly dawn and the **light** was getting stronger. **(noun)**  
    - It was a **light** meal, but quite filling. **(adjective)**
    - When she smiles, it **lights** up her entire face. **(verb)**
- I think you need to **pepper** the soup. (verb)  
    - The soup has too much **pepper** in it. (noun)
- Tennis players usually have strong **forearms**. **(noun)**  
    - He knew he would be questioned, so he was **forearmed**. **(verb)**
- I always got good grades at school. **(noun)**  
    - The teacher **graded** the papers carefully. **(verb)**
- They laid flowers on the **grave**. **(noun)**  
    - There are some **grave** decisions to be made. **(adjective)**
- I'll hide the children's presents behind the book shelf. **(verb)**  
    - Shoes are usually made of animal **hide**. **(noun)**
- A cup of tea will soon **perk** you up. **(verb)**  
    - One of the **perks** in this job is that the company gives you a flat. **(noun)**

**Words Easily Confused**  
- **ambiguous** (not very clear): The prisoner's statement is rather **ambiguous** in places.  
- **ambivalent** (having a mixed feeling): After what he has done, I was a little **ambivalent** as to how I should behave.  
- **appraise** (decide the quality or value): It was difficult to **appraise** her suitability for the job.  
- **apprise** (inform): Have they **apprised** you of the changes in their circumstances?  
- **censor** (examine and remove anything that is considered offensive): His latest novel was **censored** by the government.  
- **censure** (officially criticise): The members of the committee were **censured** for having accepted a bribe.  
- **definite** (clearly; fixed): She said that she would give me a **definite** answer by tomorrow.  
- **definitive** (final; decisive): He thinks that he has written the **definitive** book on Mother Teresa.  
- **decent** (fairly good): She is being very **decent** about the whole affair.  
- **dissent** (disagree): I'm afraid this is where I **dissent** from the general opinion.  
- **discreet** (cautious; wise): He is very **discreet** and will never let the secret out.  
- **discrete** (separate and distinct): There are four **discrete** groups within the class.  
- **notable** (important): There were several **notable** people at the meeting.  
- **noticeable** (easy to notice): There is a slight stain on the table cloth, but it's hardly **noticeable**.  
- **official** (approved of or done by someone in authority): You have to get **official** permission to build a house.  
- **officious** (too eager to boss people): His mother-in-law is so **officious** that he does not let her visit his house.  
- **statue** (an image made in stone or metal): She stood as still as a **statue**.  
- **stature** (the degree to which someone is admired): As a scientist, he is a man of international **stature**.  
- **statute** (a law that has been passed by a parliament or council): You are required by **statute** to do this.  
- **vociferous** (having loud and strongly expressed opinions): She was **vociferous** in her criticism of him.  
- **voracious** (eating or wanting large quantities of food): The appetite of most children seems **voracious**.
