# Synthesis of Sentences  

**Synthesis** is the process by which a number of simple sentences are combined to form a new sentence which may be simple, compound or complex.  

**Combining Simple Sentences to Form One Simple Sentence**  
Two or more simple sentences can be combined to form another simple sentence—  
- by using a participle. For example,  
    - Girish completed his homework. He went to play.
    - **Having** completed his homework, Girish went to play.
- by using an infinitive. For example,  
    - I shall go to my old school. I will meet my teachers.
    - I shall go to my old school **to meet** my teachers.
- by using a noun or a phrase in apposition. For example,  
    - Nagasaki was once a heap of rubble. It is now a bustling city. 
    - Nagasaki, **once a heap of ruins**, is now a bustling city.
- by using prepositions **for/by + gerund + noun**. For example,  
    - He received the bravery award. He saved his brother from drowning. 
        - He received the bravery award **for saving his brother** from drowning.
    - He sells clothes. He earns his living. 
        - He earns his living **by selling clothes**.
- by using adverbs. For example,  
    - I reached the spot. I reached in an instant.
    - I reached the spot **instantly**.
- by using **too + adjective/adverb + infinitive**. For example,  
    - The box is very heavy. I can't carry it.
    - The box is **too heavy** for me to carry.  

**NOTE**:  
The present participle is used when the simple sentences have the same subject.  

**NOTE**:  
A phrase in apposition describes the noun before it. The word or phrase in apposition is marked off from the thing to which it refers by a pair of commas.

**Combining Simple Sentences to Form a Compound Sentence**  
Two or more simple sentences can be combined to form a single compound sentence by using coordinating conjunctions like **and, not only...but also, as well as, but, yet, nevertheless, however, or, so, for, either...or, neither...nor, otherwise, therefore**. For example,  
- He is a teacher. He is an athlete.  
    - He is **not only** a teacher but also an athlete.
- It is snowing. The ceremony will be held on time.  
    - It is snowing **but** the ceremony will be held on time.
- He is not a fool. He is not a cheat.  
    - He is neither a fool **nor** a cheat.
- He did not return the library book. He was fined.  
    - He did not return the library book, **therefore**, he was fined.

**Combining Simple Sentences to Form a Complex Sentence**  
You have learnt that a complex sentence contains one principal clause and one or more subordinate clause. Two or more simple sentences can be combined to form a complex sentence by changing one simple sentence into the main clause and converting the others into subordinate clauses. This can be done—  
- by using a noun clause. For example,  
    - He will make an excellent teacher. I believe it.
    - I believe **that he will make an excellent teacher.**
    - You are the captain of your team. Always remember this.
    - Always remember **that you are the captain of your team.**
- by using an adjective clause. For example,  
    - The girl lives in Delhi. She has just joined the college.
        - The girl **who has just joined the college** lives in Delhi.
    - Bring me the bag. It is on the top rack.
        - Bring me the bag **that is on the top rack.**
- by using an adverb clause. For example,  
    - The boys won the match. They rejoiced.
        - The boys rejoiced **when they won the match.**
    - I went to the market. I needed some oranges.
        - I went to the market **because I needed some oranges.**
