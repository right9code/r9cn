# Nouns  

A noun is the name of a person, a place, an animal, a feeling or an idea.  

**Kinds of Noun**  
Nouns can be classified into five kinds.  

The name of a particular person, place, monument or building is called a **proper noun**. For example, **Florence Nightingale, Mumbai, Howrah Bridge**.  

**NOTE**:  
Proper nouns always begin with a capital letter. We do not use a, an or the before proper nouns.  

The name of a person, place, or building in general is called a **common noun**. For example, **man, river, monument**.  

The name used for a collection of people, animals, birds or objects is called a **collective noun**. For example, **flock, herd, family**.  

The name of an idea, a concept, a state of being or a feeling is called an **abstract noun**. For example, **beauty, hatred, trust**.  

The name of the matter or substance of which a thing is made is called a **material noun**. For example, **silver, wood, paper**.

**Gender**  
There are four genders in the English language.  

Nouns are said to be in the **masculine gender** if they refer to males. For example, **grandfather, uncle, tiger, peacock**.  

Nouns are said to be in the **feminine gender** if they refer to females. For example, **grandmother, aunt, tigress, peahen**.  

**NOTE**:  
Words of the feminine gender like actress or poetess are used less often these days. Instead, the usage of nouns of the common gender is preferred.  

Nouns of the common gender may be of the masculine or the feminine gender. For example, parent, leader, cousin, child, doctor.  

**NOTE**:  
Nouns of the neuter gender are usually non-living things.  

Nouns of the neuter gender are neither of the masculine gender nor the feminine gender. For example, table, book, torch, pen.

**Number**  
In English, we say a noun may be either **singular** or **plural** in number.  

The **singular** form of a noun denotes one person, place or thing.  
For example, **chair**, **torch**, **knife**.  

The **plural** form of a noun denotes more than one person, place or thing.  
For example, **chairs**, **torches**, **knives**.  

**NOTE**:  
Nouns can also be classified as **countable** or **uncountable**.  
- A **countable noun** denotes that which can be counted. For example, we can say, one **girl**, ten **boys**, two **apples**. We usually add one of the articles (a, an or the) or a **number** before countable nouns.  
- An **uncountable noun** refers to things that cannot be counted. For example, **news**, **tea**, **salt**, **glass**. We do not use a or an with uncountable nouns. However, we can say, a **cup of tea** or a **pinch of salt**.
