# Transformation of Sentences  

Read these sentences.  

- The policeman must run fast to catch the thief.  
- The policeman must run fast or he will not catch the thief.  
- The policeman must run fast if he wants to catch the thief.  

Did you notice that though these sentences have been expressed in different ways, the basic meaning has not changed?  

Transformation is the process of changing the structure of a sentence without altering the meaning.  

**Simple to Compound**  
Look at the sentences given above once again. The first sentence is a simple sentence. A simple sentence can be transformed into a compound sentence by expanding a word or a phrase into a main clause using a coordinating conjunction. For example,  
- He must work hard to win the scholarship. **(simple)**  
- He must work hard and win the scholarship. **(compound)**  

**A. Convert these simple sentences into compound sentences.**  
1. Being lazy, he failed.  
2. Seeing his teacher, he stopped.  
3. Wishing to build a school, he started to raise a fund.  
4. He must leave immediately to catch the train.  
5. Despite his wealth, he is not admired.  
6. Besides helping me with money, he gave me a very warm coat.  
7. Fearing suspension, the workers withdrew the agitation.  
8. When the theft was discovered, the whole house was searched.  
9. He must admit the truth to avoid punishment.  
10. In spite of our very sincere efforts, we could not save the calf.  

**Simple to Complex**  
A simple sentence can be transformed into a complex sentence by expanding a word or a phrase into a subordinate clause, which could be a noun, an adjective or an adverb clause. For example,  
- I saw a beautiful girl. **(simple)**  
- I saw a girl who was beautiful. **(complex)**  

**B. Convert these simple sentences into complex sentences.**  
1. We believe in his honesty.  
2. The man confessed his guilt.  
3. We returned home at sunset.  
4. The storm abated around noon.  
5. He is a man of many talents.  
6. The poor deserve our support.  
7. The news of his arrival pleased us all.  
8. I know him to be a committed worker.  
9. Our teacher will be our guide during the trip.  
10. Despite a lot of difficulties, we succeeded in reaching the base camp for the final trek.  

**Complex to Simple or Compound**  
We can convert a complex sentence into a simple or a compound sentence. For example,  
- This boy, who is very talented, is also hard-working. **(complex)**  
- This boy is talented and very hardworking. **(simple)**  
- This boy is not only talented but also very hardworking. **(compound)**  

In order to change a complex sentence into a simple sentence, we must remove the subordinate clause. We must ensure that there is only one verb in the new sentence framed.  

When we have to transform a complex sentence into a compound sentence, we must change the subordinate clause into a coordinate clause using a suitable coordinate conjunction.  

**C. Convert these complex sentences into simple sentences.**  
1. He is a man who has no sense of pride.  
2. When you reach home, you must call me.  
3. If he delays, he might miss the bus.  
4. I saw a stag that had a beautiful silken coat.  
5. He accepted what we proposed.  
6. We exercise so that we may keep fit.  
7. Suresh has a house that has been mortgaged.  
8. Although I am not very fond of him, I gave him my pen.  

**D. Convert these complex sentences into compound sentences.**  
1. I found the pen that I lost.  
2. As I have fever, I cannot go to work today.  
3. I will dismiss you if you do not admit your fault.  
4. As the day was very hot, we were very tired.  
5. Our picnic was cancelled, as it started to rain.  
6. When the sun set, we started our return journey.  
7. Although we were tired, we did not give up.  
8. Although I was on time, I missed the train.  

**Compound to Simple or Complex**  
A compound sentence can also be converted into a simple or a complex sentence. For example,  
He is old but he still works hard. **(compound)**  
Despite being old, he still works hard. **(simple)**  
Though he is old, he still works hard. **(complex)**  

In order to transform a compound sentence into a simple sentence, we must replace one of the coordinate clauses and replace with a word or a phrase.  

While transforming a compound sentence into a complex sentence, a subordinating clause must take the place of a coordinate clause and a subordinating conjunction must replace the coordinating conjunction.  

**E. Convert these compound sentences into simple sentences.**  
1. He is rich but not happy.  
2. I called out but he did not hear me.  
3. He finished swimming and got dressed for dinner.  
4. The Parliament passed the bill but the people were dissatisfied.  
5. You must either pass this exam or leave the college.  
6. They took every precaution but they could not prevent frost bite.  
7. He studied very hard, yet he did not score well in the exams.  
8. The boy had never been to school, and he could not read.  

**F. Convert these compound sentences into complex sentences.**  
1. He saw the snake, but he did not stop.  
2. I received the message and I left immediately.  
3. Many diseases are caused by bacteria and we know this.  
4. You must wear warm clothes or you will catch a cold.  
5. They tried to bribe the official, but they did not succeed.  
6. The house was burnt down, but no one was injured.  
7. He may be at office and I might meet him there.  
8. He wishes to secure his position, yet he is not working hard enough.  

**G. Rewrite these sentences as directed.**  
1. But for your temper, we would have secured the contract. (Rewrite using **if**.)  
2. Though the men had lost their leader, they did not abandon the mission. (Rewrite **without the subordinate clause**.)  
3. He has lost all his money, but he has no regrets. (Rewrite using **although**.)  
4. As soon as I had seated myself, the bell rang. (Use no **sooner... than**.)  
5. The team accepted my proposal. (Use the **verb form of proposal**.)  
6. He is too arrogant to listen to advice. (Rewrite using **an adverb clause of reason**.)  
7. I endured the hardship because I had no choice. (Rewrite as **two principal clauses**.)  
8. The young boy entered the hall with much hesitation. (Use the **verb form** of **hesitation**.)  
9. There was a young architect in our office when the accident occurred. (Begin with **At the time**.)  
10. She laid the table after she had finished cooking. (Begin: **When she...**)  
11. Mr Roy is correcting the answer scripts of the last examination. (Begin: **The answer scripts...**)  
12. In May I will be going to London with Ma, Baba and Shyam.  
(Use the word **accompanied** and **rewrite**)  
13. The weather in Jaipur is much better than it is at home. (Begin: **At home...**)  
14. She is learning Chinese in order to communicate with her Chinese clients. (Use the word purpose and rewrite)  
15. He did not understand fully what he was supposed to do. (Use the word **lacked** and rewrite)  
16. The child is so naughty that he cannot be left alone even for a minute. (Begin: **It is not advisable...**)  
17. Mr Shetty was mild mannered and humble too. (Use **not only** and **but also**)  
18. On being scolded, the pupil started crying. (Begin with **when.**)
