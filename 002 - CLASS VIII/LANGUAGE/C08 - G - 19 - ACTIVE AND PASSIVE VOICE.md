# Active and Passive Voice  

Read these sentences.  

- He **painted** a picture.  
- A picture *was* **painted** by him.  

Both the sentences have the same meaning but have been constructed differently. In the first sentence, the highlighted verb indicates that the subject **(he)** did the action. In the second sentence, the function of the verb is to show that the subject **(a picture)** was acted on.  

The verb that expresses that the action is carried out by the subject is said to be in the **active voice**. The active voice is used when the agent or the doer is to be made prominent.  

The verb that expresses what is done to the subject is said to be in the **passive voice**.  

See how the following sentences in the active voice have been changed into passive voice without changing their meanings.  
- The referee ordered a re-match.  
    - A re-match was ordered by the referee.
- Shireen bought a house.  
    - A house was bought by Shireen.  

**NOTE**:  
The last word of any verb in the passive form will be written in the past participle form.  

Let us revise the rules of conversion from active to passive voice.  
- The positions of the subject and the object of the active verb are interchanged. The subject of the verb in the active voice is made the object of the verb in the passive voice.  
- In the passive voice, the preposition by is added before the object.  
- The number of the verb is changed according to the number and person of the subject.  
      - He loves me.
    - I am loved by him.
- Active verbs followed by a preposition retain the preposition. For example,  
    - The students laughed at the clown.
    - The clown was laughed at by the students.  

Not all tenses take the passive form. Study the table to understand how the verb is changed into the passive voice form in different tenses.  

| Tense                          | Active voice                          | Passive voice                          |  
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |  
| **Simple present**             | He **reads** a book.                  | A book is **read** by him.             |  
| **Present continuous**         | He **is reading** a book.             | A book is **being read** by him.       |  
| **Present perfect**            | He **has read** a book.               | A book **has been read** by him.       |  
| **Present perfect continuous** | He **has been reading** a book.       | —                                      |  
| **Simple past**                | He **read** a book.                   | A book **was read** by him.            |  
| **Past continuous**            | He **was reading** a book.            | A book **was being read** by him.      |  
| **Past perfect**               | He **had read** a book.               | A book **had been read** by him.       |  
| **Past perfect continuous**    | He **had been reading** a book.       | —                                      |  
| **Simple future**              | He **will read** a book.              | A book **will be read** by him.        |  
| **Future continuous**          | He **will be reading** a book.        | —                                      |  
| **Future perfect**             | He **will have read** a book.         | A book **will have been read** by him. |  
| **Future perfect continuous**  | He **will have been** reading a book. | —                                      |  

**NOTE**:  
- If the active verb has two objects—one direct and the other indirect—either of them becomes the subject of the passive verb, and the other remains as an object in the passive voice. The object that comes after the passive verb is called the retained object. For example,  
  - My father gave me a calculator. **(active voice)**
  - I was given a calculator by my father. **(passive voice)**
  - A calculator was given to me by my father. **(passive voice)**  

Study this table to understand how imperative sentences are changed into the passive voice.  

|  Active voice | Passive voice  |  
| --- | --- |  
|  Take it away. | Let it be taken away.  |  
|  Do it tomorrow. | Let it be done tomorrow.  |  
|  Open the window. | Let the window be opened.  |  
|  Do not leave the food uncovered. | Let not the food be kept uncovered.  |  

- The imperative sentence in the passive voice takes the form—  
  **Let + object + be + past participle**
- If the sentence is in the negative, the form is—  
  **Let not + object + be + past participle**

Study this table to understand how interrogative sentences are changed into the passive voice.  

|  Active voice | Passive voice  |  
| --- | --- |  
|  Did Ravi invite you? | Were you invited by Ravi?  |  
|  Do the children like sweets? | Are sweets liked by the children?  |  
|  Have you finished the project? | Has the project been finished by you?  |  
|  Who did this? | By whom was this done?  |  
|  Who hit the ball? | By whom was the ball hit?  |  
|  When will they announce the date of the wedding? | When will the date of the wedding be announced? (They is not used.)  |  

The passive voice is used when the person or thing acted upon is to be made prominent. The passive voice is thus preferred when the active form would involve the use of a vague pronoun or noun (**somebody, they, people, anybody**) as subject. For example,  

- My purse has been stolen. (Someone has stolen my purse.)  
- I was asked my name. (They asked me my name.)  
- I have been invited to the party. (Someone has invited me to the party.)  

**NOTE**:  
Do, does and did are not used in the passive voice.

We prefer to use the passive voice when—  
- the doer is not known or when it is not intended to name him. For example,  
    - The player was carried out.
- we wish to focus on the person/thing acted upon rather than the doer or the agent. For example,  
    - The shop was destroyed by lightning.
- the subject in the active voice is implied from the context. For example,  
    - He was dropped from the team (by the selectors).  

**NOTE**:  
Since the object of a verb in the active voice becomes the subject of the passive form, it means that only transitive verbs can be used in the passive, because an intransitive verb has no object.
