# Direct and Indirect Speech  

You have already learnt that there are two ways of reporting the words of a speaker—**the direct speech** and **the indirect**, or r**eported speech**.  

**Direct speech** reports the exact words of the speaker. In indirect speech, we report what the speaker said without quoting his/her exact words.  

For example,  

- Rahul said, 'I want to buy a badminton racquet.' (direct)  
- Rahul said that he wanted to buy a badminton racquet. (indirect)  

Let us compare the two ways of speaking.  

| Direct speech                                                                                                         | Indirect speech                                                                                                                        |  
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |  
| The exact words of the speaker are repeated.                                                                          | The speaker's words are reported by someone in his or her words.                                                                       |  
| The words of the speaker are enclosed within inverted commas.                                                         | Since the exact words of the speaker are not reproduced, the inverted commas are removed.                                              |  
| The introductory verb is said.                                                                                        | The introductory verb is usually said or told.                                                                                         |  
| A comma separates the introductory part and the exact words of the speaker.                                           | The subordinate conjunction that is used to join the introductory part to the reported part. The comma is not used.                    |  
| The speaker uses the first person pronoun to speak for himself/herself.                                               | The first person pronoun is changed to the third person pronoun.                                                                       |  
| The verbs are usually in the present tense forms.<br>- is, am, are<br>- has, have<br>- can<br>- shall, will<br>- must | The verbs are usually changed to the corresponding past tense forms.<br>- was, were<br>- had<br>- could<br>- should, would<br>- had to |  

All present tense forms of the verb in direct speech are changed into past forms in indirect speech. Study this table to understand how personal pronouns and possessives change in reported speech.  

| Direct speech                                                                                      | Indirect speech                                                                                      |  
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |  
| Simple present <br>Jai said, '**I** *like* eating pizza.'                                          | Simple past <br>Jai said that **he** *liked* eating pizza.                                           |  
| Present continuous <br>Rita said, '**My** aunt *is* baking a cake.'                                | Past continuous <br>Rita said that **her** aunt was *baking* a cake.                                 |  
| Present perfect <br>Rocky said, '**I** have *slept* for a while.'                                  | Past perfect <br>Rocky said that **he** *had slept* for a while.                                     |  
| Present perfect continuous <br>She said, '**I** *have been swimming* since I was eight years old.' | Past perfect continuous <br>She said that **she** *had been swimming* since she was eight years old. |  
| Simple past <br>He said, '**I** *bought* a book for my mother.'                                    | Past perfect <br>He said that **he** *had bought* a book for his mother.                             |  
| Past continuous <br>He said, '**Rana** *was exercising* in his room.'                              | Past perfect continuous <br>He said that **Rana** *had been exercising* in his room.                 |  
| Past perfect <br>Rosy said, '**Rita** *had done* this before.'                                     | Past perfect <br>Rosy said that **Rita** *had done* this before.                                     |  

If the reported speech expresses a universal truth or reports a statement that is still true now, the tense of the verb remains unchanged.  
- The teacher told the class, 'The moon is earth's satellite.'  
    - The teacher told the class that the moon is earth's satellite.
- Pratibha said, 'My mother doesn't like eggs.'  
    - Pratibha said that her mother doesn't like eggs.

**Reporting Questions**  
Let us see how we should change questions from direct to indirect speech.  

- The reporting verb must be changed to **asked, enquired, wondered, questioned**. For example,  
    - Mani said to Kunal, 'Where are **you** going?'
    - Mani **asked** Kunal where **he** was going.
Notice that in the direct speech, the subject comes after the helping verb. However, when we report a question, the subject is placed before the helping verb.  
- When we report a **wh-** question, we use the **wh-** word as a conjunction. For example,  
    - Jaya asked Parul, 'What is the name of your dog?'
    - Jaya asked Parul **what** the name of her dog was.
- When we report a **yes/no** question, we connect the reporting question using **if** or **whether**. For example,  
    - I asked him, 'Will you come for dinner?'
    - I asked him **whether** he would come for dinner.
- We should keep in mind the tone of the question and the sentiment of the speaker when we change a question from direct to indirect speech. For example,  
    - My mother said, 'Would you like a cup of tea?'
        - I said, 'Yes.'
    - My mother asked me whether I would like a cup of tea. I replied that I would.

**Reporting Command or Requests**  
Let us now take a look at the rules for changing commands, requests, advice, etc., into indirect speech.  

- We use reporting verbs like **tell**, **warn** to report orders and instructions. We also use the **to + verb** construction. For example,  
    - The teacher said to the pupils, 'You must apologise to her right now.'
        - The teacher ordered the pupils to **apologise** to her right then.
    - I said to my friend, 'Could you make me a cup of hot chocolate?' 
        - I requested my friend **to make** me a cup of hot chocolate.  

**Reporting Exclamations**  
Let us now take a look at the rules for changing exclamations and wishes into indirect speech.  
- The reporting verb is changed into **ask, advise, tell, request,** etc.  
- Words such as **hurrah, alas, oh,** etc., are omitted. However, the feeling expressed must be conveyed appropriately by using expressions such as **exclaimed with joy, said with a sigh,** etc.  
- The sign of exclamation is omitted. For example,  
    - 'Oh no! We've missed the bus,' he said.
    - He exclaimed sadly that they had missed the bus.

**Conversion of Indirect Speech into Direct Speech**  
We know that indirect speech is a means of reporting what somebody has said without using the actual words of the speaker. The best way to change from indirect to direct speech is to mentally identify the two parts of the sentence.  

- The main part will contain a reporting verb like **said, tell, suggested, requested,** etc.  
- The other part will contain what is being reported, that is, the part which is the quoted section in direct speech.  
- The part that will become the quote once we have made the transformation is the object of the reporting verb.  
    - He told me that my parcel had reached the previous week.
        - He said to me, 'Your parcel reached last week.'
    - The teacher asked the boy whether he had completed his homework.
        - The teacher said to the boy, 'Have you completed your homework?'
    - His mother urged him to take his studies seriously.
        - His mother said to him, 'You should take your studies seriously.'
    - He ordered his servant to be quiet and listen to his words.
        - He said to his servant, 'Be quiet and listen to my words.'
    - He exclaimed that it was a terrible sight.
        - 'What a terrible sight it is!' said he.
