# Continuous Tenses  

You have learnt that verbs in the continuous tense are used to denote actions that have not been completed.  

**Present Continuous Tense**  
The present continuous tense is used—  
- to describe an action that is happening now. For example,  
    - He is **swimming** in the lake now.
    - Mohini is **washing** the car.
- to describe an action that has been going on over a period of time, but may not be going on at the time of speaking. For example,  
    - I am **living** with my grandparents.
    - Murali is **learning** to drive.
- for an action that will take place in the near future. For example,  
    - We **are going** to have dinner at a Chinese restaurant tonight.
    - I **am going** to the theatre tomorrow.  

**NOTE**:  
We use adverbs or phrases of present time such as **now**, **at this moment** when we use the present continuous tense.  

**NOTE**:  
We do not use continuous tense for—  
- verbs of sense and perception. For example,  
    - I hear the noise. (correct)
    - I am hearing the noise. (incorrect)
    - I see the school. (correct)
    - I am seeing the school. (incorrect)
- verbs expressing state of mind or emotion. For example,  
    - I admire you. (correct)
    - I am admiring you. (incorrect)
- verbs of thinking or other mental activity. For example,  
    - I think you are wrong. (correct)
    - I am thinking you are wrong. (incorrect)
- verbs of possession. For example,  
    - I own a car. (correct)
    - I am owning a car. (incorrect)

**Past Continuous Tense**  
The past continuous tense is used—  
- to describe an action going on at a point of time or over a period of time in the past. The time of action may or may not be indicated. For example,  
    - I **was working** till late last night.
    - They **were watching** TV till 1:00 a.m. last night!
- to describe an action that was in progress when another action took place. For example,  
    - They **were having** dinner, when I arrived.
    - When I met him, he **was rehearsing** for the show.
In the above sentences, the verbs in the past continuous tense describe longer actions while verbs in the simple past describe shorter actions.

**Future Continuous Tense**  
The future continuous is used—  
- to denote an action that will be in progress at some point of time in the future. For example,  
    - Rohan will be eating dinner at that time.
- to denote future events that are planned. For example,  
    - The Cabinet will be announcing the annual budget.
