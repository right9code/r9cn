## Chapter Details

### 01 — [[C07 - 01 - THE DAY THE RIVER SPOKE|The Day the River Spoke]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Jahnavi | A girl of nearly ten who wants to go to school like her siblings and, with courage, finally gets to attend |
| The River | The personified, sleepy-voiced river that befriends Jahnavi and urges her to slip into school and believe in herself |
| Mother | Jahnavi's mother, who kept saying "maybe next year," but is glad the teacher came and admits she too once wished to study |
| Father | Jahnavi's father, whom the teacher visits and persuades to let her go to school |
| Gopi (Ettan) | Jahnavi's elder brother, a "nice lad" in the next class at school |
| Meena | Jahnavi's elder sister, whom Jahnavi wants to read like |
| Ramu | The little brother born when Jahnavi was five, whom she once minded and who now goes to school |
| Little Appu | The smallest sibling whom Jahnavi minds; asleep on her shoulder at school |
| Chandu | The local boatman with a catamaran who could someday take Jahnavi to see a ship |
| The teacher | The village school teacher who lets Jahnavi stay in class and talks to her father about admitting her |
| The schoolboys | The boys in class who recognise Jahnavi as Gopi's sister |
| Grandmother | The one who long ago refused to let Jahnavi's mother go to school (recalled by Mother) |

### 03 — [[C07 - 03 - THREE DAYS TO SEE|Three Days to See]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Helen Keller | The blind and deaf author-narrator who imagines what she would see with three days of sight and urges readers to treasure every sense |
| Her friends | The people whose kindness made her life worth living; she knows them only through the feel of their faces |
| The city people | The ordinary folk at the busy corner whose smiles, determination and suffering she would watch on her third day |

### 04 — [[C07 - 04 - ANIMALS BIRDS AND DR DOLITTLE|Animals, Birds, and Dr. Dolittle]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Doctor John Dolittle | The Puddleby people's doctor who, on his patients' advice, learns animal languages and becomes a world-famous animal doctor |
| Polynesia | The Doctor's parrot, who reveals that animals talk and patiently teaches him the Birds' A.B.C. |
| The Cat's-food-Man | Dolittle's patient with a stomach-ache who first suggests he become an animal doctor, praising his cat book |
| Theodosia | The Cat's-food-Man's wife, a scholar who read Dolittle's book aloud to her husband |
| Jip | The Doctor's dog, whose ear-scratching and nose-twitching are shown to be speech |
| The plough horse | The patient going blind in one eye who asks for (and gets) green spectacles like the Doctor's |
| The vet over the hill | The incompetent vet who never looked at the horse's eyes and kept giving it big pills |
| The old ladies and farmers | Puddleby folk who bring overfed pugs and poodles, sick cows and sheep once word spreads |

### 06 — [[C07 - 06 - SAY THE RIGHT THING|Say the Right Thing]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Mary Shaw | The over-confident young girl who, despite her mother's coaching, insults the guests at every turn and even muddles the farewell |
| Mrs. Shaw | Mary's mother, who coaches her daughter to say kind, pleasing things and desperately tries to smooth over each blunder |
| Mrs. Harding | The wife of wealthy Mr. Harding, newly moved to Lanfield, who is mortally offended (children, clothes, staying in bed, her dog) |
| Mrs. Lee | Mr. Harding's married sister and the guest who each time quietly reveals that Mary has just insulted her own family |
| Mr. Harding | The wealthy banker who bought a big house in Lanfield; the very commuter "living in trains" whom Mary mocks |
| Mr. Best | The neighbourhood banker Mary ridicules for commuting to London and never reading a book |
| Mrs. Best | His wife, whose blue dress and red coat Mary laughs at |
| Mrs. Cotter | The neighbour Mary mocks for staying in bed in the morning and always sending for the doctor |
| Mrs. Grantley | Mrs. Shaw's best friend, whom Mary calls too talkative |
| Mr. Pomeroy | The neighbour seen on his horse, whom Mary criticises for riding and practising shooting |
| Towzer | The Hardings' dog, spotted dirty at the window, whose looks Mary mocks |
| Mrs. Lee's mother | The unnamed "very talkative woman" whom Mary's chatter unwittingly offends |

### 07 — [[C07 - 07 - MY BROTHERS GREAT INVENTION|My Brother's Great Invention]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Anita | The fourteen-year-old narrator, Anand's sceptical but sensible sister who wants no part of the blame |
| Anand | Anita's younger brother, a would-be scientist whose burglar alarm soaks Papa and whose time machine makes a thief vanish |
| Papa | Their father, the burglar alarm's first (and wettest) victim, who bans further experiments and later confiscates the machine |
| Ma | Their understanding mother, who shields Anand, watches her oven and mixer, and makes him promise not to touch the time machine |
| Boppa | The colony's new sweeper, really the thief who breaks in at night and disappears into Anand's time machine, leaving only his green scarf |
| Mrs. Sharma | Sharma Aunty next door who, alerted by the children's shouts, arrives with the rescuing party |
| Mr. Sharma | Sharma Uncle, who unlocks the door, indulges Anand's theories with a smile and spends the night guarding the house |
| Dr. Mohan | A neighbour who joins the rescue and, peering everywhere, helps search for the missing intruder |

### 09 — [[C07 - 09 - NORTH SOUTH EAST WEST|North, South, East, West]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Shaana | The girl from Rameswaram island who tours India with her parents and writes postcards to her class about each place |
| Amma | Shaana's mother, who teaches her on the road (surprise tests), explains wetlands and grieves the Sundarbans flooding |
| Appa | Shaana's father, fun-loving (snowballs, surfing, diving) but scared off rafting forever |
| Shaana's classmates | The friends back at school to whom all the postcards are addressed |
| PT Miss | The school's physical training teacher, sent love all the way from the Thajiwas glacier |
| Library Ma'am | The school librarian, also remembered in Shaana's greetings |

### 10 — [[C07 - 10 - THE TUNNEL|The Tunnel]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Suraj | The adventure-loving boy from town who waits for the midday train, walks the dark tunnel and helps drive out the leopard |
| Sunder Singh | The lonely watchman who calls the tunnel his own (merely "lent to the Government"), knows the jungle and its leopard, and lights the dark for passing trains |
| The leopard | "Sunder Singh's leopard" — lithe and sinewy, it minds its own business but is found crouching in the tunnel and driven out before the night mail |
| Suraj's father | The businessman who naps after lunch and, thinking Suraj wanders too much, takes him to Delhi on a business trip by the night mail |
| The engine driver | The unseen trainman whom the watchman's red flag (day) or lamp (night) warns of danger in the tunnel |

### 12 — [[C07 - 12 - CONQUERING THE SUMMIT|Conquering the Summit]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Arunima Sinha | A national-level volleyball player who lost a leg in a 2011 train accident, vowed to climb Everest, and in 2013 became the first Indian female amputee to summit it (later Padma Shri) |
| Bachendri Pal | The first Indian woman to summit Everest, who trained Arunima and told her she had already conquered the Everest within her |
| Arunima's father | An army engineer who gave the family its sporting inclination (noted in her background) |
| Arunima's mother | A health supervisor (noted in her background) |
| Swami Vivekananda | Arunima's idol, whose photograph she leaves at the summit of Everest |

### 13 — [[C07 - 13 - A HOMAGE TO OUR BRAVE SOLDIERS|A Homage to Our Brave Soldiers]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Soumya | The Bengaluru student who visits the National War Memorial on a school trip and writes a long, moved letter to Ananda; she aspires to be courageous like Albert Ekka and to join the army |
| Ananda | Soumya's friend in Chandigarh, a stamp-collecting (philately) letter-writer who replies with a Chandigarh war memorial memory and resolves to join the Armed Forces and make an inspiring collage |
| The teacher | Soumya's teacher on the Delhi trip, who explains the wars, the tablets of names and the gallantry awards (PVC, MVC, KC, VrC, SC) |
| Soumya's classmates | The class that splits into five groups to present the bravehearts' stories at the school assembly |
| Shri Narendra Modi | India's Prime Minister, who envisioned and inaugurated the National War Memorial in February 2019 |
| Major Somnath Sharma | The soldier posthumously awarded India's first Param Vir Chakra for the Battle of Badgam, 1947 |
| Lance Naik Albert Ekka | The 14 Guards hero of the Gangasagar assault (1971), killed after silencing two machine guns; his citation leaves the students awestruck |
| Flying Officer Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon | A posthumous PVC hero of the 1971 war, named among the awardees |
| Second Lieutenant Arun Khetarpal | Another posthumous PVC hero of the 1971 war, in the same list |
| Major Hoshiar Singh | A PVC winner of the 1971 war, in the same list |
| Captain Mahendra Nath Mulla | The Navy officer awarded the Maha Vir Chakra for exemplary courage and leadership in 1971 |
| Major Padmapani Acharya | The 2 Rajputana Rifles company commander, posthumous MVC of the 1999 Kargil War, who before battle wrote to his father that he did not fear death; the subject of Soumya's planned presentation |
| Major Acharya's father | The parent to whom the major wrote his fearless parting letter |
| Captain Anuj Nayyar | The 17 Jat officer posthumously awarded the MVC for the 1999 Kargil War, whose story Ananda reads in the newspaper |
| Meena Nayyar | Captain Anuj Nayyar's mother, author of the memoir "Tiger of Drass – Capt. Anuj Nayyar – 23 – Kargil Hero" |
| Ananda's parents | The mom and dad remembered with regards, her mother's chhole bhature fondly recalled by Soumya |
| Amit | Someone at Ananda's home whom Soumya asks to be greeted (a brief friendly mention) |

### 15 — [[C07 - 15 - RANI ABBAKKA|Rani Abbakka]]

| Character | Who they are |
|-----------|--------------|
| Rani Abbakka | The dauntless queen of Ullal who refuses tribute to the Portuguese, builds merchant ships, allies with the Zamorin, trades with Arabia and routs the Portuguese |
| Veera Narsimha | Rani Abbakka's husband, who thinks her defiance a hasty decision and orders soldiers to stop her, whereupon she returns to rule Ullal independently |
| The Portuguese envoy | The Governor's messenger who demands the long-overdue tribute and warns Her Majesty she will regret her stand |
| The Portuguese Governor | The colonial power behind the envoy, whose forces later launch the night attack on Ullal |
| Rani Abbakka's mother | The mother who, on her deathbed, hears Abbakka's vow to protect Ullal's freedom and never surrender to the Portuguese |
| The Zamorin of Kozhikode | The ruler who joins Abbakka in league against the Portuguese and in the profitable trade with Arabia |
| The rajas | The neighbouring rulers who, feeling no match for Portuguese power, decline to support her cause |
| The people and soldiers of Ullal | The loyal subjects whose patriotic support carries their queen's fight against the Portuguese |
